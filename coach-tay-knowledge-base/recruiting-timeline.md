#### TL-006 · How a coach's interest typically develops, by year
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:**
  - **Before freshman year:** mail and follows are "very much so a foot in the door."
  - **Freshman year:** "just interest… a good freshman probably isn't good enough… unless you're like phenomenal." You could be on a list.
  - **Summer between freshman and sophomore year:** "a great time… to really improve and show a lot of growth."
  - **Sophomore year:** "quite a bit of interest… really going to show you where you're playing."
  - **Summer between sophomore and junior year:** "the biggest summer."
  - **Junior year:** "go time… you're going to have your offers."
  - **Before senior year:** commit.
- **Source:** [R04 @00:34–02:15]; the board's view in [R02 @09:52–10:17] ("by this time they have to be ready, which is so young")
- **Flags:** A late start "still can be about the same by time you get to junior and senior year… you just didn't have all the beforehand stuff." [R04 @02:15–02:32]

#### TL-014 · The D1 recruiting year, month by month, and what to do in each stretch
- **Label:** EXPLICIT (her walkthrough and advice); dates ⚠️ VERIFY
- **Principle:** Her R23 walkthrough of the 2026–27 D1 women's basketball calendar, with her advice for each stretch:
  - **August (quiet, with a recruiting shutdown):** no D1 coaches at club events. Campus visits are possible in the quiet days. "Definitely we shouldn't be at any tournaments" in the shutdown. [R23 @01:25–03:52]
  - **September (§ contact):** contact for seniors and two-year PSAs. Others can be evaluated at scholastic activities only. [R23 @03:55–05:06]
  - **October–February (evaluation, scholastic only):** "They can only go see you in high school." Christmas dead period. [R23 @05:06–05:52]
  - **March (§ again):** scholastic only. [R23 @05:54–06:05]
  - **Early April (dead), then quiet:** "another time for visits." [R23 @06:05–06:24]
  - **Late April (spring live, non-scholastic, Apr 23–25):** a few local AAU tune-up games before it are fine. [R23 @06:28–07:19]
  - **Early May (shutdown), then May 14–16 (live):** another tune-up might be valid if April went badly. [R23 @07:19–07:50]
  - **Late May to mid-June:** practice weekends, not a month of travel. [R23 @07:50–08:10]
  - **June (‡ approved scholastic events):** team camps and certified camps. High school focus. "Basketball is basketball." [R23 @08:10–09:15]
  - **July:** early-July gear-up, then the "major live periods" Jul 9–12 and Jul 23–26. [R23 @09:15–09:40]
- **Her words:** "So let's start in August of 2026. Um, that is when this calendar starts." [R23 @01:22–01:29]
- **Applies to:** Every grade from 9th (when events become "intentional," per the M2.G9 tab), applied each year. Seniors are the ones the September/March contact periods mention.
- **Source:** [R23 @01:22–10:15]
- **Flags:** ⚠️ VERIFY (V-23): checked against D1CAL-F, all periods match (see TL-015). Calendar dates change every year. Corroborates R12 (timeline cross-check table) and STR-014 / STR-076.

#### TL-017 · About two years out, a program's need for her class comes into focus, but it's never final
- **Label:** EXPLICIT (the quote); INTERPRETATION (mapping it to 10th grade)
- **Principle:** For the youngest classes on the board, coaches are "more just kind of watching." As a class reaches about two years from enrollment, staffs "start… seeing how you… shape out to their roster" and can see their own roster "a little bit more clearly." Nothing is final, because every season players quit or transfer. Offers to that class depend on her improvement over the next year and on how the current roster "is shaking out."
- **Her words:** "once you get down to these last two, they're going to be more just kind of watching. But as you enter that 2029 year, they are going to start kind of seeing how you see fit and seeing how you shape out to their roster. But by that two year, like two year out mark, they're going to be able to see their roster a little bit more clearly. And this is never finalized, right? Like, because after every season, there's always kids that are maybe going to quit or try to transfer" [R27 @10:00–10:28] · "mostly these are going to be just kind of contingent on, um, how they improve over that next year, um, during high school and AAU… as well as they will be able to see how their current roster is shaking out and have a better idea of who and what they need." [R26 @06:03–06:23] · "they are thinking this far in advance." [R27 @06:36–06:38]
- **Applies to:** In her fall-2026 frame, 2029 = sophomores, so the "two year out mark" lines up with 10th grade (INTERPRETATION).
- **Source:** [R27 @06:05–07:10, 10:00–10:31], [R26 @06:03–06:23]
- **Flags:** Corroborates TL-006 (sophomore year "quite a bit of interest") and TL-008 (look two years ahead). Compare TL-010 / Q-039 and Q-061 on year labels.

#### TL-018 · The board stops at freshmen, but 6th–8th grade are the years that get her ready
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** Her example board has no 7th- or 8th-grade columns. By the freshman class, though, coaches are watching and "becoming aware of your name," whether or not they talk to her. So readiness can't be crammed into the months before freshman season. It's built in middle school ("prepping your mind… your body… good habits").
- **Her words:** "two columns that are not present would be your 7th and 8th grade class… it's so crucial that when you're already in this 2030 class, a freshman class, coaches are watching you. They're looking at you, whether they're talking to you yet or not, that's different, but they see you… they are beginning to becoming aware of your name. And so you have to be ready by time this happens, which doesn't happen the couple months before your… freshman high school season… it happens in six [sic, likely "sixth,"] seventh grade in eighth grade, when you're prepping your mind, you're prepping your body, you're getting those good habits… So even though seventh and eighth grade is not physically on the list, those years are crucial because they are years that prep you." [R26 @07:05–08:01]
- **Applies to:** 7th–9th grade families (possibly 6th; see flags).
- **Source:** [R26 @06:23–08:04]
- **Flags:** Corroborates TL-002, MTH-004 and MTH-015. ⚔️ Minor tension with TL-010 / FIT-024 (R02-T C31: 2031 = 8th graders, whom a major program "may be watching… tracking her development") → Q-061. "Six" at @07:44 may be a caption error; don't cite it as a 6th-grade recommendation until confirmed. The video ends mid-sentence at @08:01.

#### TL-021 · Elite camps change purpose by grade: growth for 9th–10th, not offers for 11th–12th
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** Freshman and sophomore years are the best time ("especially, especially, especially") to use elite camps to test and build her game on her own. As a junior, "or definitely not… going into senior year," don't go "hoping for these offers." See STR-091.
- **Source:** [R30 @05:36–06:52]

#### TL-022 · Every winter counts: summer AAU looks need a strong high school season behind them
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** Summer exposure followed by a weak or missing winter makes coaches think "maybe they just caught you on a good day." "If you've got exposure, and you got looks in AAU in the summer, it's not like, okay, we're good. High school doesn't matter until the spring… That's not true."

- **Chatter starts:** Coaches contact HS/AAU coaches. Hearing nothing doesn't mean nothing is happening. [M2.G9 @00:04–01:05]
- **Events and camps:** "This is where we start to make a turn… more intentional." Learn what a live period is: "the easiest way to narrow down what events you're going to." Elite camps are "the kind of camps" to attend, for experience and possible interest. [M2.G9 @01:05–02:03]; [R13 @03:51–04:56]
- **Workouts:** Raise the intensity of everything, relative to her body. [M2.G9 @02:03–03:28]
- **List:** "During this year is where you want to be building her list… might be the most important one." [M2.G9 @03:47–04:02]
- **Film:** Start filming all HS and AAU games. [M2.G9 @04:02–05:01]
- **AAU:** The freshman-year team should be "the team." [M2.G7 @05:39–05:46]
- **Get ahead:** "Freshman year, you need to learn and understand absolutely everything… all the way down to making the decision on what school." [M2.G9 @05:01–05:16]
- **Wants list:** Start the living "what I want" list around freshman year. [WANTS-T W1]
- **Academics:** Get the counselor involved early; check NCAA-approved core courses. [R01-T S3, N2–N3]
- **Records:** Start the recruiting folder and keep it updated. [R17-T C2], [RECINFO-T ¶10]
- **Elite camps:** Go for experience. [R13-T ¶7]

- **Film (why freshman year):** Filming from the stands, editing and storing take time to learn. Interest "can always start" freshman year, so film should be "in your back pocket." "Staying ready so you don't have to get ready." Keep a rolling set (about five games), swapping in "newer, fresher" games. [R20 @00:11–01:23, 08:10–08:23] (STR-072, MTH-045)

- **Calendar:** Get comfortable reading the current NCAA D1 calendar and map the four AAU live windows with the AAU coach. Tune-ups close to home. [R22 @00:09–00:17; R23 @09:47–10:49] ⚠️ VERIFY (V-23) (STR-075, STR-078)

- **Elite camps:** Test her game alone: 1-on-1, 3-on-3, being coached by someone new, picking up drills quickly. [R30 @05:36–06:28]

## 10th grade (Sophomore)
- **"Prime time":** 10th and 11th grade are when coaches are "legit looking… is this kid going to be good enough"; "everybody starts to fall off or… shape out." She can "entertain coaches and not make any decisions." [M2.G10 @00:00–00:33]
- **Events:** "Only be going to Live Events." 2–3 elite camps ("3 is a little on the high end. 2 is good and 1 is probably fine"), chosen from schools showing chatter or interest that are legit options. It's "all about evaluation." [M2.G10 @00:33–02:13]; [R13 @04:56–05:52] (⚔️ see Q-021)
- **Workouts:** Intense; "start pushing some weight around." [M2.G10 @02:15–02:36]
- **List and outreach:** "Start taking it into your own hands." Start outreach yourself. Narrow the list. [M2.G10 @02:36–03:44]
