# Recruiting Strategies

Outreach, film, exposure, and the other practical strategies Coach Tay teaches.
Labels and conventions: see `README.md`.

## 1. Recruiting communication and outreach

#### STR-001 · Practice recruiting calls with prepared questions. The parent coaches from the side.
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** Early on, a parent can be on recruiting calls. Better still, practice the calls and have questions written down so the athlete doesn't need help, while the parent prompts ("Hey, ask coach this"). By the next year she shouldn't need you as much.
- **Source:** [M1.L3 @02:01–02:31]

#### STR-002 · From sophomore year, the athlete does her own outreach
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** Freshman year is for seeing what interest comes. In 10th grade, "you gotta start taking it into your own hands… you're gonna start outreach, doing that stuff yourself." By 11th grade, no outreach to "random schools."
- **Source:** [M2.G10 @02:36–03:02], [M2.G11 @02:11–02:14]
- **Flags:** How to write outreach messages isn't covered yet. R16 and R19 (Batch 3) may help.

#### STR-058 · Her annotated outreach email template
- **Label:** EXPLICIT (graphic)
- **Principle:** Send to both the head coach and an assistant. Subject: "Prospective Student-Athlete – [Name] – Class of [Year]." Introduce yourself and express interest. Give a key-info list: name, phone, email, X handle, high school/city, grad year, height, position, HS jersey #, AAU team, AAU jersey #, film link with "Anyone with the link can view" sharing. Invite them to games, with HS games (date/opponent/time/location) and AAU tournaments listed. Close professionally and gratefully.
- **Source:** [EMAILTEMPLATE-F]; consistent with [RADIUS-T EM1–EM2], [FILMORG-T ¶6], [R19-T ¶10–11]

#### STR-003 · Ask direct questions to find out where she stands
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** On calls, by text, or on visits: "Where does she currently stand in your recruiting class?" "Are you super interested, or still evaluating her?" "What do you need to see from her?" After an elite camp: "Now that you've seen me… what do you think I need to work on?"
- **Source:** [R04 @07:23–08:04], [R13 @05:38–05:49]
- **Flags:** The longer question list has been **received** as a graphic. 50+ questions in nine groups (program/style, coaching/communication, academics, development, campus, wellbeing, financial/NIL, next steps, open-ended) [CONVO-F]. Also see NARROW-T N1–N23, LEVEL-T S10, WAITING-T ¶2.

#### STR-004 · Keep responding to schools that have actually offered
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** Ignoring offering schools while waiting on a dream school's interest makes them "take their focus somewhere else."
- **Source:** [R03 @03:10–03:53]

#### STR-005 · Unsigned seniors: flood with film, stats and outreach
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** Seniors with no offers should flood schools with film and stats, and ask HS and AAU coaches directly for help sending it out: "a lot, a lot of outreach."
- **Source:** [M2.G12 @02:30–03:02]

#### STR-006 · Expect silence during a recruiting shutdown
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** During a shutdown, coaches can't reply. A week without an answer is "very normal."
- **Source:** [R12 @02:19–02:42]
- **Flags:** ⚠️ VERIFY calendar

#### STR-007 · Tell coaches about positions you're capable of playing
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** If your AAU or HS team doesn't play you at point guard, say so to college coaches: "I don't always get a lot of minutes at this position, but I am capable of… playing point guard, and I am very interested in it." That "goes a long ways."
- **Source:** [R09 @08:07–08:21]

#### STR-038 · Equip your HS and AAU coaches: a list, a plan, a drafted email
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** Coaches "do not have to have a huge list of college connections." Most "are not recruiting coordinators" and may not know what to do. Don't ask "Can you help get my daughter recruited?" Give them a school spreadsheet (School / Level / Coach / Email / Why interested), her info, schedule, film, and a drafted intro email they can edit and send. What coaches can do: reach out, introduce, give an honest evaluation, explain her role, help coaches find her, take calls, vouch for her character and work ethic, communicate interest, give an outside read on fit. College coaches ask them "How serious are they about our program?" Keep them informed about schools reaching out, her interest or loss of interest, camps, and good conversations. "You bring the plan. Your coach brings credibility."
- **Source:** [R16-T ¶1–12], [R19-T ¶1–7, ¶12, ¶15–16]
- **Flags:** Her drafted email template is in `sources/R19-text.md` ¶5.

#### STR-039 · Several legitimate touchpoints, without spamming
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** Coaches' inboxes are full, so "Getting overlooked doesn't always mean you're not good enough." You email, your HS coach emails, you see them at an event, you send updated schedules and new film, you follow up "appropriately." Contact the right staff members (head coach, position assistant, recruiting coordinator). But don't have mom, dad, the HS coach and the AAU coach all email every staff member on the same day: "strategic repetition."
- **Source:** [R19-T ¶8–11, ¶16]

#### STR-040 · High school exposure: "make the trip make sense"
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** You can be recruited from high school, but it's harder to get a coach to a regular-season game. Exposure events let coaches see many players at once. Target early-season, Thanksgiving, Christmas/holiday and regional tournaments, plus schools within driving distance. A good invitation: "We're playing in a tournament this weekend… I wanted to make sure you had our schedule. I'm wearing #12." Ask "WHY WOULD THIS COACH COME?" ("Because my daughter is really good" isn't enough). Formula: right school + right distance + right event + right communication + right performance. "Give coaches a reason to come. Then when they come… Give them a reason to come back."
- **Source:** [R18-T ¶2–13], [RADIUS-T EM0–FM1]
- **Flags:** Email templates in `sources/RADIUS-text.md` EM1–EM2.

#### STR-041 · Track every contact, then track offers and visits separately
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:**
  - **Tab 1, "Coach Contact Tracker":** school, coach name, title, email, phone, date first contacted, method, whether the HS or AAU coach contacted them, response, notes.
  - **Tab 2, "Offers & Visits":** offered, date, details, unofficial and official visits with dates, next step.

  "YOU CONTACTING A SCHOOL ≠ THAT SCHOOL RECRUITING YOU." "DON'T JUST TRACK WHO KNOWS YOUR NAME. TRACK WHO IS MOVING THE RELATIONSHIP FORWARD." A later lesson will add school-comparison fields.
- **Source:** [TRACKER-T ¶1–6], [OFFERTRACK-T ¶1–5]
- **Flags:** An example note mentions "Offer expires August 1" (exploding offers aren't otherwise covered, Q-043).

#### STR-061 · Time outreach around big events and the postseason, not a "random Tuesday"
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** Emails from her and from her HS/AAU coaches work better when they're tied to something happening: a big tournament coming up, or making regionals or the state tournament. Then "it's relevant to what you have going on," not "some random Tuesday or Wednesday morning." You can't make volunteer coaches follow a schedule ("you're just grateful that they're sending the email over"), but you can ask for timing. Don't keep resending the same email "over and over," but having the HS and AAU coaches each send one helps.
- **Her words:** "it's very helpful, uhm, to be timely in terms of, if you're have a big tournament coming up, or if you're making it to regionals or the state tournament or something like that, at the high school level, uhm, that's a good time to have your high school coach send it out, and for you to send it out, the email, so, you know, it's relevant to what you have going on, it's not just some random Tuesday or Wednesday morning, it's something that has some intention." [R19 @07:40–08:08]
- **Applies to:** All grades doing outreach (10th+ per STR-002); the HS season especially.
- **Source:** [R19 @07:09–08:08]
- **Flags:** Corroborates STR-039 (strategic repetition) and STR-040. Postseason timing (regionals/state) is new from the video. ⚠️ D1 contact/evaluation windows during the HS season still apply to how coaches respond (V-04).

#### STR-068 · Log everything, down to a follow, plus what each coach asked for
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** The recruiting section of the folder can be "as little as followed her on Twitter," and goes up through calls, texts, emails, planned visits and offers. It's also the place for notes on coach requests, e.g., "Coach Smith asked me if I could send him over my schedule for AAU when it comes out," so nothing depends on memory: "you don't have to keep it all in your brain."
- **Her words:** "you can keep as little as followed her on Twitter, you can keep people that have called, texted, people that have emailed, uhm, visits obviously that she has planned, and definitely once we get to offers and stuff like that, and then that's a good place to keep notes" [R17 @05:26–05:42]
- **Applies to:** All grades once any contact starts.
- **Source:** [R17 @05:23–06:04]
- **Flags:** Corroborates STR-041 (tracker) and STR-044. New: logging follows, and logging follow-up requests as notes. "AEU" at @05:51 is a caption error for "AAU."

## 2. Highlight film: production and distribution

#### STR-008 · Start filming freshman year
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** Film every HS and AAU game. Learn how to film well if your teams don't record. Save and organize it, and replace it each year.
- **Source:** [M2.G9 @04:02–05:01]
- **Flags:** Full game vs. highlights is in R20/R21 (Batch 3).

#### STR-009 · After committing, study your future team's film
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** Ask your future coach for film. Watch last season's games ("the freshest film you can see") and learn the plays, the defense and your teammates' names.
- **Source:** [M2.G12 @00:55–02:06]

#### STR-042 · Full game film first, highlights second
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** "FULL GAME FILM FIRST. ALWAYS. Especially when you are first getting your name out there." A highlight reel "tells them what you look like when things go right." A full game shows off-ball movement, defense, communication, reaction to mistakes, decisions, rebounding, sprinting back, and impact when your "superpower" isn't working. The coach asks "Is this player worth my time to go see?" Build a body of work (game 1, 2, 3: "This is who she is"). Don't send only your best game. Highlights come once coaches know you: "A highlight tape should enhance your reputation. It shouldn't have to create your reputation." The path: Film → Evaluation → Live viewing → Relationship → Recruiting. Label film with name, grad year, high school, opponent, date, jersey number.
- **Source:** [R20-T ¶1–14]

#### STR-043 · Store and send film: one library, links not files, test every link
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** Google Drive as the default library (YouTube Unlisted is acceptable). Folder "[NAME] — RECRUITING FILM" → Full Games → by season. File names: "PLAYER NAME — GRAD YEAR — [TOURNAMENT —] OPPONENT — DATE." Share as "Anyone with the link → Viewer." Test from another device. Email or text the link, never the file. Text only if the coach already communicates that way. Don't send 15 links; start with the most relevant full game. Organization also signals "Can this player follow through? … Is this family organized?" Film-sending checklist in the source. Goal: send film "in less than a minute."
- **Source:** [FILMORG-T ¶1–16]
- **Flags:** Platform sharing details are product facts that may change.

#### STR-044 · One recruiting information document plus a simple Drive structure
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** A starred Google Doc, "FIRST NAME LAST NAME — RECRUITING INFORMATION," with: name, grad year, height, position, high school, AAU program, jersey number; athlete email (professional, name-based, actually checked) and phone; NCAA ID and account email (**no passwords**); film links; latest transcript link; HS/AAU schedules and upcoming exposure events. Drive folders: Film, Transcripts, Schedules, Recruiting Documents. Keep it updated. The athlete should know where everything is and be able to answer "Absolutely, Coach. I'll send it over." "OPEN. COPY. SEND."
- **Source:** [RECINFO-T ¶1–13], [R17-T ID1–C3]

#### STR-070 · Crop game film to her minutes and mark each check-in
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** Cut each game file to the stretches when she's on the floor, so a coach doesn't sit through "three and a half minutes" waiting to spot her. Each segment runs "whenever she subs in to whenever she checks out." Put the segments together in one file if possible, or at least in the same folder. A text overlay can mark "checking back in, checking out." She ties this to the "coaches don't have time" objection: it "matters… how it is stored and sent."
- **Her words:** "some people will say that coaches don't have time to watch full game film, which isn't entirely true. Uhm, it just matters a lot. It just how it is stored and sent" [R20 @01:27–01:39]; "have it cropped towards your daughter's actual minutes, right? We don't want, if she's not a starter, we don't want the start of the game and… watching, you know, three and a half minutes until she gets in" [R20 @01:47–02:04]; "So what we want is to have it cropped towards, like, you know, whenever she subs in to whenever she checks out and that keeps it really nice and nice and easy… in the same folder, in the same section, or if you can crop it together, that's really helpful… You can always add, like, text overlay" [R20 @02:14–02:37]; "So it's not just a bunch of random minutes where she's not in there." [R20 @02:43–02:50]
- **Applies to:** All players, most of all non-starters and players with limited minutes.
- **Source:** [R20 @01:27–02:50]
- **Flags:** ⚔️ CONFLICT (tension) with STR-042 / R20-T ¶4 ("The coach gets to see the entire game") and FILMORG-T ¶15 ("Is this a full game?") (Q-055). Also see EVAL-045: cropping removes bench behavior that coaches evaluate (INTERPRETATION). R20-T ¶11 ("They might watch your shifts") is consistent with cropping. Caption at @02:39 ("Mecca and checking out") is garbled.

#### STR-071 · When to send film: always when interest is low, and still when interest is high
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** With no or little interest, send film repeatedly ("sending out film, sending out film, sending out film"). The goal is for a coach to watch and then "carve out time to come see you in person." With a lot of interest, keep sending, because "interest isn't an offer," and film plus good stats can push a player "over that edge for an offer." Consistency matters: "one good game film is not going to be enough." Keep showing that she's up to date.
- **Her words:** "obviously if you have a lot of interest, that's awesome… and you can still send film because interest isn't an offer, right?" [R20 @05:58–06:07]; "if you don't have interest, this is when you want to start sending out film, sending out film, sending out film, because your goal here is to have a coach see your film. And then probably want to come see you in person, make time to carve out time to come see you in person." [R20 @06:14–06:31]; "Um, being consistent with it is super, super important. Okay, one good game film is not going to be enough." [R20 @06:31–06:37]; "if you're not getting interest, you're not, you don't have offers and coaches rolling in, get on the film." [R20 @09:59–10:09]
- **Applies to:** All grades from freshman year. Especially players without interest. Also players with interest but no offer.
- **Source:** [R20 @05:56–06:37, 09:56–10:12, 12:22–12:30]
- **Flags:** Corroborates STR-042 (R20-T ¶6, ¶13), FIT-013 ("Interest is not an offer") and STR-005 ("flood with film"). The video adds "keep sending even with interest." R20-T ¶6 only addresses the no-interest case. They complement each other rather than conflict. How often to send is MISSING (Q-057).

#### STR-072 · Start filming freshman year to learn the craft, then keep a rolling set of recent games
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** Record every game from freshman year. Filming from the stands, editing, cropping and storing all take practice ("it kind of takes a little bit of time, to get good at filming games"). Freshman film will be "replaced by… new and current film constantly," but interest can start freshman year, so it's good to have film "in your back pocket." During the season, rotate the set: keep the latest games, and when "a newer, fresher game" comes in, "drop one of those other games off."
- **Her words:** "starting freshman year… I highly, highly, highly, highly, highly suggest starting to record every single game… because I believe it kind of takes a little bit of time, to get good at filming games, especially… if you're a parent in the stands and you're watching… it can be a little challenging to keep up, but also just learning how to edit them, and… knowing how to start to store them" [R20 @00:15–00:49]; "you can always start getting interest freshman year, so if you are getting that interest, then you already have film and stuff like that too in your back pocket." [R20 @01:08–01:20]; "have it be replaced, be replenished, right? Like try to, okay, we have it. These five games, well, we got a newer, fresher game. Okay. Drop one of those other games off and so on." [R20 @08:10–08:23]
- **Applies to:** 9th grade on (filming); every season (rotation).
- **Source:** [R20 @00:11–01:23, 08:07–08:23, 10:09–10:39]
- **Flags:** Corroborates STR-008 and EVAL-027 (M2.G9: film from freshman year, "replace it each year"). The video goes further: replace continuously during the season, not just yearly. "Five games" is her example of a working set, matching FILMORG-T ¶13 (Game 1–5). It's not a rule. #### STR-073 · Targeted highlights: show the coach what she hasn't seen yet
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** Highlights come after a coach has watched two or three full games, or seen her in person once. Then a highlight tape shows improvement ("this is how I keep stacking it on") or fills a specific gap in what the coach has seen. Her examples: they "haven't seen you hit threes," so send the threes from the weekend you "finally went off from threes," or "look at all these drives I had this weekend with my left hand." Keep them to "little quick bits," and send another full game along with them.
- **Her words:** "I'm not completely against them… I just believe in them after… full game film has already been sent" [R20 @08:28–08:35]; "now you can send them a highlight tape of like, this is how I keep improving, this is how I keep stacking it on… Maybe they haven't seen you hit threes. So you're like, okay, I finally went off from threes. So I'm showing them some threes. Or they haven't seen you drive with your left hand. So now you showed them a highlight reel of look at all these drives I had this weekend with my left hand." [R20 @08:57–09:20]; "Little Quick Bits, but then you can also, like, stack another Game Film on there with those" [R20 @09:20–09:26]; "I just believe in them, again, being, working together… not just one or the other." [R20 @09:28–09:34]
- **Applies to:** Players a coach already knows (some interest established).
- **Source:** [R20 @08:23–09:41]
- **Flags:** Corroborates STR-042 (R20-T ¶9–10: "A highlight tape should enhance your reputation"). New in the video: highlights aimed at a specific gap the coach hasn't seen, and pairing them with another full game. This lines up with EVAL-003 (coaches watch specific, fixable things, e.g. "can… finish with her left hand").

#### STR-074 · Film must be watchable and dated: framing, jersey number, name, grad year, event and date
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** The picture has to be clear. Jersey numbers should be readable, and the camera shouldn't be "super zoomed out" or "super zoomed in" (another reason to practice filming early). Her name and graduation year go "on everything." The high school goes on high school film. For non-HS film it's "not necessarily as important," but make clear whether it's AAU or HS. Date it, so a coach knows it's current and not "film from three years ago. She's not even the same player anymore." Her label example: "this was from Run [for] the Roses on July 17th." The objection "coaches don't have time to watch film" really means "coaches don't have time to watch film that's unorganized." When it's easy, "they're more than willing to watch. Coaches watch film all the time."
- **Her words:** "make sure that it's clear. Make sure that you… can see the jersey numbers… really what's going on. It's not, like, super zoomed out or, like, super zoomed in and hard to read. That's also why I recommend practicing" [R20 @10:52–11:09]; "If you don't really know, you can have a date so they know what's up to date. So it's not like, oh, well, this is film from three years ago. She's not even the same player anymore" [R20 @11:31–11:39]; "Well, coaches don't have time to watch film that's unorganized… But if it's easy, they're more than willing to watch. Coaches watch film all the time… so it's not… like adding film to their play is really a big deal." [R20 @11:56–12:17]
- **Applies to:** All film sent to coaches.
- **Source:** [R20 @10:41–12:30]

## 3. Exposure: camps, showcases, club/AAU, events

#### STR-010 · Know why you're at an event, by grade
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:**
  - **7th–8th grade:** Go to *some* big events for comfort with big gyms, multiple courts and noise, and to feel that coaches are watching. Don't expect offers or even interest. Don't spend "weekend after weekend" and "so much money" on them.
  - **9th grade:** More intentional. Learn live periods.
  - **10th grade on:** Live events only, chosen around schools with real interest.
- **Source:** [M1.L2 @04:51–05:36], [M2.G7 @00:41–01:39], [M2.G9 @01:05–01:38], [M2.G10 @00:33–00:40], [R11 @03:46–04:57]

#### STR-011 · Use the D1 calendar to choose events, whatever level you're aiming for
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** Big tournaments are scheduled around when D1 coaches can attend, and D2 mostly follows that schedule because of budgets. Even if she's aiming for JUCO, understand the D1 calendar: "the skeletal system of how these tournaments are going to operate." Line up the AAU schedule against it ("only two of these out of seven are even going to be opportunities to get recruited").
- **Source:** [R11 @07:35–08:10], [R12 @10:09–11:06]
- **Flags:** ⚠️ VERIFY calendar specifics (V-04)

#### STR-012 · Count the coaches at your court
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** For freshmen and up, avoid always playing on "court 58" while coaches line up at courts 1–10. If there aren't several coaches watching, that's not a good sign.
- **Source:** [R11 @02:59–03:46]

#### STR-013 · Time not spent at tournaments goes into skill development
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** "I don't value them going to tournaments and tournaments… when they could be using that time to broaden their game" (ball handling, shooting, driving). Watch for AAU coaches who say "it's so she gets recruited" to justify endless games.
- **Source:** [R11 @08:31–09:20]

#### STR-014 · A couple of tune-up tournaments before live periods are fine
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** After HS season ends in March, playing one or two AAU tournaments helps the team get used to each other before the spring live periods, "so… we're ready when the coaches actually come."
- **Source:** [R12 @11:12–11:31], [R11 @08:15–08:27]
- **Flags:** ⚔️ Slight tension with "only… Live Events" in 10th grade (Q-021)

#### STR-015 · Elite camps: what they're for, by grade
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:**
  - **Freshman:** Go to some, especially if invited, for experience and to rise to the occasion. Not to get recruited.
  - **Sophomore:** Be more intentional. Don't go to all five invites. Pick about two from the schools you're most interested in, "show them really what I've got," and ask for feedback. The 10th-grade tab says 2–3 (2 good, 3 high, 1 fine).
- **Source:** [R13 @03:51–06:04], [M2.G10 @00:40–00:47], [M2.G9 @01:38–02:01]

#### STR-045 · Before you book a tournament, ask who will actually watch
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** "Don't confuse activity with progress." Questions: Is it during a period when coaches can evaluate? Which coaches are expected? Will they be on her courts? Does her AAU program have the relationships and reputation to get players evaluated? Is it right for her age and stage? "Are we spending money because this event is genuinely valuable — or because everyone else is going?" 7th–8th graders: non-live big events still have value for comfort, "but that shouldn't be the end goal."
- **Source:** [R11-T ¶1–10]

#### STR-046 · Elite camps by grade (written)
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** Freshman: experience. Watch whether she rises to the competition, communicates, competes when she's not the best, and handles challenge. Sophomore: be intentional. Ask "What schools… are actually interesting to my daughter?" and use that school's camp to get in front of the staff and judge fit. Camps build confidence ("I belong here"). "Know WHY you're attending."
- **Source:** [R13-T ¶6–9]
- **Flags:** The written text gives no camp count (the video said 2, or 2–3).

#### STR-047 · Recruiting trips are business trips
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** At an exposure tournament: locked in at the gym, focused in warmups, engaged on the bench, composed between games, appropriate at the hotel and restaurants. "You never know who knows who." It applies at school too.
- **Source:** [ALWAYSEVAL-T ¶2–8]

#### STR-066 · High-school-exposure tactics: pick the day, pick the event, then keep them coming back
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** Her video adds these tactics to "make the trip make sense":
  - **Events that gather teams:** season-kickoff tournaments, Thanksgiving or Christmas classics, traditional tournaments, and the state postseason ("regionals and sectionals and however each state does it").
  - **Name the other recruit:** if a school is recruiting "number four" on another team at the tournament, email: "I know that you're recruiting some people at this tournament already. My game is at 7 p.m."
  - **Check their schedule:** look up the college team's schedule and invite them on a day they don't have a game.
  - **After they've seen her in person:** "now you can send more film because they've seen you in person and they have a better idea." This is the "snowball" that becomes "a really, really, really big snowman."
  - **Stay top of mind:** keep in contact, keep inviting, and use their camps "to see you again."
- **Her words:** "you can also have to, um, check their schedule and, you know, invite them on a day that, um, they don't have a game, because you can find that information as well." [R18 @05:36–05:49]; "Then now you can send more film because they've seen you in person and they have a better idea. And that's how you get that… little, you know, snowball to get into a really, really, really big snowman." [R18 @07:37–07:50]; "it'd be a good opportunity to maybe try to hit up some camps to see you again." [R18 @08:24–08:30]

## 5. Working with high school and club coaches

#### STR-018 · Choosing an AAU team: stability first, then the coach's priorities
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** Stay with one AAU team from 8th grade on (see EVAL-025). Switch only for real problems: "bite the bullet." What makes a good AAU coach:
  1. **Cares more about getting players recruited than about winning.** Uses a real rotation, gives everyone time, lets players make mistakes without being pulled, doesn't sub out to avoid a mismatch.
  2. **Serves the family, not themselves.** Asks what she actually wants and doesn't oversell.
  3. **Develops skills.** Mostly skill work and motion offense, with a few set plays, not "plays, plays, plays."

  Smaller rosters: "seven or eight… Nine, maybe. Ten's too much." Fewer players means less drama and everyone plays. Today's teams often hold tryouts, so ask the coach questions to judge fit.

#### STR-035 · What makes a great AAU team (written)
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** Find the fit around 7th–8th grade and give it time. Frequent switching makes her "difficult to follow" and undermines loyalty. Key question: "Does this coach care more about my daughter's future than winning the next game?" Winning still matters (better brackets and courts), but not "at the expense of your daughter's development or recruiting opportunity." A good AAU coach doesn't need hundreds of connections. They need to communicate honestly with college coaches, understand her goals, help coaches find her, reach out when appropriate, and know when to step back. Questions before choosing: Will she play? Will they develop her? Will they help her get *evaluated*? Will they communicate with coaches? Do they understand recruiting? Will they support her goals? Can we build a multi-year relationship? Don't choose on "the biggest name, the best uniforms, or the most wins."

#### STR-036 · When to leave an AAU team, and how
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** Talk with the coach first. Legitimate reasons:
  - No meaningful playing opportunity after discussing it
  - Coaching that damages her confidence
  - Priorities that conflict with her goals
  - No development
  - Promises not kept
  - An unhealthy environment
  - Wrong position with no willingness to develop her
  - The wrong level of competition
  - Being blocked from appropriate opportunities
  - Communication breakdown
  - The program has fundamentally changed
  - Safety or welfare concerns (no need to stay)

  If you leave, "don't disappear": send programs the new team, coach contact, schedule, court numbers and contact changes ("We changed programs, but we haven't disappeared"). Leave professionally. The test: "Am I leaving because this is genuinely no longer the right environment… or because I think another team will get her recruited faster?"

#### STR-037 · Choosing a skills trainer: pay for development, not difficulty
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** A good trainer asks her position, target level, strengths, weaknesses, game struggles, what skills she needs, what her coach sees, and what the next level requires, then has a plan. Ask: Is it improving something she needs? Can she use it in a game? Is she getting feedback? Are the details corrected? Is it progressing? Quality group training (competitive small groups) is valuable, but 20 kids in lines isn't. "She needs the right training at the right time for the right reason." Sometimes she needs to "go play."

#### STR-060 · Make their part a 10-to-30-minute job

## 7. Parent involvement and recruiting strategy

#### STR-030 · What parents do early on
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** Early on, the parent carries most of the logistics: which tournaments, which team, the first conversations with coaches. Parents also provide access to a gym, a hoop, the rec center, and weight training if school doesn't offer it. And they teach ownership: "you told me this is your goal, this is what it's going to take"; take responsibility for mistakes; don't complain. "7th and 8th grade, teaching, teaching, teaching." The athlete learns from watching how her parents handle things.
- **Written list:** Parents should be responsible for:
  1. Understanding the recruiting timeline and rules
  2. Learning how the recruiting process actually works
  3. Helping create a realistic plan
  4. Keeping track of important dates, events, academics, film, and communication
  5. Helping evaluate opportunities and spending decisions
  6. Making sure their daughter has the resources and support she needs
  7. Helping her communicate professionally when needed
  8. Asking thoughtful questions and helping her make informed decisions
  9. Protecting her from unnecessary pressure, confusion, and outside opinions
  10. Encouraging her when recruiting gets frustrating or uncertain
  11. Knowing when to step in, and when to step back
  
  "Your job is to guide the process. Her job is to own it."
- **Source:** [M1.L3 @01:20–01:43, 03:10–04:52], [M1.L3-T P1–P11, ¶1]

#### STR-053 · Teach her to own the logistics too
- **Label:** EXPLICIT

- **Source:** [RECINFO-T ¶11], [FUEL-T ¶6], [R19-T ¶13]

#### STR-034 · What the athlete gradually owns
- **Label:** EXPLICIT

  1. Knowing what she wants from her basketball career
  2. Understanding what coaches are looking for
  3. Developing her game and athletic ability
  4. Taking academics seriously
  5. Learning how to advocate for herself
  6. Communicating with coaches
  7. Watching and understanding her own film
  8. Knowing her schedule and recruiting information
  9. Building confidence and learning to handle rejection
  10. Developing the habits and character coaches evaluate
  11. Asking questions
  12. Being invested in her own future

#### STR-031 · Decide the decision circle early, and keep it small
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:**
  1. The athlete (leader).
  2. The parent(s) (guide).
  3. Optional, if they listen, don't take over, and give constructive feedback rather than ultimatums:
     - **High school coach:** "highly recommend" if active and willing.
     - **AAU coach:** good if they have time, you trust them and you communicate well.
     - **Skills trainer:** knows her game, often played in college.
     - **Grandparent:** good, but watch for alum bias.
     - **Family friend or mentor.**

  "I don't suggest that this list be very long." The written text adds: "more people does not automatically mean more help… too many voices can create confusion." Her example: AAU vs. switch teams vs. a different position vs. lifting more vs. a different school, until the athlete hears "six different versions of what her future should look like. That's not a plan." Choose people who "genuinely understand your daughter's goals, care about her development, and are willing to work together." Others "can support her from the outside without having a vote in every decision." Coaches will ask who's involved and will contact them, especially the HS and AAU coaches, to find out her interests, distance limits, major and growth. Everyone in the circle needs to know her actual wishes so they don't misrepresent her.

#### STR-054 · What you say vs. what she hears
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** First questions like "Did you win?", "How many points?", "Did you start?", "How many minutes?", "Why did Coach take you out?", "You should have shot that," "You're better than her," "That coach doesn't know what they're doing" can teach her that scoring, winning, starting and minutes define her worth. They can also teach her that coaches are the problem and teammates are competition. Results she names: forcing shots, devastation after low scoring, jealousy, every substitution felt as punishment. "Not about blaming parents." Step one is just to notice: "IF I WERE MY DAUGHTER, WHAT WOULD I THINK MATTERS MOST TO MY PARENT?"
- **Source:** [PARENTSAY-T ¶1–F5]

#### STR-055 · What to say instead
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** Change the first question: "HOW DID YOU FEEL ABOUT HOW YOU PLAYED?", then listen. Ask about what she controls (effort, attitude, communication, preparation, response to mistakes, coachability, hustle, focus). Praise more than the result ("I loved how hard you ran back after that turnover"). "WHAT YOU NOTICE BECOMES IMPORTANT." Let her talk first. "What did you learn today?" After a bad game: "Tough one. I love you. We'll get another chance." After a great game, go deeper than points. Don't make every car ride a film session. "Do you want to talk about the game now, later, or not at all?" Help her build her own evaluating voice. "I LOVE WATCHING YOU PLAY."
- **Source:** [PARENTINSTEAD-T ¶1–C12]

#### STR-056 · Coaches are watching parents too
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** "THE PLAYER DOESN'T ALWAYS COME ALONE." A coach may ask "DO I WANT TO DEAL WITH THIS FOR FOUR YEARS?" Parents can hurt recruiting: one frustrated moment won't, but "becoming a liability" can. Rules for the stands:
  - Don't coach from the stands.
  - Raise concerns with HS/AAU coaches at a set-up meeting, not right after games.
  - Let her have her own role conversations ("Coach, what do I need to improve to earn more opportunities?").
  - Leave referees alone.
  - Respect event staff.
  - Don't talk badly about other players.
  - When college coaches are present, don't perform, explain or chase them ("DON'T FORCE ACCESS").
  - Be "INVOLVED," not "INTRUSIVE."
  - She learns her responses from yours.
- **Source:** [PARENTWATCH-T ¶1–B14]

#### STR-057 · Support without creating pressure
- **Label:** EXPLICIT

Hidden pressure sounds like "Other girls are working while you're sitting here" or "We're spending all this money…" "You cannot want a college basketball career badly enough for both of you." "REST IS NOT LAZINESS." Burnout signs: dreading basketball, every basketball conversation becoming an argument, exhaustion, irritability, checking out. The answer may be space, not more pushing. She's still your daughter "whether she… Plays Division I or never plays college basketball at all."
