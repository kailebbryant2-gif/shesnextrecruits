[¶1] Before you start taking advice from everyone around you, decide who is actually going to be involved.
[¶2] That could include: Athlete · Parent/guardian · High school coach · AAU coach · Trainer · Grandparent · Other trusted family member · Mentor
[¶3] But more people does not automatically mean more help.
[¶4] In fact, too many voices can create confusion.
[¶5] One person might say she needs to play AAU. Another says she needs to switch teams. Someone else thinks she should play a different position. Her trainer thinks she needs to lift more. Her coach thinks she needs to work on something completely different. Grandma thinks she should look at one school. Dad likes another.
[¶6] And suddenly the athlete is hearing six different versions of what her future should look like.
[¶7] That's not a plan.
[¶8] Keep the circle tight.
[¶9] Choose the people who genuinely understand your daughter's goals, care about her development, and are willing to work together.
[¶10] Everyone else can support her from the outside without having a vote in every decision.
[¶11] And most importantly: Make sure your daughter has a voice at the table.
[¶12] This is her basketball career.
[¶13] You can guide her. You can educate her. You can advocate for her. You can open doors. You can help her make decisions.
[¶14] But you cannot care more about her future than she does.
[¶15] She has to be invested.
[¶16] The earlier she learns that this is something she has ownership in rather than something her parents are doing for her the better prepared she will be when it's time to communicate with coaches, evaluate opportunities, and ultimately make her own college decision.
[¶17] We want to build the support system around the her, not talk and decide for her.
