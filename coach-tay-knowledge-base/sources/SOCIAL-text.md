[¶1] Here's something families don't always realize about recruiting: A college coach may know a lot about your daughter before they ever speak to her. Imagine a coach is sitting at a showcase tournament. They see a player they like. They write down her name. Or maybe another coach tells them: "Hey, you need to check out this kid." Maybe your high school coach sends them an email. Maybe your AAU coach mentions you. Maybe you send the recruiting staff an email with your film. What's one of the easiest things for that coach to do next? Pull out their phone and search your name. And very quickly, they're starting to put together a picture of who you are.
[¶2] YOUR ONLINE PRESENCE IS PART OF YOUR RECRUITING PROFILE. For basketball recruiting, X — formerly Twitter — can be an incredibly useful tool. A well-built profile can immediately tell a coach: Graduation year. Height. Position. High school. AAU program. Jersey number. Film. Contact information. And something coaches are absolutely going to notice: YOUR OFFERS. If you've received offers and you're posting them, a coach can quickly see which programs have already decided you're worth recruiting. That information can influence the initial picture they're building of you.
[¶3] OFFERS CREATE CONTEXT. Let's say a coach watches you play and thinks: "I like her." Then they search your profile and see that several programs at their level have already offered you. That can reinforce their initial evaluation. They may think: "Okay. Other programs we compete with are seeing something here too." It can create another layer of credibility around the player. On the other hand, maybe they search and see that the programs showing the most serious interest so far are primarily from a lower level than theirs. That can create context too. They may wonder: "What are those programs seeing?" "Why haven't programs at our level offered yet?" "Is she someone we need to evaluate more closely?" That doesn't mean they automatically decide you can't play at their level. And it certainly doesn't mean your offers determine your ability. COACHES STILL HAVE TO EVALUATE YOU FOR THEMSELVES. But remember what we've talked about throughout this course: Recruiting is a process of gathering information. Your film is information. Your size is information. Your position is information. Your AAU program is information. Your high school coach's opinion is information. Your performance is information. Your recruiting activity can become information too.
[¶4] DON'T LET THAT SCARE YOU. I don't want athletes reading this and thinking: "Well, I don't have any offers, so coaches are going to think I'm terrible." No. Every player's recruiting process starts at zero. Someone has to be the first offer. And we've already talked about how recruiting timelines, positions, roster needs, exposure, geography, and countless other factors affect when offers happen. An offer is not a ranking system for your worth as a basketball player. I'm explaining this because I want you to understand how coaches gather information and how quickly an initial impression can form. And that brings us to something even bigger.
[¶5] WHAT ELSE IS THAT COACH GOING TO SEE? The coach came to your X profile looking for basketball information. But while they're there... They're going to see everything else you've posted too. They're going to see your reposts. Your replies. Your photos. Your captions. The things you joke about. The way you talk about other people. The things you complain about. The way you talk about coaches. Potentially the language you use. And if they search your name beyond X, they may find your Instagram, TikTok, or other public accounts too. That's why this conversation isn't really just about X. IT'S ABOUT YOUR ENTIRE ONLINE PRESENCE.
[¶6] YOU ARE ALLOWED TO BE A TEENAGER. I want to be very clear about this. I'm not telling young athletes that their social media has to look like it was created by a 40-year-old marketing department. Be a kid. Have friends. Laugh. Post pictures. Have interests outside basketball. Go to dances. Go on vacation. Be silly. Have a personality. Basketball does not need to consume every part of your life. But we've talked about this before: YOU HAVE SOMETHING AT STAKE. Your friend who has absolutely no desire to play college sports may not think twice about what she posts. She might post something with inappropriate language. She might repost something vulgar. She might post a picture that you wouldn't necessarily want a college coach seeing. She gets to make those choices for herself. But your goals are different. If you're asking a college to invest a roster spot, scholarship money, coaching, resources, and years of development into you, it is smart to be more cautious about the public version of yourself. That's not unfair. That's understanding what you're working toward.
[¶7] BEFORE YOU POST, ASK ONE QUESTION. You don't have to analyze every picture for 30 minutes. Just get into the habit of asking: "WOULD I CARE IF A COLLEGE COACH SAW THIS?" If the answer is: "Absolutely not." Great. Post it. If the answer is: "Uhhhh... probably." That should tell you something. Err on the side of caution. You have worked too hard on your game, your body, your academics, your film, your communication, your exposure, and your recruiting process to create an unnecessary question mark because of a social media post.
[¶8] THINK ABOUT THE SCROLL. This is the part I really want athletes to picture. A coach finds your X account because they're looking for your recruiting information. They see your bio. They start scrolling. They're looking for film. They're looking for your schedule. They're looking for offers. They're trying to learn more about you. What are they scrolling past to get there? That's the question. If they have to scroll past: Constant profanity. Inappropriate or overly provocative content. Disrespectful comments. Drama. Fighting with people online. Complaining about playing time. Negative comments about coaches. Making fun of teammates or opponents. Reposting offensive material. Or anything else that makes them question your judgment... You just gave them information you probably never intended to give them. And remember: They don't know you yet. They don't have years of context about your personality. They're forming an impression from what you've chosen to make public.
[¶9] THIS DOESN'T MEAN ONE POST AUTOMATICALLY ENDS YOUR RECRUITING. I don't want to create unnecessary fear around social media either. Coaches understand they're recruiting teenagers. They're not expecting perfection. One silly post isn't automatically going to destroy your basketball career. But recruiting boards can have a lot of names on them. We've talked about this. A coach is constantly gathering reasons to move someone up, keep evaluating them, or move on. So why introduce unnecessary questions? Your goal isn't to create a fake personality online. Your goal is simply: DON'T GIVE A COACH A REASON TO QUESTION SOMETHING THAT HAS NOTHING TO DO WITH YOUR ABILITY TO PLAY BASKETBALL.
[¶10] YOUR SOCIAL MEDIA CAN ACTUALLY HELP YOU. I also don't want this lesson to sound like social media is something athletes should be afraid of. It can be an incredible recruiting tool. Your profile can show coaches: Your development. Your schedule. Your film. Your workouts. Your accomplishments. Your offers. Your personality. Your teammates. Your work ethic. Your excitement about basketball. Your academic accomplishments. Your character. Your interests outside the sport. You can create an online presence that makes a coach think: "I want to learn more about this kid." That's powerful.
[¶11] DO AN ONLINE AUDIT. Every athlete who wants to play college basketball should occasionally search her own name and look at her accounts like a stranger would. Not like yourself. Not like your best friend. Not like your mom. LIKE A COLLEGE COACH. Open your X. Open your Instagram. Open your TikTok. Google your name. Look at your bio. Look at your profile picture. Look at your reposts. Scroll back. Ask: Can a coach quickly figure out who I am? Can they find my graduation year? Can they find my height and position? Can they find my team? Can they find my film? Can they find my recruiting information? And then ask: "IS THERE ANYTHING HERE I WOULD HAVE TO EXPLAIN?" That's a great question. Because if there is... You may want to clean it up before someone else finds it.
[¶12] YOUR DIGITAL REPUTATION IS STILL YOUR REPUTATION. We've talked about how you carry yourself at tournaments. We've talked about the bench. We've talked about warmups. We've talked about hotels. We've talked about school. We've talked about how you act when you think nobody important is watching. Social media is simply another extension of that conversation. Who are you when the gym isn't watching? Because now, sometimes... The gym can find out.
[¶13] MAKE THEIR FIRST IMPRESSION EASY. When a coach searches your name, I want them to find a profile that makes their job easier. They should quickly understand: WHO YOU ARE. WHAT CLASS YOU'RE IN. WHERE YOU PLAY. WHAT POSITION YOU PLAY. HOW BIG YOU ARE. WHERE THEY CAN WATCH YOUR FILM. WHAT YOUR RECRUITING ACTIVITY LOOKS LIKE. And as they scroll, they should see a young person who appears to understand that she is representing herself. Not a perfect person. Not a fake person. A smart one. Because you have worked incredibly hard to get a coach interested enough to search your name. DON'T WASTE THAT MOMENT. MAKE SURE WHAT THEY FIND GIVES THEM ANOTHER REASON TO KEEP LOOKING.
