[I1] This is one of my favorite topics to talk about because... I USED TO RUN THESE. Organizing recruiting visits was one of the biggest parts of my job as a recruiting coordinator. I planned official visits. I planned unofficial visits. I created itineraries. I coordinated with professors. I found players to spend time with recruits. I organized meals. I arranged tours. I helped create the experience the recruit and her family were going to have when they stepped onto our campus. And here's the part families don't always realize: EVERY VISIT WAS NOT DESIGNED THE SAME. Some visits? We wanted that kid. BAD. We wanted her leaving campus thinking: THIS IS WHERE I WANT TO GO. Those were the visits where we were pulling out all the stops. And sometimes? We liked the player. We wanted to stay on her list. But we weren't ready to go all-in. Maybe someone else was higher on our recruiting board. Maybe we were still evaluating her. Maybe we wanted to keep the relationship alive while we waited to see what another recruit did. Those visits could still be good. But they weren't necessarily designed to have her walking out ready to commit. YES — THERE CAN BE A DIFFERENCE. That doesn't mean every program operates exactly the way mine did. I'm telling you what I personally saw and participated in from the other side of the desk. And I want families to understand what's happening when they walk onto campus.
[D1] FIRST: WHAT IS AN OFFICIAL VISIT? The simplest definition: THE SCHOOL IS PAYING FOR AT LEAST PART OF THE VISIT. An NCAA official visit is a visit financed in whole or in part by the college. Depending on the division and the circumstances, NCAA rules allow schools to cover certain things such as transportation, lodging, meals, reasonable entertainment and admissions to home athletic events. The exact benefits differ by division. This is why official visits often feel more elaborate. The school has much more ability to create an experience around you.
[D2] WHAT IS AN UNOFFICIAL VISIT? The simplest definition: YOU ARE GENERALLY PAYING YOUR OWN WAY. An unofficial visit is primarily at the athlete/family's expense. You can still: Meet coaches. Tour campus. See facilities. Talk basketball. Ask questions. Meet people. Spend time learning about the program when recruiting rules permit. But the school has tighter restrictions on what it can provide. Across NCAA divisions, prospects can take unlimited unofficial visits, subject to applicable sport-specific timing and recruiting-calendar restrictions. And don't make the mistake of thinking: UNOFFICIAL = THEY AREN'T SERIOUS ABOUT ME. Not necessarily. Unofficial simply describes how the visit is being financed and governed.
[V1] NOW LET ME TELL YOU WHAT A VISIT CAN ACTUALLY LOOK LIKE. When a program REALLY wants you... They want you to feel special. And they are good at it. Because that's recruiting. We might ask your: Shoe size. Jersey number. Favorite foods. Favorite snacks. We knew things about you before you arrived. You walk into the locker room... and there might be your jersey. Your number. Your name. Your information printed on an itinerary. Everything feels personalized. You take pictures. You put on the uniform. You stand in the arena. You see yourself on the video board or in graphics where permissible. And suddenly you're imagining yourself there. THAT'S THE POINT.
[V2] THE PICTURES ARE SUPPOSED TO MAKE YOU FEEL SOMETHING. You put on that jersey... And for a second you're not imagining being recruited anymore. You're imagining being on the team. That's powerful. The photos. The locker room. The arena. The gear. Your name. The jersey. The graphics. They're helping you visualize: "THIS COULD BE MY LIFE." Again... That's recruiting. Enjoy it. Take the pictures. Have fun. Feel special. Just understand what's happening.
[V3] AND THEN THERE'S THE ITINERARY. A really good visit can feel effortless. But behind the scenes? Somebody may have spent HOURS making it feel that way. Where you're eating. Who you're meeting. When you're touring. Which professor you're talking to. Which player is spending time with you. Which coach sits next to Mom. Which coach talks basketball with Dad. When you see the locker room. When you see the arena. When you take pictures. When you have the serious conversation. It's intentional. I know because... I WAS THE PERSON MAKING THE SCHEDULE.
[V4] IF WE REALLY WANTED YOU... We wanted to know: What's your favorite restaurant? What kind of food do you like? What's your favorite snack? Do you love ice cream? Okay. We're going for ice cream. You love a certain restaurant? Maybe that's where we're eating. You have a particular academic interest? We're finding someone in that department you'll connect with. You have a certain personality? We're thinking about which player you'll connect with. You value faith? Maybe we're connecting you with people who can speak to that part of campus life. You're outgoing? Maybe we're pairing you with one of our most outgoing players. You're nervous? We're finding someone who is warm and easy to talk to. WE WANTED YOU TO CONNECT. Because if you connect emotionally with the people and the experience... You are much more likely to picture yourself there.
[V5] AND YES — WHO YOU MEET CAN BE INTENTIONAL. This is something I REALLY want families to understand. If I'm organizing your visit... Why would I intentionally pair you with the teammate who's miserable? Why would I choose the quietest person on campus if I want you to have great conversations? Why would I arrange your academic meeting with someone who doesn't connect well with recruits? I wouldn't. I'm trying to show you the program at its best. That doesn't mean anyone is lying. The outgoing player really is on the team. The incredible professor really does teach there. The beautiful dorm really exists. The amazing restaurant really is nearby. BUT YOU ARE BEING SHOWN A CURATED VERSION. That's the part I want you to remember.
[V6] GET PAST THE FIRST LINE. This might be my biggest advice for visits: TALK TO PEOPLE THEY DIDN'T SPECIFICALLY PUT IN FRONT OF YOU. The player assigned to you? Absolutely talk to her. But also talk to the girl sitting at the end of the bench. Talk to the sophomore who barely plays. Talk to the senior. Talk to the freshman. Talk to the injured player. Talk to the transfer. Talk to athletes from another sport. Talk to students walking around campus. If you're eating in the dining hall, look around. If you're touring the dorm, pay attention. If you meet another professor in your intended major, ask questions. You want to know: WHAT DOES THIS PLACE FEEL LIKE WHEN NOBODY IS TRYING TO RECRUIT ME?
[V7] TALK TO THE PLAYER WHO ISN'T PLAYING. This is one of my favorites. Everyone loves Coach when they're starting and playing 30 minutes. I want to know: How does Coach treat you when you're NOT playing? Ask: "How does Coach communicate with you about your role?" "Can you go into Coach's office?" "How does the staff handle players who are struggling?" "What happens after a bad game?" "What's the hardest part about playing here?" Those answers tell me a lot more than: "Do you like Coach?"
[V8] PAY ATTENTION TO WHAT THEY DON'T SHOW YOU. Beautiful arena? Great. What do the dorms look like? Beautiful practice facility? Great. Where do freshmen actually live? Amazing training room? Great. What does treatment look like at 6 a.m. when 12 athletes need something? Great academic presentation? Great. What happens when a required lab conflicts with practice? Incredible dining hall? Great. Is it open when basketball practice ends? KEEP GOING ONE QUESTION DEEPER.
[V9] OFFICIAL DOES NOT AUTOMATICALLY MEAN OFFER. This is another misconception. An official visit is meaningful. The school is investing resources into getting you to campus. But: OFFICIAL VISIT ≠ AUTOMATIC OFFER. And it definitely doesn't automatically mean: You're their #1 recruit. Look at the entire relationship. How frequently are they communicating? Have they clearly explained where you stand? Have they offered? What role are they discussing? Who else are they recruiting? Where are you on their board? Remember: DON'T COUNT CONTACT. COUNT COMMITMENT.
[V10] AND UNOFFICIAL DOES NOT MEAN UNIMPORTANT. A coach may REALLY want a player on campus for an unofficial visit. Especially when the athlete is close enough to drive. An unofficial visit can still include meaningful conversations and give you an incredible amount of information. Sometimes an unofficial visit can actually be helpful because there is less production around it. You may get a more ordinary look at campus. Use that.
[R0] THE NCAA RULES YOU NEED TO KNOW. These rules change, so families should ALWAYS confirm the current rules with the school's compliance office before making travel plans. But as of the 2026–27 recruiting cycle, here's the important framework.
[R1] NCAA DIVISION I WOMEN'S BASKETBALL — OFFICIAL VISITS. For women's basketball, Division I official visits may begin January 1 of the athlete's junior year of high school. Prospects are no longer limited to the old five total Division I official visits. They may take official visits to as many different NCAA schools as they want after the permissible date, but generally only one official visit per school; a head-coaching change can create an exception allowing another visit. Before a Division I official visit, the athlete must provide the school with an academic transcript, register with the NCAA Eligibility Center and be placed on that school's institutional request list. Current Division I rules permit no more than two consecutive nights of lodging in conjunction with the official visit. Division I can provide allowable transportation, lodging, up to three meals per day plus reasonable snacks, and specified reasonable entertainment within NCAA limits. For women's basketball specifically, official visits generally cannot occur during the July evaluation periods unless the athlete has reached one of the specified commitment-related exceptions in the rule. UNOFFICIAL VISITS: In Division I basketball, an unofficial visit with athletics-department involvement can occur at any time, subject to the women's basketball recruiting calendar. The athlete can take an unlimited number of unofficial visits. Women's basketball has an important July restriction: unofficial visits generally cannot occur during the July evaluation periods unless the athlete meets the applicable commitment-related exception.
[R2] NCAA DIVISION II — OFFICIAL VISITS. A Division II official visit may begin June 15 immediately preceding the athlete's junior year of high school. An institution generally finances one official visit for a prospect, with limited exceptions such as a head-coaching change or the separate post-high-school period. Current NCAA guidance also confirms that prospects can take official visits to as many different NCAA schools as they like after the permissible date. The athlete must satisfy the applicable pre-visit requirements, including providing the required academic information; current NCAA guidance also identifies Eligibility Center registration for Division I and II official visits. The official visit may not exceed 48 hours. UNOFFICIAL VISITS: Division II prospects may make unlimited unofficial visits at their own expense. NCAA Division II rules also permit unofficial visits before June 15 preceding junior year, though what coaches can do and the recruiting activity allowed can depend on other recruiting rules and calendar restrictions.
[R3] NCAA DIVISION III — OFFICIAL VISITS. A Division III official visit can begin January 1 of the prospective student-athlete's junior year. A prospect gets one official visit per Division III institution. The visit cannot exceed 48 hours. Division III schools may provide allowable lodging, meals, entertainment and transportation within their rules. And remember something we've discussed throughout this course: DIVISION III DOES NOT OFFER ATHLETIC SCHOLARSHIPS. But Division III programs absolutely recruit athletes, and official visits can still be part of that process. UNOFFICIAL VISITS: Division III unofficial visits can occur at any time and are unlimited. There is no general time limit on the length of the unofficial visit. Current Division III rules permit one on-campus meal for the prospect and accompanying individuals on an unofficial visit, but lodging and entertainment are much more restricted than on an official visit.
[R4] WHAT ABOUT NAIA? Do NOT assume NCAA recruiting rules apply to NAIA programs. They don't. NAIA recruiting is significantly more open. The NAIA states that there are no recruiting-calendar restrictions, and prospective athletes can communicate directly with NAIA coaches. Because NAIA visit practices and what a particular institution will pay for can differ from the NCAA framework, ask the school directly: "What expenses are you covering for this visit?" "Are there any restrictions we need to know about?" "Is this considered a recruiting visit under your institutional or conference policies?" Don't try to apply Division I terminology and assumptions to every NAIA school.
[R5] WHAT ABOUT JUCO? Junior-college recruiting rules are also their own world. NJCAA schools operate under NJCAA recruiting rules rather than NCAA Division I, II or III visit rules. So again: ASK THE SCHOOL EXACTLY WHAT IT IS OFFERING. Is transportation covered? Hotel? Meals? How long is the visit? Can family attend? Is this considered an official recruiting visit? What expenses are covered? And because junior-college rules can change, verify the current NJCAA rules with the athletic department before making plans.
[E1] NOW HERE'S WHAT I CARE ABOUT MORE THAN THE RULES. I want you to understand: A VISIT IS A RECRUITING EXPERIENCE. Enjoy it. But evaluate it. If they put your name everywhere... Enjoy it. If they put you in the uniform... Take the pictures. If they know your favorite snacks... Eat them. If they take you to your favorite restaurant... Have a great dinner. If they take you for ice cream... Get the ice cream. YOU ARE ALLOWED TO LOVE BEING RECRUITED. Just don't confuse: FEELING SPECIAL FOR 48 HOURS with BEING HAPPY THERE FOR FOUR YEARS.
[E2] WATCH THE EFFORT — BUT DON'T OVERANALYZE EVERY DETAIL. I told you that when I worked in recruiting, there were visits where we pulled out all the stops. That information matters. But I don't want you leaving a visit thinking: "They didn't get her favorite candy, so they must not really want her." No. Different schools have different budgets. Different staffs organize visits differently. Different divisions have different rules. Sometimes someone simply forgets something. Look at the whole recruiting relationship. Did they prepare? Do they know your daughter? Are they communicating consistently? Are they clear about where she stands? Have they offered? Are they answering difficult questions? Does their behavior match their words? That's much more meaningful.
[E3] AND WHEN THEY SHOW YOU THEIR BEST... GO LOOK FOR NORMAL. This is the lesson I want you to remember. Recruiting is designed to show you the best. YOU NEED TO FIND NORMAL. Talk to the player they didn't assign you. Walk through the regular student areas. Eat where players actually eat. Ask what Tuesday practice is like. Ask the bench player about Coach. Ask the injured player how she was treated. Ask the senior what she wishes she knew as a freshman. Ask the student in your major whether balancing it with basketball is realistic. Look at the dorm she'll ACTUALLY live in. Find out what players ACTUALLY do during summer. Ask when they ACTUALLY get to go home for Christmas. Because you're not choosing: THE 48-HOUR VISIT. You're choosing what happens when the visit is over.
[E4] THE VISIT IS THEIR OPPORTUNITY TO RECRUIT YOU. IT IS ALSO YOUR OPPORTUNITY TO RECRUIT INFORMATION. Go in with questions. Go in with your list. Pay attention. Enjoy the experience. But don't be afraid to look behind the curtain. Because I have BEEN behind that curtain. I helped create the experience. I knew who we wanted recruits to meet. I knew what we wanted them to see. I knew what we wanted them to feel. And that's exactly why I'm telling you: DON'T ONLY PAY ATTENTION TO WHAT THEY PUT IN FRONT OF YOU. PAY ATTENTION TO WHAT YOU FIND WHEN YOU LOOK PAST IT. That's how you stop choosing the recruiting fairy tale... and start figuring out whether you actually want the real life that comes after it.
