# R11-T · Being at a Tournament Is Not the Same as Being Evaluated (written text)

[¶1] One of the biggest misconceptions I see from parents is: "We're going to all of these tournaments, so our daughter is getting exposure." Not necessarily.
[¶2] You can spend an entire weekend at an exposure event, play five games, and have very little actual recruiting evaluation happen. I've seen families spend thousands of dollars traveling to tournaments, only to end up on Court 58 — while the coaches they want to see are spending their time on Courts 1–10.
[¶3] Being at the tournament is not the same thing as being where coaches are evaluating. Before you spend the money on another tournament, ask yourself: Who is actually going to be watching my daughter play? That question should influence which events you attend, which AAU program you choose, and where you spend your recruiting budget.
[¶4] This is why your AAU team matters. Getting on the right team — ideally as early as possible — can make a huge difference. You want to be around coaches and organizations that understand the difference between simply attending exposure events and actually putting athletes in situations where college coaches are evaluating.
[¶5] You don't need to chase every tournament. You need to be intentional about the right tournaments, the right team, the right time, and the right level of exposure.
[¶6] What about 7th and 8th grade? This is where I want to make an important distinction. When your daughter is younger, attending larger events that aren't live periods can still have value. She can learn what these environments feel like. She can get comfortable playing in large gyms, around multiple courts, with college coaches walking around. She can learn how to compete when she knows she's being watched. And she can begin understanding what it means to perform in an environment that feels different from a normal weekend tournament. But that shouldn't be the end goal.
[¶7] As your daughter gets older and recruiting becomes more serious, when and where she is playing matters much more. You need to understand the NCAA recruiting calendar and know when college coaches are permitted to evaluate prospects in person. The NCAA recruiting calendar is public information, and I have included the current calendar in your resources so you can reference it throughout the recruiting process.
[¶8] Don't confuse activity with progress. Going to more tournaments doesn't automatically mean you're doing more recruiting. Five tournaments where the right coaches aren't present may be less valuable than one strategically chosen event where your daughter is actually being evaluated. The goal isn't to be everywhere. The goal is to be where it matters.
[¶9] Before you book the next tournament, ask: Is this event during a period when college coaches can evaluate? What coaches are actually expected to be there? Will they be on the courts where my daughter is playing? Does her AAU program have relationships and a reputation that help get players evaluated? Is this event appropriate for her current age and recruiting stage? Are we spending money because this event is genuinely valuable — or because everyone else is going?
[¶10] You don't need to chase exposure. You need to understand exposure.
