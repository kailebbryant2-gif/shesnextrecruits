[¶1] Let's talk about one of the most debated topics in recruiting: Highlight reels vs. full game film. You are going to hear different opinions about this. Some people will tell you: "Coaches don't have time to watch full games." Others will tell you: "You have to have a great highlight tape." Here's my opinion from being on the other side of this: FULL GAME FILM FIRST. ALWAYS. Especially when you are first getting your name out there.
[¶2] YOUR HIGHLIGHT REEL SHOWS YOUR BEST MOMENTS. Of course it does. That's the point. You can take your best shots, best passes, best finishes, best defensive plays and put them all together. And there's nothing wrong with that. But here's the problem: A highlight reel doesn't necessarily tell a coach who you are as a player. It tells them what you look like when things go right.
[¶3] A college coach needs to know: How do you move without the ball? What do you do when you don't have the ball? How do you defend? Do you communicate? How do you react after a mistake? Do you make the right decision when your first option isn't there? Can you handle pressure? Do you rebound? Do you sprint back? Do you play hard when nobody is scoring? What happens when you're having a bad shooting night? How do you impact the game when your "superpower" isn't working? A highlight reel can't answer all of those questions. A full game can.
[¶4] FULL GAME FILM SHOWS THE TRUTH. This is why I want full game film to be your first priority. It shows the truth of what kind of player you are. Not just your best 45 seconds. Not just the games where you scored 25. Not just the tournament where every shot went in. The coach gets to see the entire game. And that's actually what you want. Because your goal isn't to trick a coach into thinking you're a great player. Your goal is to show a coach why they should come watch you play in person. That's a completely different objective.
[¶5] THINK ABOUT WHAT THE COACH IS ASKING. When I receive film from a player I've never seen before, I'm not just asking: "Can she make shots?" I'm asking: "Is this player worth my time to go see?" That's a huge distinction. If I watch your full game and see: Good decision-making; Consistent effort; Defensive ability; Basketball IQ; Communication; Versatility; Toughness; Good movement; Skill; A player who consistently impacts the game — I might think: "I need to see her in person." That's the goal.
[¶6] WHAT IF YOU DON'T HAVE MUCH INTEREST YET? This is where film becomes especially important. If you're already getting recruited by several schools and coaches are regularly coming to your games, great. But what if you're not? That's when you should be sending film. If you're emailing coaches and saying: "I would love for you to evaluate me." You need to give them something to evaluate. And I would much rather send a coach a full game that accurately represents the player than a two-minute highlight tape that only shows the best plays.
[¶7] CONSISTENCY MATTERS. And I don't mean send one full game and never update it. Your film should become a body of work. As your season continues, continue collecting games. Now a coach can watch: Game 1: "Okay, I see her." Game 2: "She's doing it again." Game 3: "Okay. This is who she is." That third game is important. Because now the coach isn't wondering: "Was that one good game?" They're starting to understand: "This is what I can expect from this player."
[¶8] DON'T ONLY SEND YOUR BEST GAME. This is another mistake. If you scored 30 points one night and 8 the next, don't only send the 30-point game. Send games that show the player you actually are. If you're a 10–12 point player who impacts the game in six different ways, I want to see that. If you're a great defender who doesn't always score a ton, I want to see that. If you're a point guard who controls the game without scoring 20 points, I want to see that. If you're a post player who dominates the glass and protects the rim, I want to see that. Don't build your film around making yourself look like a different player. Build it around showing coaches what you actually bring.
[¶9] SO WHEN DO HIGHLIGHTS BECOME USEFUL? I'm not saying: "Never make a highlight tape." I actually think highlight tapes can become very useful. But I want you to understand when. Once you've established a reputation with a coach... Once they've watched your full games... Once they know your name... Once they understand what kind of player you are... Now your highlight reel becomes a great supplement. At that point, you aren't trying to introduce yourself from scratch. You're reinforcing what they already know. You can take clips from multiple games and put them together. Now the coach can quickly see: Your shooting; Your finishing; Your passing; Your defense; Your athleticism; Your best plays. And they already have the context.
[¶10] THINK OF IT THIS WAY. EARLY RECRUITING: FULL GAME FILM = "THIS IS WHO I AM." ESTABLISHED INTEREST: HIGHLIGHT FILM = "HERE ARE SOME OF THE BEST THINGS I DO." That's the difference. A highlight tape should enhance your reputation. It shouldn't have to create your reputation.
[¶11] AND YES, COACHES ARE BUSY. Someone will probably tell you: "Coaches don't have time to watch a full game." They're busy. Absolutely. But don't confuse: "A coach may not watch the entire game immediately" with: "A full game isn't valuable." Those are very different things. A coach might click your full game and watch the first few minutes. They might skip around. They might watch your shifts. They might come back later. They might send it to another coach. They might watch it after you've already gotten on their radar. The important thing is that when they want to evaluate you more deeply... the film is there.
[¶12] YOUR FILM SHOULD MAKE A COACH WANT TO SEE YOU LIVE. This is the real goal. You're not trying to get recruited solely from a video. You're trying to use film to get the coach to take the next step. Film → Evaluation → Live viewing → Relationship → Recruiting. Your film should make a coach think: "I want to see what this kid looks like in person." And once they come to your game? Now you have another opportunity to show them who you are.
[¶13] YOUR FILM STRATEGY. If you're actively trying to get your name out there and don't have the interest you're hoping for: 1. SEND FULL GAME FILM. Not just highlights. 2. SEND IT CONSISTENTLY. Give coaches multiple opportunities to evaluate you. 3. MAKE SURE THE FILM IS EASY TO FIND. Don't make a coach hunt for your game. 4. LABEL IT CLEARLY. Include: Player name; Graduation year; High school; Opponent; Date; Jersey number. 5. KEEP ADDING GAMES. Your recruiting film should grow with your season. 6. THEN ADD HIGHLIGHTS. Once coaches know who you are, use highlights to reinforce your strengths.
[¶14] ONE LAST THING. Don't be afraid of a coach seeing the parts of the game that aren't highlights. That's the point. If you're a great player, you should be able to show a coach more than your best 10 possessions. Show them your decisions. Show them your effort. Show them your defense. Show them your communication. Show them your mistakes and how you respond to them. Show them how you play when you're not scoring. Show them the player. Because ultimately, that's who they're recruiting. FULL GAME FIRST. HIGHLIGHTS SECOND. Don't just show a coach what you can do when everything goes right. Show them why they should want to come see you do it in person.
