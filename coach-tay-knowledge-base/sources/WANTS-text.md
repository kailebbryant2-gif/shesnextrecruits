# WANTS-T · Know What You Want Before They Start Recruiting You (written text)

- **Source:** Written lesson text pasted by the user, 2026-11-11 (Batch 4)
- **Module placement:** Unknown (college research / decision section). Not confirmed.
- Kept verbatim. Paragraph labels added for citation.

[¶1] Before the phone calls. Before the visits. Before the offers. Before the coaches start telling you how perfectly you fit their program... I WANT YOU TO FIGURE OUT WHAT YOU WANT. Not what your parents want. Not what your AAU coach thinks sounds impressive. Not what looks best on Instagram. Not what school has the biggest name. WHAT DO YOU ACTUALLY WANT FROM YOUR COLLEGE EXPERIENCE? Because once recruiting starts getting serious, coaches are going to show you the very best version of their program. That's part of recruiting. They are recruiting you. They're going to show you the facilities. The uniforms. The locker room. The campus. The arena. The gear. The apartments. The weight room. The highlights. The championships. They're going to tell you about their vision for you. And some of it may be absolutely incredible. But if you don't know what matters to you before all of that happens, it can become very easy to fall in love with the fairy tale instead of evaluating whether the school actually gives you the experience you want. DECIDE WHAT YOU WANT BEFORE SOMEONE STARTS SELLING YOU WHAT THEY HAVE.
[W1] START THIS LIST EARLY. I would start doing this around freshman year. Not because a freshman should know exactly where she wants to go to college. She probably doesn't. And that's okay. This isn't a final decision. THIS IS A LIVING LIST. Start it freshman year. Add to it sophomore year. Change things junior year. Cross things off. Move things up. Move things down. You may think something is incredibly important at 14 and realize at 17 that you don't care about it at all. You may visit a campus and discover something you never knew mattered to you. That's exactly why we're starting early. You're learning yourself while you're learning recruiting.
[W2] START WITH BASKETBALL. Forget school names for a minute. What do you actually want your college basketball experience to look like? Do you want to: Play early? Compete for championships? Be part of a rebuilding program? Play at the highest possible level even if your role may be smaller? Go somewhere you have a realistic opportunity to start? Play fast? Play in a certain offensive system? Be developed at a certain position? Have a coach who is extremely demanding? Have a coach who is more relationship-oriented? Be part of a big roster? Be somewhere with a smaller, tight-knit team? Have an experienced coaching staff? Have access to strong strength and performance resources? Play in front of bigger crowds? Compete close enough that your family can regularly watch you? There isn't one correct answer. But you need to start figuring out your answer.
[W3] THEN THINK ABOUT COLLEGE OUTSIDE OF BASKETBALL. This matters just as much. Remember: You're not only choosing a basketball program. YOU ARE CHOOSING A PLACE TO LIVE. Potentially for four years. What do you want? Big school or small school? City or college town? Warm weather or four seasons? Close to home or far away? Two hours away? Six hours away? A flight away? Do you care if your parents can come to games? Do you want a huge campus? Would you rather know everyone? Do you care about football and the traditional big-college experience? Do you want Greek life? Do you care about campus activities? What kind of dorms do you want? What kind of community do you want? What does your ideal Saturday look like when you DON'T have basketball? These things matter.
[W4] THINK ABOUT YOUR EDUCATION. Basketball is important. But you're also going to college. What do you think you might want to study? Does the school have that major? How important are academics to you? Do you want smaller classes? Do you want a particular academic program? Do you want internship opportunities? Graduate school opportunities? What kind of career are you considering? You don't need to have your entire life figured out at 15. But start asking the questions.
[W5] THINK ABOUT MONEY. This needs to be part of the list too. What can your family realistically afford? How important is scholarship money? Would you consider a school that isn't offering a full athletic scholarship if the total financial package makes sense? What would the school actually cost after: Athletic aid. Academic aid. Grants. Other scholarships. Family contribution. Don't let the word "scholarship" distract you from the actual number. Eventually, the question is: WHAT WILL THIS SCHOOL ACTUALLY COST MY FAMILY? That's what matters.
[W6] NOW START SEPARATING WANTS FROM NON-NEGOTIABLES. This is where the list becomes really valuable. Some things are: NON-NEGOTIABLE. Some things are: REALLY IMPORTANT. And some things are: WOULD BE NICE. Those are not the same. Maybe: Must have my academic program = NON-NEGOTIABLE. Within five hours of home = REALLY IMPORTANT. Cool uniforms = WOULD BE NICE. That's very different from putting everything on one giant wish list. Because you're probably not going to find a school that checks every single box. The goal is to know which boxes matter most.
[W7] DON'T BUILD THE LIST AROUND DIVISION. Notice what I haven't told you to put at the top: DIVISION I. We've already talked about having an honest conversation about level. Your list should be about the experience. If you tell me: "I want to play significant minutes. I want a close relationship with my coaches. I want my parents to be able to attend games. I want to study nursing. I want a smaller campus. I want to compete for championships." Great. Now we can evaluate schools based on whether they can actually give you those things. Not simply: "It's D1." A division label doesn't tell you what your experience will be.
[W8] BE CAREFUL WITH THE FAIRY TALE. Recruiting can feel incredible. A coach calls you. They tell you how much they love your game. They tell you: "We think you could be really special here." Then you visit. Your name is on a locker. There's a jersey waiting for you. The staff knows your family. The players are welcoming. You take pictures. You see the arena. You imagine yourself running out of the tunnel. And suddenly you're thinking: THIS IS IT. Maybe it is. But this is exactly why you need your list. Because after the excitement settles, you need to be able to ask: Does this actually match what I said I wanted?
[W9] DON'T LET SOMEONE CREATE YOUR PRIORITIES FOR YOU. Imagine you never cared about having a huge arena. Then you visit one and suddenly the coach is showing you the arena and talking about the atmosphere. Now it feels important. You never cared about having five different uniform combinations. Then you see them laid out on the visit. Now that feels important. You never wanted to be eight hours from home. But the facilities are beautiful. Maybe distance suddenly doesn't feel like a big deal. And maybe that's genuine. YOU ARE ALLOWED TO CHANGE YOUR MIND. But ask yourself: "Did I actually change my mind, or did I just get impressed?" Those are different.
[W10] YOUR LIST PROTECTS YOU FROM THE MOMENT. Recruiting is emotional. That's why I want this written down. Not just: "I kind of know what I want." Actually write it. Because your freshman-year self might tell you something your senior-year recruiting emotions need to hear. Maybe you've written for three years: "Being close enough for my family to attend games is extremely important to me." Then a beautiful school 12 hours away starts recruiting you. Now you have something to think about. Maybe the answer becomes: "I've changed. I'm ready to go far away." Great. Change the list. But make the decision intentionally.
[W11] KEEP EDITING IT. Every year, come back to this. Ask: Is this still important to me? Did anything change? Did I learn something about myself? Did a college visit change what I thought I wanted? Did talking to college players teach me something new? Did my basketball goals change? Did my academic interests change? Did my relationship with basketball change? Your list should grow with you.
[W12] WHEN RECRUITING GETS SERIOUS, PULL THE LIST BACK OUT. This is when all of this work becomes useful. Now Coach A calls. Coach B offers. Coach C wants you on campus. Instead of getting swept up in whoever called most recently... GO BACK TO YOUR LIST. What did you say you wanted? Now compare. Maybe School A has the biggest name... but School B checks almost every box you've written down for three years. That's information.
[W13] YOUR LIST CAN ALSO HELP YOU ASK BETTER QUESTIONS. If playing early is important... You need to ask about the depth chart. If your position matters... Ask exactly where the coach sees you playing. If development matters... Ask how they develop players at your position. If academics matter... Ask how basketball works around your major. If family attendance matters... Look at distance and the travel schedule. If coaching relationships matter... Pay close attention to how the staff communicates with their current players. Your list tells you what you need to investigate.
[W14] START YOUR LIST. I want every athlete completing this course to begin building a list. Start with: BASKETBALL — What do I want my basketball experience to feel like? COACHING — What kind of coach and relationship do I want? ROLE — How important are playing time, starting, and my position? SCHOOL — What kind of campus and college experience do I want? LOCATION — How far from home am I comfortable going? ACADEMICS — What do I need academically? FINANCES — What does my family need financially? LIFESTYLE — What matters to me outside basketball? Then take everything you've written and mark it: MUST HAVE; REALLY WANT; WOULD BE NICE. And keep it. Come back to it every year.
[W15] YOU DON'T NEED TO KNOW THE SCHOOL YET. YOU NEED TO START KNOWING YOURSELF. That's the entire point of this exercise. Before you're choosing between logos... Before you're comparing offers... Before someone is telling you how amazing their program is... Know what matters to you. Because every college coach is going to have a story to tell you about why their school could be the perfect place. Your job isn't to decide whether the story sounds good. YOUR JOB IS TO DECIDE WHETHER IT FITS YOUR STORY. Start the list early. Change it as you grow. Be honest with yourself. And when recruiting gets exciting... DON'T FORGET WHAT YOU WANTED BEFORE EVERYONE ELSE STARTED TELLING YOU WHAT YOU SHOULD WANT.
