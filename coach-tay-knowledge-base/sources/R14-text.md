[¶1] Choosing the right AAU team can have a huge impact on your daughter's development and recruiting opportunities. Ideally, you want to find the right fit as early as possible — especially around 7th or 8th grade — and then give that relationship time to develop.
[¶2] Constantly changing teams can make it difficult for college coaches to follow your daughter. Every time she changes teams, coaches may have to figure out: Where is she playing now? Who is coaching her? What team is she with? Where can I see her play? You don't want your daughter to become difficult to follow.
[¶3] And there is another reason staying with a program can matter: It demonstrates loyalty and commitment. College coaches aren't only recruiting basketball players. They're recruiting people they hope will become part of their program for four years. If a player has shown that she commits to something, builds relationships, and doesn't constantly leave when something better comes along, that can be meaningful.
[¶4] That doesn't mean you should never change teams. Sometimes a team isn't the right fit. Sometimes the development isn't there. Sometimes the opportunities aren't what you were promised. But changing teams every season simply because another program looks more impressive can create more problems than it solves.
[¶5] So what actually makes a great AAU team? Here's the question I want you to ask: Does this coach care more about my daughter's future than winning the next game? And yes — I really mean more than winning games.
[¶6] Winning matters. A successful team can earn better brackets, better courts, and better opportunities to be seen. But winning should never come at the expense of your daughter's development or recruiting opportunity.
[¶7] A great AAU coach understands that sometimes the best thing for your daughter is putting her in a position where she can actually play. She needs meaningful minutes. She needs opportunities to make mistakes and learn. She needs to be in situations where college coaches can evaluate what she can actually do. A team that wins every game but keeps your daughter sitting on the bench may not be the best recruiting situation for her.
[¶8] Your AAU coach should also understand their role in recruiting. A good AAU coach doesn't necessarily need to have hundreds of college connections. Connections are helpful, but they're not everything. What matters is whether the coach is willing to communicate. They should be willing to: Talk with college coaches when appropriate; Communicate your daughter's strengths honestly; Understand your daughter's recruiting goals; Help coaches find her at events; Reach out to programs when appropriate; Help your daughter get the right exposure; Understand when to advocate for her; Understand when to step back and let her advocate for herself.
[¶9] And they need to understand boundaries. Sometimes a coach needs to make an introduction. Sometimes they need to advocate. Sometimes they need to be persistent. And sometimes they need to get out of the way and let the athlete and college coach build their own relationship. A great AAU coach knows the difference.
[¶10] And remember: recruiting does not fall entirely on the AAU coach. Your AAU coach is only one piece of the recruiting team. Your high school coach can play an important role too. Your daughter should have a role. You have a role. And depending on the situation, trainers, mentors, and other trusted people can help. The goal is not to find one coach who will magically get your daughter recruited. The goal is to build a small, informed team of people who understand her goals and are willing to help her get there.
[¶11] Before choosing an AAU team, ask: Will my daughter actually play? Does this coach care about developing her? Will they help her get evaluated — not just enter tournaments? Will they communicate with college coaches when appropriate? Do they understand the recruiting process? Will they support her goals even when those goals aren't identical to the team's goals? Does this feel like a program we can build a relationship with for several years?
[¶12] Don't choose an AAU team simply because they have the biggest name, the best uniforms, or the most wins. Choose the program that gives your daughter the best environment to develop, compete, be evaluated, and build a recruiting path. The right AAU team isn't just the team that wins. It's the team that helps your daughter win at the next level.
