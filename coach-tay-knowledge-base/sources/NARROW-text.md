# NARROW-T · Narrowing the List: Choose the Life, Not Just the School (written text)

- **Source:** Written lesson text pasted by the user, 2026-11-11 (Batch 4)
- **Module placement:** Unknown (decision section; follows WANTS-T). Not confirmed.
- Kept verbatim. Section labels added for citation (numbered sections kept as N1–N23).

---

[¶1] You made your list. You know what you think you want. Now schools are starting to become real possibilities. This is where I want families to SLOW DOWN. Because choosing a college basketball program isn't really choosing where your daughter will play basketball. SHE IS CHOOSING WHAT HER LIFE IS GOING TO LOOK LIKE. Where she wakes up. Who she's around. What she eats. How she spends Christmas. How much she sees her family. What her body goes through. How she spends her summers. Who coaches her every day. What she studies. How much your family pays. What happens when basketball isn't going well. And THAT is what we need to investigate.
[¶2] DON'T CHOOSE THE RECRUITING VERSION OF THE SCHOOL. Recruiting is the best version. The visit is planned. Everyone knows you're coming. The coaches are excited. The players know you're a recruit. You see the facilities. You get pictures. You hear about everything the program can offer. You might hear: "We see you making an immediate impact." "We think you're exactly what we need." "We're going to develop you." "We're building something special." Maybe every word is genuine. But you aren't choosing the recruiting experience. YOU'RE CHOOSING THE TUESDAY IN FEBRUARY EXPERIENCE. When you're tired. You have an exam tomorrow. Your body hurts. It's freezing outside. You've barely played the last three games. You miss home. Coach is frustrated. Practice was hard. And nobody is recruiting you anymore. WOULD YOU STILL WANT TO BE THERE? That's what we're trying to figure out.
[N1] 1. HOW DO THEY ACTUALLY PLAY? Don't choose a program because you like the logo and then discover their basketball makes you miserable. Watch full games. What style do they play? Fast or slow? Transition or half court? Motion? Ball screens? Post-heavy? Guard-heavy? Do they shoot a lot of threes? Do guards have freedom? Does the coach control every possession? Do they press? Switch defensively? Play zone? What type of players actually get minutes? Most importantly: WHERE DOES YOUR DAUGHTER FIT IN THAT SYSTEM? A coach can love her. That doesn't automatically mean the system fits her.
[N2] 2. WHAT DOES "WE SEE YOU PLAYING..." ACTUALLY MEAN? Get specific. What position are you recruiting her to play? Who is currently playing that position? What year are they? Who else are you recruiting at that position? Are you recruiting her as a point guard... or does the roster already have four? Are you telling her she'll play the 4... when she spent years developing herself as a wing? Ask. Don't assume.
[N3] 3. WHAT IS THE COACH ACTUALLY LIKE? Recruiting Coach and Everyday Coach may feel very different. During recruiting, a coach wants you. Once you're there, they're coaching you. Ask current players: What is Coach like after you make a mistake? What is practice like after a loss? How does Coach handle conflict? How does Coach communicate when you're not playing well? Can you go into their office when you're struggling? How are players who aren't getting minutes treated? How does the staff handle disagreements? Your daughter needs to think about what kind of coaching she responds to. Some athletes thrive under extremely demanding coaches. Others don't. Neither automatically makes the coach or athlete bad. THE QUESTION IS FIT.
[N4] 4. WHAT HAPPENS WHEN YOU AREN'T PLAYING? This is HUGE. Everyone imagines college basketball assuming they're playing. Now imagine she isn't. Would she still like the school? Would she still like the people? Would she still value the education? Would she still enjoy the campus? Would she still want to live there? Because injury happens. Depth charts change. Transfers come in. Freshmen come in. Coaches change their minds. Players develop. Roles change. IF BASKETBALL ISN'T GOING PERFECTLY, IS THERE STILL ENOUGH THERE TO MAKE HER HAPPY?
[N5] 5. HOW FAR AWAY IS HOME — REALLY? Saying: "It's only six hours." sounds different than living six hours away. Can your family realistically attend games? How often? Is it drivable? Do you need flights? What do those flights cost? Does the nearest airport require another hour drive? Does she have a car? How will she get home? What happens if she gets sick? What if she's homesick? What if there's a family emergency? Some athletes LOVE being far away. Others realize very quickly that they don't. Neither is wrong. Know yourself.
[N6] 6. ASK ABOUT HOLIDAYS. Parents and athletes often don't think about this until the first season. College basketball does not necessarily stop because it's Christmas. Ask: When do players normally leave for Christmas? When do they have to return? Do you play over Thanksgiving? Do you play around Christmas? Do players typically get to go home? How long is winter break for the basketball team — not the regular student body? The university calendar and the basketball calendar are not the same thing. If family traditions and holidays are incredibly important to your daughter, she needs to understand what she may miss.
[N7] 7. WHAT DOES SUMMER ACTUALLY LOOK LIKE? Do not assume your daughter gets May through August at home. Ask: When are players expected back? What summer training is expected or required under applicable rules? Are there summer classes? How much of the summer are players typically on campus? What do current players actually do? If she wants to go home, travel, work, or complete an internship, how realistic is that? A coach saying: "You'll have some time off." isn't enough. Ask what last summer actually looked like.
[N8] 8. WHAT DOES A NORMAL DAY LOOK LIKE? This is one of my favorite questions: "WALK ME THROUGH A NORMAL TUESDAY DURING THE SEASON." What time do players wake up? Weights? Breakfast? Classes? Treatment? Film? Practice? Study hall? Dinner? Homework? When do they actually get back to their room? Now ask about offseason. College basketball is a major time commitment. Your daughter needs to understand what she's agreeing to.
[N9] 9. DOES THE SCHOOL ACTUALLY HAVE HER MAJOR? Not: "They have good academics." Does it have what SHE wants? And if she doesn't know her major yet, does it have several programs she could realistically see herself pursuing? Then go deeper. Some majors have: Labs. Clinical hours. Student teaching. Internships. Required placements. Classes offered only at certain times. Ask: "CAN BASKETBALL PLAYERS REALISTICALLY MAJOR IN THIS?" Don't only ask the coach. Ask academic support. Ask players. Look at course requirements.
[N10] 10. HOW MUCH DOES THE COACH ACTUALLY VALUE EDUCATION? Every coach is going to tell you academics matter. Look at behavior. What happens when a required class conflicts with something basketball-related? How are labs handled? What about tutoring? Study hall? Academic advising? Missed classes from travel? Internships? Clinical placements? What majors do current players have? Have players successfully completed demanding majors? The answers tell you much more than: "We care about academics."
[N11] 11. CAN SHE HAVE A LIFE OUTSIDE BASKETBALL? This matters. Can players join organizations? Church groups? Campus ministry? Student government? Clubs? Sororities? Volunteer organizations? Internships? Part-time jobs, if permitted and realistic? Can she attend campus events? Or is basketball essentially her entire college experience? Maybe she's completely okay with basketball consuming most of her time. Maybe she's not. KNOW BEFORE YOU GO.
[N12] 12. WHAT IS THE TEAM CULTURE ACTUALLY LIKE? Don't only ask: "Do you guys like each other?" Go deeper. Do players spend time together outside basketball? Is there constant drama? Are upperclassmen welcoming? Are freshmen treated well? Is there competition without hostility? How do teammates respond when someone else earns minutes? How are transfers treated? What happens when someone is struggling? Does the team feel like people your daughter can live with every day? Because she's going to spend A LOT of time with them.
[N13] 13. DOES SHE SEE HERSELF IN THE PEOPLE AROUND HER? Campus culture matters. Look beyond the basketball court. How diverse is the student body? How diverse is the team? How diverse is the coaching staff and athletic department? Will your daughter feel comfortable in that environment? Are there communities, organizations, cultural groups, churches, or other spaces important to her? There isn't one right answer for every athlete. The question is: CAN SHE SEE HERSELF BUILDING A LIFE THERE?
[N14] 14. WHAT WILL SHE EAT? It sounds small. Until you're eating there every day. Ask about: Meal plans. Dining hall hours. Food quality. Options around practice schedules. Late-night food. Food during school breaks. Travel meals. Dietary restrictions. Access to sports nutrition support. Whether athletes have kitchens. What happens when the dining hall is closed. Food affects everyday quality of life AND athletic performance. Don't laugh this one off.
[N15] 15. WHERE WILL SHE LIVE? Will freshmen have roommates? Can athletes choose them? Are teammates normally paired together? What are the dorms actually like? Where do upperclassmen live? Can athletes move off campus? Is housing guaranteed? What does housing cost? Is summer housing included or separate? If she's required to stay during summer training, who pays for housing and food? Again: Small details become big details when you live them every day.
[N16] 16. WHAT WILL FOUR YEARS ACTUALLY COST? Parents, don't only look at Year One. DO THE FOUR-YEAR MATH. Tuition. Housing. Meal plan. Fees. Books. Transportation. Flights. Gas. Parking. Summer school. Summer housing. Additional meals. Personal expenses. Any remaining amount after athletic, academic, institutional, or other aid. Ask what aid is guaranteed, what can change, and under what circumstances. A school that's affordable freshman year needs to still make sense later. DON'T FALL IN LOVE BEFORE YOU UNDERSTAND THE NUMBERS.
[N17] 17. NIL IS PART OF THE CONVERSATION NOW — BUT DON'T BUILD THE WHOLE DECISION AROUND IT. NIL has changed college sports. Depending on the program and level, athletes may encounter different NIL opportunities, school resources, collectives or third-party arrangements, and—at some Division I schools—other forms of athlete compensation under current rules. So ask questions. What opportunities have players actually had? Who helps athletes understand contracts and rules? What is guaranteed versus merely possible? What happens if her role changes? What happens if the coach leaves? Is anything being discussed actually in writing? DON'T CONFUSE A POSSIBILITY WITH A PROMISE. Rules and school practices can change, so families should verify the current details directly with the school and appropriate compliance staff when making a decision.
[N18] 18. WHAT SUPPORT EXISTS WHEN HER BODY ISN'T OKAY? She's an athlete. Injuries happen. Ask: Who is the athletic trainer? How accessible are they? What medical resources are available? Physical therapy? Strength and conditioning? Sports medicine? Nutrition? Mental health resources? What happens if she needs surgery? What happens academically while injured? What happens to her role while recovering? And one uncomfortable but important question: HOW ARE INJURED PLAYERS TREATED WHEN THEY CAN'T HELP THE TEAM WIN? Talk to players who have actually been injured if possible.
[N19] 19. WHAT HAPPENS IF THE COACH LEAVES? This one gets overlooked. You may absolutely love the coach recruiting your daughter. But coaches change jobs. Staffs get fired. Assistants leave. Programs change. So ask: "IF THIS COACH WASN'T HERE TOMORROW, WOULD I STILL WANT THIS SCHOOL?" That question matters. You should absolutely consider the coaching staff. But don't make the entire decision dependent on one person staying for four years.
[N20] 20. TALK TO PLAYERS WITHOUT THE SALES PITCH. Current players can tell you things a brochure can't. And when appropriate, former players can be extremely helpful too. Ask real questions: What surprised you after you got here? What's the hardest part about playing here? What do you wish you knew before committing? How does the coach act when things aren't going well? How does the staff treat players who don't play? Do you feel like you can be honest with the coaches? What does summer actually look like? How much time do you really get at home? If you had to choose again, what would you want to know first? Listen.
[N21] 21. PAY ATTENTION TO ROSTER MOVEMENT. Look at the roster. Who stayed? Who left? How many players transferred in? How many transferred out? How many freshmen remained? Did several players from the same class leave? One transfer doesn't tell you a whole story. Players transfer for countless reasons. But patterns can create questions worth asking. Don't automatically assume something terrible happened. INVESTIGATE.
[N22] 22. LOOK AT THE DEPTH CHART — NOT JUST THE OFFER. An offer feels amazing. But ask: Who is ahead of her? How many years of eligibility do they have? Who else is being recruited? How many transfers does this program typically bring in? Does the coach usually play freshmen? How many players actually get meaningful minutes? Is your daughter being recruited because they genuinely see a need for her skill set? Again: GETTING THE OFFER AND GETTING ON THE FLOOR ARE DIFFERENT THINGS.
[N23] 23. ASK THE UNCOMFORTABLE QUESTIONS BEFORE YOU COMMIT. This is not the time to worry about being annoying. Be respectful. But ask. What happens if I don't play? What happens if I get injured? What happens if my coach leaves? What happens if I change majors? What happens if my financial situation changes? What happens if another player transfers in at my position? What happens if I struggle academically? What happens if I want an internship? What happens if I want to go home during summer? What happens if basketball stops being the best part of my experience? These questions aren't negative. THEY'RE RESPONSIBLE.
[T1] WHY PLAYERS TRANSFER. There is no single reason athletes transfer. Sometimes it's basketball. Sometimes it's playing time. Sometimes it's coaching. Sometimes it's academics. Sometimes it's money. Sometimes it's distance from home. Sometimes it's team culture. Sometimes it's campus fit. Sometimes it's a coaching change. Sometimes it's an opportunity somewhere else. Sometimes the athlete simply changes. And sometimes transferring IS the right decision. The goal of this course is not to make transferring seem like failure. THE GOAL IS TO REDUCE THE NUMBER OF SURPRISES. We want your daughter making the most informed decision she possibly can with the information available at the time.
[T2] NOW GO BACK TO YOUR ORIGINAL LIST. Remember the list you created before recruiting got serious? Pull it out. Now start comparing reality to what you said mattered. SCHOOL A — What does it actually give me? SCHOOL B — What does it actually give me? SCHOOL C — What does it actually give me? Not: Who has the coolest gear? Who posted me? Who has the biggest name? Who made the visit feel the most exciting? Ask: WHICH SCHOOL MOST CLOSELY MATCHES THE LIFE I SAID I WANTED?
[T3] AND PARENTS — LET HER ANSWER. You should absolutely help. You should look at finances. Ask questions. Notice red flags. Help her think long-term. Protect her from making a decision based entirely on emotion. But remember: SHE HAS TO LIVE THERE. She has to wake up there. Go to class there. Practice there. Get coached there. Sit on the bench there. Win there. Lose there. Eat there. Sleep there. Miss home there. Make friends there. Grow up there. So make sure you're helping her figure out what SHE wants, not choosing the school you would have chosen at 18.
[T4] DON'T JUST CHOOSE THE BEST RECRUITING EXPERIENCE. CHOOSE THE BEST REAL LIFE. The recruiting photos eventually stop. The visit ends. The gear becomes normal. The offer announcement gets buried on social media. And then... YOU HAVE TO LIVE THERE. So ask the questions now. Investigate the uncomfortable things now. Talk about money now. Talk about playing time now. Talk about home now. Talk about academics now. Talk about summers now. Talk about coaching now. Talk about life outside basketball now. You cannot guarantee that your daughter will never want to transfer. People change. Programs change. Circumstances change. But you CAN help her walk into college with her eyes open. DON'T CHOOSE THE FAIRY TALE. CHOOSE THE PLACE THAT STILL MAKES SENSE WHEN THE FAIRY TALE WEARS OFF.
