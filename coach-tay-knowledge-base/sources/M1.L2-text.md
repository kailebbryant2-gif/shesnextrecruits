[¶1] The biggest mistake parents make in the recruiting process is waiting too long to start.

[¶2] A lot of families think recruiting begins when their daughter is a sophomore or junior in high school. In reality, those are often the years when the biggest recruiting decisions are already being made. The preparation for those moments needs to happen long before then.

[¶3] Only a small percentage of high school basketball players will ultimately play NCAA basketball.

[¶4] According to the NCAA's most recent participation data, approximately 4.7% of high school girls basketball players go on to compete at an NCAA school ... and only about 1.4% go on to play Division I basketball.

[¶5] That doesn't mean your daughter can't be one of them.

[¶6] It means you shouldn't leave something this competitive to chance.

[¶7] The goal of this course isn't to tell you that your daughter needs to be in full-on recruiting mode in 7th or 8th grade. It's also not about putting unnecessary pressure on a young athlete.

[¶8] It's about being informed early enough to make good decisions.

[¶9] There are things you can do years before an offer ever comes that can put your daughter in a much better position when that opportunity does come.

[¶10] You can help her learn how to advocate for herself.

[¶11] You can help her understand what coaches actually evaluate.

[¶12] You can help her develop the habits, academics, skills, strength, confidence, and character that will matter later.

[¶13] You can understand what needs to happen this year while also looking ahead to what will be expected next year, and the year after that.

[¶14] That's the difference between being reactive and being prepared.

[¶15] Don't wait until someone tells you, "You should have started this sooner."

[¶16] Start learning now. Start preparing now. And give your daughter the best chance to control what she can control before the recruiting window gets smaller.
