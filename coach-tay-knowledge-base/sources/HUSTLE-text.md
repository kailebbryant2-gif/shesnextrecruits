# HUSTLE-T · Hustle Is a Skill and It Can Be Developed (written text)

[¶1] We just talked about all of the things you can do outside of the box score to get recruited. Now I want to talk about something important: How do you actually become a player who hustles? Because I don't want you to think hustle is something you're either born with or you're not. Some players are naturally more aggressive. Some naturally chase every loose ball. Some naturally sprint everywhere. But hustle can also be developed. And I think the best way to develop it is to stop thinking about hustle as one giant thing. Instead... Think about it as a series of small decisions.
[¶2] HUSTLE HAPPENS ONE DECISION AT A TIME. A player doesn't suddenly decide: "I'm going to hustle really hard today." And then magically become a hustle player. It happens possession by possession. You shoot the ball. Decision: Sprint back. You get beat. Decision: Recover. Your teammate falls. Decision: Help her up. The ball is loose. Decision: Go get it. Your team turns the ball over. Decision: Sprint back. You see a player cutting. Decision: Talk. Your teammate gets beat. Decision: Help. The shot goes up. Decision: Find someone and box out. You're tired. Decision: Keep going. That's hustle. It's a bunch of small decisions made correctly, over and over again.
[¶3] START MAKING THE DECISION BEFORE THE PLAY HAPPENS. This is where you can really train yourself. Don't wait for the situation to happen and then decide whether you're going to hustle. Decide beforehand. Before the game: "Every time I shoot, I am sprinting back." "Every shot that goes up, I am finding someone to box out." "Every time someone gets beat, I'm going to communicate." "Every loose ball is mine until someone else gets it." "Every time my teammate falls, I'm helping her up." "Every transition opportunity, I'm sprinting the floor." Now you've given yourself rules. You're not waiting to see how you feel. You're not waiting for your coach to yell. You've already made the decision.
[¶4] YOU HAVE TO PRACTICE HUSTLE. Here's something coaches know: You don't suddenly become a different player when the game starts. Your habits in practice show up in games. That's why coaches who are serious about building hustle don't just talk about it. They build it into practice. They reward it. They stop practice to point it out. They create competitive situations where hustle matters. They track effort plays. They use drills where players have to compete for loose balls, rebound, sprint, defend, and make plays under pressure. So if you want to become a better hustle player: Hustle in practice. Not just when your coach is watching. Not just when you're trying to make the team. Not just the week before a tournament. Every practice.
[¶5] CREATE YOUR OWN HUSTLE CHECKLIST. I want you to actually start keeping track of this. Before practice, pick 2–3 hustle behaviors you're going to focus on. For example: TODAY'S HUSTLE GOALS ☐ Sprint back every time ☐ Talk on defense ☐ Box out every shot ☐ Get on the floor for 50/50 balls ☐ Help teammates up ☐ Sprint into every offensive possession ☐ Pick up full court ☐ Be first to the huddle ☐ Stay engaged on the bench ☐ Encourage teammates. Don't pick ten things at once. Pick a few. Then make yourself do them over and over. Because repetition turns a decision into a habit. And eventually, you won't have to think: "I need to sprint back." You'll just sprint back.
[¶6] MAKE HUSTLE VISIBLE. Here's another thing I want athletes and parents to understand. A lot of hustle plays don't show up in traditional statistics. So you have to start recognizing them yourself. Keep track of things like: Deflections; Loose balls; Offensive rebounds; Extra possessions; Charges; Sprint-backs; Full-court pressure; Help rotations; Great closeouts; Saved balls; Communication; Teammate assists that came from your effort; Getting teammates organized; Winning 50/50 situations. Some coaches actually track "effort stats" because what gets measured and recognized tends to become more important to players. You don't have to obsess over the number. The point is to start asking: "How did I impact the game today when I didn't have the ball?"
[¶7] DON'T CONFUSE HUSTLE WITH RUNNING AROUND CRAZY. This is important. Hustle doesn't mean: "Run everywhere as fast as possible." Hustle still requires basketball IQ. You don't need to dive on every ball that is rolling three feet away. You don't need to sprint out of position just to look energetic. You don't need to foul someone because you wanted to look aggressive. Smart hustle is better than reckless hustle. The goal is to make the correct decision quickly and with effort. That's why hustle and basketball IQ actually work together. The better you understand the game, the more opportunities you recognize to make hustle plays.
[¶8] USE SMALL-SIDED GAMES TO DEVELOP IT. One of the best ways to develop hustle is to put yourself in situations where effort actually changes the outcome. That's one reason small-sided games can be so useful. Instead of only doing a drill where you already know exactly what is going to happen, play 1-on-1, 2-on-2, 3-on-3, or other competitive situations where the outcome is live and unpredictable. Now you have to: React; Compete; Recover; Rebound; Defend; Communicate; Make decisions; Chase loose balls; Transition; Respond after mistakes. And you have to do it while tired and under pressure. That's much closer to what happens in a real game.
[¶9] REWARD YOURSELF FOR THE RIGHT THINGS. Here's another mental shift I want you to make. Stop only celebrating: "I scored 20." Start celebrating: "I never stopped running." "I got 7 deflections." "I sprinted back every possession." "I guarded their best player." "I helped my teammates." "I won the 50/50 balls." "I stayed engaged when I wasn't playing." Because those are the habits that eventually help produce the box-score numbers.
[¶10] HUSTLE CAN BECOME YOUR REPUTATION. Imagine a coach watches you for five minutes. You haven't scored. You haven't gotten a rebound. You haven't gotten a steal. But they see: You sprint back. You communicate. You pressure the ball. You help. You get a deflection. You dive for a loose ball. You get your teammate up. You sprint the floor. You make the extra effort. The coach starts thinking: "This kid competes." Then they watch you again. Same thing. And again. Same thing. Now you have something extremely valuable: A reputation. Not just as a scorer. Not just as a shooter. Not just as an athlete. As someone who competes.
[¶11] THE GOAL IS TO MAKE HUSTLE AUTOMATIC. This is where I want you to get. At first, hustle takes conscious thought. You have to remind yourself: "Sprint back." "Box out." "Talk." "Go get it." But if you consistently make those decisions, eventually your brain starts making them automatically. That's when hustle becomes part of who you are as a player. You don't have to think about whether you're going to sprint back. You just do. You don't have to think about whether you're going to help your teammate up. You just do. You don't have to think about whether you're going to chase the loose ball. You just do. That's when you've developed the skill.
[¶12] YOUR CHALLENGE. For your next 10 practices, don't make your goal to score more. Don't make your goal to look impressive. Pick three hustle behaviors. For example: 1. Sprint back every possession. 2. Talk on every defensive possession. 3. Go after every 50/50 ball. Then track yourself. At the end of every practice, ask: Did I do it? Not: "Did I play well?" Not: "Did I make my shots?" Not: "Did I score?" Ask: "Did I make the decisions I said I was going to make?" That's how you build accountability. That's how you build habits. That's how you build hustle. And that's how you turn something that seems like effort into an actual skill.
[¶13] REMEMBER THIS. Hustle isn't one big decision. It's hundreds of tiny decisions. Play after play. Possession after possession. Practice after practice. And if you consistently make the decision to do the things that other players are unwilling to do... Eventually, coaches won't describe you as a player who tries to hustle. They'll describe you as: "A HUSTLE PLAYER." And that is a reputation worth having.
