[¶1] Now let's look at the chart you just saw. These are rough estimates of the typical size ranges you may see at each level of college basketball. And the first thing I want you to notice is: HOW MUCH THEY OVERLAP.
[¶2] There isn't a magic height where Division I starts. There isn't a height where Division II starts. There isn't a height where Division III, NAIA or JUCO starts. You will find all different heights at all different levels. You will find smaller guards playing at the highest levels. You will find bigger guards playing at lower levels. You will find undersized posts who have figured out how to be incredibly productive. You will find tall players who still aren't recruited.
[¶3] HEIGHT IS NOT A DESTINATION. It's simply one part of the equation. And this is exactly why I wanted you to see this chart. Because if you look at your daughter's height and immediately decide: "She's too short." or "She's tall enough, so she'll be fine." You're missing the bigger picture.
[¶4] THERE IS A LOT OF ROOM FOR POSSIBILITIES. If your daughter understands how to train... If she understands what coaches are actually looking for... If she develops her skill set... If she learns how to become versatile... If she figures out what her superpower is... If she learns how to market herself... There are a lot more possibilities than you may think. This is why I don't want you deciding your daughter's future based on a measuring tape. Instead, I want you asking: "What can she become?" and "How do we make her valuable?"
[¶5] GET ON THE RECRUITING BOARD. Remember everything we talked about earlier in this course. College coaches have recruiting boards. They are looking for players who can fill specific needs. Your daughter doesn't have to be the tallest. She doesn't have to be the fastest. She doesn't have to be the most highly ranked player in her class. But she does need to give a coach a reason to put her name on that board. Maybe it's her size. Maybe it's her point guard ability. Maybe it's her shooting. Maybe it's her versatility. Maybe it's her defense. Maybe it's her strength. Maybe it's her athleticism. Maybe it's her basketball IQ. Maybe it's her intangibles. Maybe it's a combination of several things. Figure out what makes her valuable — and then learn how to put that value in front of the right coaches.
[¶6] THEN COMES THE NEXT QUESTION: Once you get on the recruiting board... HOW DO YOU GET ON THE FLOOR? Because getting recruited and getting playing time are two different things. A coach may recruit your daughter because they see something they believe they can develop. But once she's on that college roster, she still has to earn minutes. That's why everything we've talked about matters. Skill. Versatility. Strength. Basketball IQ. Defense. Communication. Intangibles. All of it contributes to becoming a player a coach can trust.
[¶7] THE BIG TAKEAWAY. Don't look at this chart and ask: "What level is her height?" Ask: "What can she do with the height she has?" There are players of all different sizes playing college basketball at every level. The difference is often who figured out how to make themselves valuable. Who figured out how to train. Who figured out how to develop. Who figured out how to market themselves. Who figured out how to get on the recruiting board. And then, once they got there... Who figured out how to get on the floor. That's what this entire section is about. Don't focus only on where your daughter fits today. Focus on becoming the most marketable player she can possibly be.
