# RECINFO-T · Keep Your Recruiting Information Ready to Send (written text)

[¶1] There are certain things college coaches are going to ask you for over and over again throughout recruiting. Your film. Your NCAA Eligibility Center number. Your email address. Your phone number. Your transcript. Your schedule. Basic information about you. None of these things are difficult to provide. But you know what CAN make them difficult? HAVING TO FIND EVERYTHING EVERY SINGLE TIME SOMEONE ASKS. A coach asks for your transcript... "Mom, where's my transcript?" A coach wants your NCAA Eligibility Center number... "Wait, where did we put that?" Someone asks for film... Now you're scrolling through your camera roll, searching old emails, looking through text messages, trying to remember which Google Drive link actually works. Don't do this to yourself. Recruiting already has enough moving pieces. This is one of the easiest ones to solve.
[¶2] CREATE ONE RECRUITING INFORMATION DOCUMENT. I recommend creating one Google Doc that becomes your recruiting home base. Name it something extremely obvious: FIRST NAME LAST NAME — RECRUITING INFORMATION. Then STAR the document in Google Drive so you can find it quickly. You can also save the link somewhere easy to access on your phone. The goal is simple: IF A COACH ASKS YOU FOR SOMETHING, YOU SHOULD KNOW EXACTLY WHERE TO FIND IT. Not tomorrow. Not after Mom gets home. Not once you find the email from six months ago. Right there.
[¶3] WHAT SHOULD BE IN THE DOCUMENT? Keep it simple. At the top: PLAYER NAME: Taylor Smith / GRADUATION YEAR: 2028 / HEIGHT: 6'0" / POSITION: Point Guard / HIGH SCHOOL: Example High School / AAU PROGRAM: She's Next 17U / JERSEY NUMBER: #3. Then include your important recruiting information.
[¶4] CONTACT INFORMATION. Athlete Email: taylorsmith28@example.com / Athlete Phone: (555) 555-5555. If appropriate, you can also keep parent contact information here for your own records. The athlete should have a professional email address that she actually checks. Something based on her name is perfect. You do not want a college coach emailing an account you haven't opened since sixth grade.
[¶5] NCAA ELIGIBILITY CENTER INFORMATION. Keep your NCAA Eligibility Center information easy to access. For example: NCAA ID: 1234567890. You may also want to keep the email address associated with your Eligibility Center account listed in your private recruiting document. Do NOT put passwords in this document. This document is meant to organize information you may need during recruiting — not become a place where you store sensitive login credentials.
[¶6] FILM. Remember the film system we already built? Put the link here. FULL GAME FILM FOLDER: [Google Drive Link] / HIGHLIGHT FILM: [Link]. Now when a coach says: "Can you send me some film?" You're not hunting for it. You open your recruiting document. Copy. Paste. Send. MAKE THE COACH'S JOB EASY.
[¶7] TRANSCRIPTS. I would also create a folder inside your recruiting Google Drive called: TRANSCRIPTS. Keep your most recent transcript there. You might have: Taylor Smith — Transcript — Fall 2027; Taylor Smith — Transcript — Spring 2028; And so on. Then inside your main recruiting document, include: MOST RECENT TRANSCRIPT: [Google Drive Link]. Just remember the distinction we've already discussed in the academics section: An unofficial transcript can be extremely useful during recruiting, but a college or eligibility organization may eventually require an official transcript sent through the proper process. Your recruiting folder doesn't replace that process. It simply means you're organized when a coach asks to see your academics.
[¶8] KEEP YOUR SCHEDULE HERE TOO. This is another one families are constantly sending. HIGH SCHOOL SCHEDULE: [Link] / AAU SCHEDULE: [Link] / UPCOMING EXPOSURE EVENTS: April 17–19 — Event Name — Indianapolis, IN; May 15–17 — Event Name — Chicago, IL; July 6–9 — Event Name — Louisville, KY. Now imagine a coach texts: "Where are you playing in July?" You don't have to ask your AAU coach. You don't have to scroll through TeamSnap. You don't have to search your mom's text messages. You already have it.
[¶9] I WOULD ORGANIZE GOOGLE DRIVE LIKE THIS. 📁 TAYLOR SMITH — RECRUITING. Inside: 📄 Taylor Smith — Recruiting Information; 📁 Film; 📁 Transcripts; 📁 Schedules; 📁 Recruiting Documents. You don't need 47 folders. You need a system that is simple enough that you will actually use it.
[¶10] UPDATE IT AS RECRUITING CHANGES. Your recruiting document shouldn't be something you create freshman year and never look at again. Update it. New transcript? Replace the old link with the newest one. New highlight video? Update it. New AAU schedule? Add it. Changed teams? Update it. New phone number? Update it. New measurements? Update them. The information a coach sees should reflect who you are right now.
[¶11] THE ATHLETE SHOULD KNOW WHERE THIS IS. Parents, this is another area where it's tempting to handle everything yourself. But remember: Your job is to guide the process. Her job is to increasingly own it. Your daughter should know how to open this document. She should know where her film is. She should know her NCAA ID. She should know where her transcript is. She should know how to copy a link. She should know how to send those things professionally. Because eventually a coach may contact her and ask: "Can you send me your transcript?" And I want her response to be: "Absolutely, Coach. I'll send it over." Not: "I think my mom has it somewhere."
[¶12] SPEED MATTERS. I'm not telling you that a coach is going to stop recruiting your daughter because it took her three hours to find a transcript. That's not the point. The point is that recruiting moves quickly. Coaches are communicating with a lot of players. They're gathering information. They're evaluating. They're making lists. They're trying to fill roster needs. When someone asks you for something they need to continue evaluating you... GIVE IT TO THEM. Make it easy. Make it professional. Make it quick.
[¶13] ORGANIZATION REMOVES STRESS. This isn't glamorous. Nobody is posting an Instagram highlight about how organized their Google Drive is. But these little logistical things matter. And more importantly, they make the recruiting process so much easier on your family. You don't want every coach request to become a family emergency. You don't want: "WHERE IS HER TRANSCRIPT?!" "WHO HAS THE FILM LINK?!" "WHAT'S HER NCAA NUMBER?!" Recruiting is going to give you enough things to think about. DON'T LET FINDING A DOCUMENT BE ONE OF THEM. Create the system once. Keep it updated. Know where everything is. And when a coach asks? OPEN. COPY. SEND. MAKE THEIR JOB EASY — AND MAKE YOUR LIFE EASIER TOO.
