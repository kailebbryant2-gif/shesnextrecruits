[¶0] 3 Mistakes Parents Make During the Recruiting Process 🏀
[M1] 1. STARTING TOO LATE. One of the biggest mistakes I see families make is waiting until recruiting is actually happening to start learning about the process. By the time college coaches are reaching out, evaluating players, and making offers, there's already so much that parents and athletes should understand. Recruiting isn't something you want to figure out as you go! I'm not saying your daughter needs to be contacting college coaches in seventh grade, but YOU should be educating yourself about the process early. Understand the timelines, what coaches are looking for, and what your daughter should be focusing on at each stage. The families who are prepared can make better decisions when opportunities start coming.
[M2] 2. NOT BEING HONEST ABOUT HER SKILL LEVEL. Every parent wants the absolute best for their daughter, and I completely understand that! But one of the most important things you can do is be realistic about where she can actually compete, contribute, and be happy. I've been on the other side of recruiting, and I can tell you that there's a BIG difference between wanting to play Division I basketball and being a Division I-level player. And that's OKAY! There are incredible opportunities at the Division II, Division III, NAIA, and JUCO levels. The goal shouldn't just be getting your daughter to the highest division possible. It should be finding a school where she can be successful academically, athletically, and personally. Don't let the name of a division overshadow what's actually best for your daughter.
[M3] 3. NOT KNOWING WHAT YOU DON'T KNOW. This one is HUGE! So many families are doing all the things they THINK they're supposed to do. Playing AAU, attending camps, paying for training, sending emails, and traveling to tournaments. But here's the question: Is there actually a strategy behind any of it? When I was a recruiting coordinator, there were things happening behind the scenes that most families had absolutely no idea about. Recruiting boards, evaluation periods, positional needs, scholarship availability, and how coaches prioritize players are just a few examples. You can spend thousands of dollars and countless hours doing basketball-related activities without actually making progress in recruiting. That's why I always say, don't confuse activity with progress! You need to understand how the process works so you can make intentional decisions instead of just hoping something works out.
[C1] Here's what I want parents to understand: You don't have to know everything about recruiting right now, but you DO need to recognize that there's a lot more to this process than simply being a good basketball player. The earlier you educate yourself, the better equipped you'll be to help your daughter navigate the opportunities and challenges ahead. That's exactly why I created She's Next. I want to take what I learned from sitting on the other side of the recruiting desk and give families the knowledge and tools to approach this process with confidence.
