[¶1] Now that you understand the difference between interest and an actual offer, let's take it one step further. Because coaches don't always tell you exactly where your daughter stands. They may say things that are technically true but don't tell you the whole story. And sometimes the most important part isn't what they say. It's when they say it, how often they say it, and what they actually do afterward. You have to learn how to listen to the language.
[TL1] FIRST: UNDERSTAND THE TIMELINE. As we've talked about throughout this course, recruiting generally builds as an athlete gets older. A simple way I want you to think about it is: FRESHMAN YEAR: Some interest. SOPHOMORE YEAR: Big interest. JUNIOR YEAR: GO TIME. RIGHT BEFORE SENIOR YEAR: Commitment time. Obviously, every player and every program is different. Some athletes are recruited much earlier. Some are discovered later. Some programs move faster than others. But this is a helpful framework for understanding what a coach's words actually mean. Because the same sentence can mean something completely different depending on when you hear it.
[FR] FRESHMAN YEAR: "WE LIKE HER." During freshman year, a coach may tell you: "We really like her." "We're going to keep watching her." "She's a player we're interested in." "Keep us updated on her schedule." That's good. You want coaches watching. You want relationships beginning. You want your daughter getting on recruiting boards. But freshman year is still largely about evaluation and projection. The coach is trying to figure out: How good can this player become? What does she look like in two years? Is she still developing? What position will she play? How much stronger can she get? How much better can she become? So don't panic if freshman year isn't full of offers.
[SO] SOPHOMORE YEAR: "WE REALLY LIKE HER." Now the language should start becoming more specific. You may hear: "She's one of the players we're really interested in." "We want to keep recruiting her." "We'd like her to come to campus." "We want to continue building this relationship." "We're watching her closely." This is when I want families to start paying much closer attention. Is the coach actually watching her? Are they calling? Are they asking about her schedule? Are they asking about her development? Are they asking about her academic situation? Are they trying to get her on campus? Are they talking about what they see her doing in their program? The more specific the conversation becomes, the more you can learn about the level of interest.
[JR] JUNIOR YEAR: THIS IS GO TIME. By junior year, things should start becoming much more clear. This is the point where coaches are making serious decisions about who they want in their future roster. So if a coach is telling you: "We really want her." You should start asking: "What does that actually mean?" Does it mean they want to continue evaluating her? Does it mean she's one of their top five? Does it mean they're preparing an offer? Does it mean they are waiting on another player? Does it mean they have already offered other players for the same spot? You need to understand the difference.
[LJ] WHAT IF THEY'RE HOLDING OUT AN OFFER LATE IN JUNIOR YEAR? This is a very important situation to understand. If your daughter is getting deep into her junior year and a school is still showing interest but has not offered, you need to find out why. There are several possibilities. Maybe they just discovered her. That's possible. Maybe she has made a huge jump and they are just now realizing how good she is. Also possible. But there is another possibility. They may have already recruited other players for that spot and lost them. Maybe their first choice committed somewhere else. Maybe their second choice committed somewhere else. Maybe their roster needs changed. Now they're looking for additional options. Those are very different situations. And I want you to find out which one you're dealing with. Because it can affect how your daughter is viewed when she arrives on campus. Was she someone they identified early and intentionally recruited? Or did they come back to her because other options didn't work out? Neither situation automatically makes the school a bad opportunity. But you should understand where you stand. You want to know whether she is coming into that program as a player they really wanted or as their fourth or fifth option. And the only way to know is to ask good questions.
[BB1] NOW LET'S TALK ABOUT THE "BACK BURNER". This is a phrase I want you to understand because it happens all the time in recruiting. A coach may have several players they like. But they can't take all of them. So they may have: Player 1 — their top choice; Player 2 — another strong option; Player 3 — another option; Player 4 — another option; And so on. What do they do? They keep those other relationships warm. That's what I mean by putting someone on the back burner. You're not out. But you're not hot. You're warm. The coach may still text. They may call occasionally. They may ask how your season is going. They may tell you they enjoyed watching her. They may check in every week or two. They want to keep the relationship alive. But they may not be pushing the relationship forward. Why? Because they're waiting on their first choice. They don't want to lose you completely in case Player 1 chooses another school. So they keep you warm.
[BB2] HOW DO YOU KNOW IF YOU'RE ON THE BACK BURNER? Look at the pattern. If a coach: Contacts you occasionally; Checks in every week or two; Doesn't move the conversation forward; Isn't talking seriously about an offer; Isn't asking about your decision timeline; Isn't pushing toward a visit; Keeps saying "we'll keep watching"; Continues showing interest but never takes the next step — You may be on the back burner. That doesn't mean they don't like your daughter. It means she may not be their first choice right now. And that's an important distinction.
[HI] WHAT DOES IT LOOK LIKE WHEN YOU'RE HIGH ON THE LIST? Now look at the other side. If a coach is: Calling frequently; Staying in regular contact; Following up after games; Asking about her schedule; Watching her consistently; Talking about her development; Asking about other schools; Asking what she wants in a college; Trying to get her on campus; Talking specifically about her role; Talking about roster spots; Asking about her decision timeline; Trying to move the relationship forward — Those are much stronger signs. A coach who really wants a player generally doesn't want to lose contact with her. They want to stay fresh. They want to know what is happening. They want to know who else is recruiting her. And they want her to know exactly how interested they are.
[CA] BUT HERE'S THE IMPORTANT CATCH. Communication frequency means something different when there is an offer. If your daughter already has an offer and a coach is calling frequently, that's a very strong sign that they are actively trying to move the relationship forward. If she doesn't have an offer, frequent communication is still positive. But it is still interest. Remember what we just learned: Interest is not an offer. So don't take frequent texts and calls and automatically turn them into: "They're going to offer her." Maybe they will. But until they actually do, you don't know that.
[LL] LISTEN FOR THE LANGUAGE BEHIND THE LANGUAGE. When a coach says: "We really like her." Ask yourself: Okay. How much? When they say: "She's one of our favorites." Ask: What does that mean compared to the other players you're recruiting? When they say: "We're going to keep watching." Ask: What are you waiting to see? When they say: "We'd love to have her here." Ask: Are you offering her a spot? When they say: "You're one of our top recruits." Ask: How many players are ahead of her? You don't have to be confrontational. You just need to understand.
[BQ] THE BEST QUESTION YOU CAN ASK. At some point, it is completely reasonable to ask a coach: "Where does she currently stand in your recruiting class?" That's a great question. It gives the coach an opportunity to be honest. You may learn: "She's one of our top two." Great. You may hear: "She's one of several players we're evaluating." Okay. Now you know. You may hear: "We're waiting to see what another player does." That's extremely important information. You may hear: "We really like her, but we don't know if we'll have a spot." Now you know that too. Information is power in recruiting.
[WD] DON'T JUST LISTEN TO WHAT THEY SAY. Watch what they do. A coach can tell you: "We love her." But if they haven't watched her in six months, that's information. A coach can tell you: "She's one of our top players." But if they aren't moving the relationship forward, that's information. A coach can tell you: "We want her here." But if another player commits and suddenly the communication disappears, that's information. Words matter. But actions matter too.
[BT] BIG TAKEAWAY. You don't need to become a detective. You just need to understand recruiting language. Remember the timeline. Freshman year → some interest. Sophomore year → big interest. Junior year → GO TIME. Before senior year → commitment. Then pay attention to the level of communication. Are they keeping her warm? Or are they actively trying to get her? Are they waiting on another player? Or is she the player they're waiting for? And most importantly: Does she have an offer, or are you interpreting interest as an offer? Don't be afraid to ask. Don't be afraid to clarify. And don't let vague language create a false sense of security. Your goal isn't to hear what you want to hear. Your goal is to understand where your daughter actually stands.
