# FILMORG-T · Collecting/Organizing Film: How to Store and Send Your Film (written text)

[¶1] Having good film isn't enough. Your film needs to be easy to find, easy to open, and easy to watch. Remember who you're sending it to: A college coach is busy. You don't want them clicking five different links, requesting access to a file, downloading a huge video, or trying to figure out which video they're supposed to watch. Make it easy for the coach.
[¶2] THE SYSTEM I RECOMMEND. For most families, I recommend using Google Drive as your central film library. You can upload your full games to Drive and organize them in folders. Then, instead of sending the actual video file every time, you send the coach a link. Google Drive allows you to set a video to: Anyone with the link → Viewer. Then you copy the link and paste it into an email or text. The coach can open the link and watch the video without you having to send the giant video file itself. This works whether you're using an iPhone, Android phone, tablet, or computer.
[¶3] YOUR FILM FOLDER. Don't just dump 20 videos into a folder called: "Basketball Videos." Organize it. I recommend creating one main folder: [PLAYER NAME] — RECRUITING FILM. Inside that folder, create: FULL GAMES. Then organize your games by season. For example: 2026–27 HS Season. Inside: 2026-11-20 vs. Smithville; 2026-11-28 vs. Jefferson; 2026-12-18 Christmas Tournament; 2027-01-05 vs. Central; 2027-01-12 vs. West. Now a coach can immediately understand what they're looking at.
[¶4] NAME YOUR FILES CORRECTLY. This sounds small. It isn't. Don't upload: IMG_4827.MOV or VID_193847.mp4. A coach has no idea what that is. Instead: Taylor Smith — 2028 — HS vs Central — 1-12-27. Now the file itself tells the coach what they're watching. I would use this formula: PLAYER NAME — GRAD YEAR — OPPONENT — DATE. If it's a tournament: PLAYER NAME — GRAD YEAR — TOURNAMENT — OPPONENT — DATE. This also makes your own film library much easier to manage six months later.
[¶5] THE MOST IMPORTANT RULE: TEST YOUR LINK. Before you send a coach your film: OPEN THE LINK YOURSELF. Even better, open it from a different device or account if possible. Make sure: The video opens; The video plays; The coach doesn't have to request access; The correct game is linked; The video isn't accidentally private; The video isn't the wrong file. Google Drive allows you to change general access and select "Anyone with the link," then assign the viewer permission. Never assume the link works. Test it.
[¶6] HOW TO SEND FILM THROUGH EMAIL. This is going to be your primary method. Do not attach a full basketball game directly to an email. Instead: Put the film in Drive → copy the link → paste the link into the email. Your email might say: Coach Smith, / My name is Taylor Smith, and I'm a 2028 guard at ABC High School. / I wanted to introduce myself and share some recent full-game film with you. / I am #12 in the video. / Full Game Film: [LINK] / Player Profile: [LINK] / I'll be playing at [tournament/event] on [dates], and I've included my upcoming schedule below. / Thank you for your time, Taylor Smith. That's it. Don't make the coach hunt for the film.
[¶7] HOW TO SEND IT THROUGH TEXT. Texting can also be useful — but only when you have a legitimate reason to text the coach. If a coach has already communicated with you by text, or has given you their number and encouraged you to send updates, you can send the Drive link directly. For example: Coach Smith! This is Taylor Smith from ABC High School. I wanted to send you my full game from Friday. I'm #12. Here's the link: [LINK]. That's incredibly easy for the coach. They tap the link. They watch. Done. Don't text a giant video file. Text the link.
[¶8] IPHONE. If you're filming or storing your video on an iPhone, you have several options. You can upload the video to Google Drive and share the Drive link. You can also share files directly from the iPhone Files app through Messages or Mail, and iCloud Drive supports sharing files and folders by link. But for recruiting, I still recommend: iPhone → Google Drive → Link → Coach. Why? Because now your film is stored in one place instead of being scattered between: Text messages; Your camera roll; iCloud; Email; Random video apps. One recruiting film library.
[¶9] ANDROID. Same idea. You don't need a completely different system because you're on Android. Upload your video to Google Drive. Set the file to: Anyone with the link → Viewer. Copy the link. Then paste it into your email or text. Google Drive supports this sharing process on Android as well. The coach doesn't care whether you filmed it on an iPhone or Android. They care whether they can click the link and watch the player.
[¶10] WHAT ABOUT YOUTUBE? YouTube can also work. If you choose to use YouTube, I recommend Unlisted, not Public. An unlisted video won't normally appear in YouTube search or on your public channel, but anyone who has the link can watch it. They also don't need a Google account just to watch the unlisted video. That makes YouTube a reasonable option for families who already use it heavily. But for this course, I would still make Google Drive the default recommendation. Why? Because I want your entire recruiting library in one place.
[¶11] WHAT I DON'T WANT YOU DOING. ❌ Don't send a 3 GB video attachment through email. Send a link. ❌ Don't send a coach 15 separate video links. Start with the most relevant full game. ❌ Don't send a link that requires permission. Test your sharing settings first. ❌ Don't make the coach download the video just to watch it. Give them something they can click and watch. ❌ Don't make them search through your entire camera roll. Organize your film. ❌ Don't send a link labeled "Video." Tell them exactly what they're watching.
[¶12] MAKE THE COACH'S JOB EASY. Imagine two players email the same coach. PLAYER A: "Here's some film!" [Random Google Drive folder with 14 videos] PLAYER B: Full Game — vs. Central — January 12 / #12 — Taylor Smith — 2028 / [Watch Full Game]. Which one do you think is easier for the coach? Player B. And recruiting is full of tiny things like this. We're constantly trying to make it easier for coaches to evaluate you.
[¶13] THE FILM SYSTEM I WANT YOU TO BUILD. By the time you're actively recruiting, you should have: MAIN FOLDER: Taylor Smith — Recruiting. FOLDER 1: Full Games. FOLDER 2: Highlight Film. FOLDER 3: Player Profile. FOLDER 4: Schedule. FOLDER 5: Recruiting Information. And inside your Full Games folder: 2026–27 HS Season: Game 1; Game 2; Game 3; Game 4; Game 5. Now when a coach asks: "Do you have more film?" You don't have to start searching your phone. You already have it.
[¶14] AND HERE'S THE BIGGER POINT. Organization isn't just for parents. It's part of being a recruit. College coaches are evaluating more than what happens between the lines. They're also getting a sense of: Can this player communicate? Can this player follow through? Can this player make my job easier? Is this family organized? You don't need to overthink that. Just be professional.
[¶15] YOUR FILM-SENDING CHECKLIST. Before sending film to a coach, ask: ☐ Is this a full game? ☐ Is this a game that accurately represents me? ☐ Is my jersey number obvious? ☐ Is the video clearly labeled? ☐ Does the link work? ☐ Can the coach watch without requesting access? ☐ Is the link easy to find in my email? ☐ Did I include my graduation year? ☐ Did I include my high school? ☐ Did I include my upcoming schedule? ☐ Did I tell them what they should look for? If all of those are checked: Send it.
[¶16] REMEMBER. Your goal isn't to impress a coach with how complicated your recruiting system is. Your goal is to make it incredibly easy for them to evaluate you. Film should be one click away. Your information should be organized. Your full games should be easy to find. And when a coach says: "Send me some film." You should be able to send it in less than a minute. That's what being prepared looks like.
