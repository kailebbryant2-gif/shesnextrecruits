[¶1] If I had to pick one position that is the truest definition of a swing player, it would be the 4. And I cannot stress this enough: 4 PLAYERS ARE SO VALUABLE. Why? Because the 4 can give a coach options. And we already know how much coaches love options.
[¶2] WHAT DOES A 4 LOOK LIKE? Think about your daughter. Maybe she has decent size, but she's not quite tall enough to be a true post. Maybe she's athletic enough to play on the perimeter, but she's not quite fast enough to consistently take smaller guards off the dribble. But here's the thing: Those smaller guards can't necessarily handle her in the post. Now you've got something really valuable. She has enough size to create problems inside. She has enough perimeter skill to move around outside. She may not be the biggest player on the floor. She may not be the fastest player on the floor. But she can create a matchup problem because she lives somewhere in between. THAT IS YOUR 4.
[¶3] WHY IS THE 4 SO MARKETABLE? Let's imagine a coach has two different lineups. SMALL LINEUP: The coach wants to play small and fast. Your daughter can play the 5. She has enough size and strength to battle inside, rebound and defend bigger players. BIG LINEUP: Now the coach wants to put more size on the floor. Your daughter moves to the 4. The coach can put a true 5 next to her and now your daughter gets to use her perimeter skills against a bigger player. That is a completely different matchup. And here's where it gets even better. If she's developed enough perimeter ability, she may even be able to give the coach some minutes at the 3. Now you've gone from: "She's a 4." to: "She can play the 3, 4 and 5." That's a completely different player on a recruiting board.
[¶4] THINK ABOUT THE MINUTES. This is where versatility becomes more than just a nice thing to have. It can create more opportunities for playing time. Let's say a coach has: A true 5; Your daughter; A true 3. If your daughter can only play the 4, there is one obvious place for her. But what happens if she can play the 5? Now she can get minutes when the team plays small. What happens if she can play the 3? Now she can get minutes when the team plays big. Suddenly, instead of being limited to one position, she can potentially fill multiple roles depending on what the team needs. That's music to a coach's ears. And honestly? That's music to your ears too. Because more ways to use your daughter can mean more opportunities for her to get on the floor.
[¶5] THE 4 CREATES MATCHUP PROBLEMS. This is another reason I love the 4 position. A good 4 can make the other team uncomfortable. Put her against a smaller player? Take her inside. Put her against a bigger player? Take her outside. Can she shoot? Can she attack? Can she pass? Can she rebound? Can she defend multiple positions? Can she post up? Can she make plays off the dribble? Now the opposing coach has a problem. Who do we put on her? That's what you want. You want your daughter to become the player that creates difficult decisions for the other team.
[¶6] THIS IS WHY WE TALKED ABOUT NOT PIGEONHOLING. Remember the first lesson in this module? If you decide too early that your daughter is only a post, you may never develop the perimeter skills that could make her an incredible 4. And if you decide she's only a guard, you may never develop the strength, post skills and inside game that could make her incredibly difficult to guard. The 4 position is one of the best examples of why I want young players developing the entire game. You don't know what her body is going to look like at 16. You don't know how fast she'll become. You don't know how skilled she'll become. And you don't know what a college coach is going to need when she's ready to play. So give her tools.
[¶7] THE PERFECT SWING PLAYER. A great 4 doesn't have to be the tallest player. She doesn't have to be the fastest player. She doesn't have to be the best ball handler. She doesn't have to be the best shooter. But she needs to have enough of everything to create options. That's what makes her valuable. She can swing. She can move up. She can move down. She can adapt to the lineup. She can exploit matchups. She can fill a hole when a teammate is injured. She can give a coach different lineup combinations. And every time you give a coach another way to use you, you give that coach another reason to recruit you.
[¶8] THE BIG TAKEAWAY. If your daughter has size but isn't quite a true 5... And she has perimeter ability but isn't quite a traditional 3... Don't think she's stuck in the middle. That may actually be her biggest advantage. She may be the perfect 4. And if she can legitimately play the 3, 4 and 5? Now you've got a swing player. That means more options for the coach. More lineup possibilities. More ways to solve problems. And potentially more opportunities for your daughter to play. Remember: COACHES LOVE OPTIONS. And a really good 4 gives them a lot of them.
