[¶1] If your daughter has big basketball goals, there is going to be work involved. Practices. Training. Lifting. Travel. Film. Camps. Recruiting. There will be times when she needs discipline and accountability. But parents, be careful that basketball doesn't become something your family is talking about, analyzing, and pushing ALL THE TIME. SHE CAN LOVE BASKETBALL AND STILL NEED A BREAK FROM BASKETBALL. Not every car ride needs to be about recruiting. Not every dinner needs to be about her game. Not every free afternoon needs another workout. Not every mistake needs a lesson. And not every opportunity needs to be taken simply because it's available.
[P1] WATCH THE PRESSURE YOU CREATE. Sometimes pressure doesn't sound like pressure. It sounds like: "You need to go shoot today." "Other girls are working while you're sitting here." "Do you really want to play in college?" "We're spending all this money on basketball..." "If you want that offer, you need to do more." One comment might not seem like much. But when basketball constantly feels connected to guilt, comparison, money, or disappointing Mom and Dad... Something she once loved can slowly start feeling like an obligation.
[P2] LET THE DREAM BECOME HERS. You can provide opportunities. You can drive her. You can pay for training. You can help her build a schedule. You can encourage her. You can hold reasonable standards. But eventually: SHE HAS TO WANT IT. We've already talked about developing the player within the player. There has to be some internal motivation. You cannot want a college basketball career badly enough for both of you. And if you're constantly pulling her toward the goal, eventually you need to ask whether she's actually running toward it herself.
[P3] REST IS NOT LAZINESS. Athletes need time to recover physically and mentally. A day off does not erase months of work. Going to a friend's house does not mean she isn't committed. Taking a vacation does not mean someone else is suddenly going to steal her scholarship. Let her have other interests. Let her spend time with friends. Let her laugh. Let her rest. Let her be a teenager. Basketball can be a huge part of her life without becoming her entire life.
[P4] PAY ATTENTION TO THE SIGNS. If the athlete who once couldn't wait to get into the gym suddenly dreads basketball... Pay attention. If every conversation about basketball becomes an argument... Pay attention. If she's constantly exhausted, overwhelmed, irritable, or mentally checked out... Pay attention. Don't automatically assume she needs to be pushed harder. Sometimes the answer is that she needs space.
[P5] SUPPORT THE PERSON, NOT JUST THE PLAYER. Your daughter should know that whether she: Scores 20 or 2. Starts or sits. Gets the offer or doesn't. Plays Division I or never plays college basketball at all... SHE IS STILL YOUR DAUGHTER. Your excitement about her cannot only show up when basketball is going well. Give her opportunities. Teach discipline. Encourage big dreams. Help her work toward them. But leave enough room for her to actually enjoy the journey. Because the goal isn't to push so hard toward college basketball that she stops loving basketball before she ever gets there. SUPPORT HER. GUIDE HER. HOLD HER ACCOUNTABLE. AND GIVE HER ROOM TO BREATHE. WE WANT TO DEVELOP A GREAT ATHLETE WITHOUT BURNING OUT THE PERSON INSIDE OF HER.
