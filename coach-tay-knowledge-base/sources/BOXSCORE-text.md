[¶1] Let's start with something really simple. WHAT IS THE BOX SCORE? The box score is the list of statistics that gets recorded during a game. For our purposes, we're talking about: Points; Rebounds; Steals; Free throws; Turnovers. These are the things that are easiest to see when someone looks at the numbers. And yes — they matter. But here's the problem: The box score doesn't tell the whole story. A coach watching your daughter isn't just looking at how many points she scored. They're watching everything she does between those numbers. And some of the things that make a coach want a player on their team never get recorded anywhere.
[¶2] THE THINGS THAT DON'T SHOW UP IN THE BOX SCORE. Here's where I want athletes to really pay attention. Because these are things you can control right now. You don't have to be 6'2". You don't have to be the fastest player on the floor. You don't have to be a Division I-level shooter. You don't even have to be the most talented player in the gym. You can make yourself stand out by doing the things that require effort, awareness, attitude, and discipline. And I would not consider these optional. These should be the goal of the game. The points, rebounds, steals, and everything else? Those are the bonus.
[H] HUSTLE. Did you sprint back on defense? Did you sprint the floor in transition? Did you chase down a loose ball? Did you dive on the floor? Did you make the extra effort to save a possession? Did you go after a rebound even when you weren't the one closest to it? A coach notices that. And here's the important part: Hustle is a choice. You don't need a special skill to sprint. You don't need a scholarship to dive on the floor. You don't need to be the most talented player to give maximum effort.
[M] WHAT HAPPENS AFTER YOU MAKE A MISTAKE? This is one of my favorite things to watch as a coach. Because everyone makes mistakes. You turn the ball over. You miss an assignment. You get beat. You miss a shot. You make the wrong read. The mistake itself isn't necessarily what I'm most interested in. What do you do next? Do you stand there? Do you put your head down? Do you complain? Or do you immediately turn around and SPRINT BACK? A player who makes a mistake and immediately gets back into the play tells me something. She understands that the next possession matters more than the last one. That is a recruiting trait.
[D] DEFENSE. A steal shows up in the box score. Great defense doesn't always. Maybe you completely shut down your matchup and she never gets a clean shot. Maybe you force her to give the ball up. Maybe you make her pick up her dribble. Maybe you force her into a bad pass. Maybe you rotate perfectly and take away a driving lane. Maybe you contest a shot without fouling. Maybe you communicate a screen and prevent your teammate from getting beat. None of those things necessarily show up as a statistic. But a coach watching the game sees them. And here's another one: Tips and deflections. Maybe you get your hand on the ball but don't actually steal it. The box score says nothing. The coach says: "I saw that." Those little plays add up.
[T] TALK. Good players communicate. Are you talking on defense? Are you calling out screens? Are you telling your teammate where the help is? Are you communicating in transition? Are you calling out the play? Are you talking when you're on the bench? Are you helping your teammates understand what's happening? You don't have to be the loudest person in the gym. But you should be communicating. And this is especially important as you get older. College basketball is fast. Coaches don't want five people silently running around the floor. They want players who see the game and communicate what they see.
[K] KNOW THE PLAYS. This one is huge. You can be incredibly athletic and still hurt your team if you don't know what you're supposed to be doing. Do you know the offense? Do you know where you're supposed to be? Do you know your reads? Do you know the defensive rotations? Do you know what happens when the ball goes somewhere? Are you paying attention in practice? Are you listening when the coach is teaching? Being focused is a skill. And coaches notice players who consistently know what they're doing.
[C] BE COACHABLE. This is one of the biggest things I watched for as a coach. When a coach corrects you: What happens? Do you roll your eyes? Do you argue? Do you make excuses? Do you look frustrated? Or do you listen, acknowledge it, and immediately try to fix it? Coaches know you're not going to be perfect. They're looking for someone they can teach. A player who can take coaching and apply it quickly is extremely valuable. Don't just listen to coaching. Show that you can use it.
[CH] CHEER FOR YOUR TEAMMATES. This might sound small. It isn't. When you're on the bench, are you engaged? Do you celebrate your teammates? Do you stand up when something great happens? Do you encourage someone who just made a mistake? Do you help create energy? Or are you sitting there looking completely disconnected from the game? Coaches notice this. Because they're not recruiting five individual players. They're recruiting a team. And they want people who make the people around them better.
[F] BE THE FIRST ONE TO HELP. This is one of those things that takes almost zero basketball skill. Someone falls? Help her up. The ball rolls away? Go get it. The referee needs the ball? Sprint over and hand it to them. Your teammate makes a great play? Celebrate her. Your teammate makes a mistake? Pick her up. Someone needs help? Be the person who helps. These little things tell me a lot about a player. They tell me about her awareness. They tell me about her attitude. They tell me about her character. And they tell me whether she is thinking about more than herself.
[R] RUN THE FLOOR. Want an easy way to stand out? RUN. When your team gets the ball: Sprint. Don't jog down the floor. Sprint past everyone. Get yourself into the offense before the defense is ready. When your team loses the ball: SPRINT BACK. Get yourself between your player and the basket. Pick someone up. Protect the rim. Help your team get organized. This doesn't require a complicated skill package. It requires effort. And coaches LOVE players who consistently run.
[P] PICK UP FULL COURT. You don't have to wait until someone tells you to play hard. If your role allows it, pick someone up. Make the other team uncomfortable. Pressure the ball. Force them to work to get over half court. And then do it again. That tells a coach: "I'm willing to do the hard things."
[FO] FOCUS. This might be one of the biggest separators of all. Are you locked in when you're playing? Are you locked in when you're on the bench? Are you listening during timeouts? Are you watching the game? Are you paying attention when the coach is talking? Are you mentally engaged even when the ball isn't in your hands? Because here's something I want every athlete to understand: You are being evaluated even when you don't have the ball.
[S1] HERE'S THE BIG SECRET. Most of these things don't require elite talent. They require: EFFORT. ATTITUDE. FOCUS. AWARENESS. DISCIPLINE. And those things can separate you from players who may be more talented than you. Think about how many players are in a gym. How many of them can score? A lot. How many can shoot? A lot. How many can handle the ball? A lot. How many are going to consistently: Sprint back after a mistake? Talk every defensive possession? Help a teammate up? Cheer from the bench? Know every play? Take coaching? Get every 50/50 ball? Deflect passes? Run the floor? Pick up full court? Make the extra effort? Stay completely engaged? Far fewer. That's your opportunity.
[S2] DON'T MAKE THESE THINGS OPTIONAL. I would actually challenge you to change the way you think about the game. Don't walk into a game thinking: "I need to score 15 points." Walk into the game thinking: "I am going to impact every possession." Sprint. Talk. Defend. Rebound. Run. Communicate. Help. Encourage. Listen. Execute. Get back after mistakes. Do the little things. And then let the box score take care of itself.
[S3] Because when you consistently play this way, something interesting happens. You start creating more opportunities for the things that DO show up in the box score. You sprint the floor → you get an easy basket. You defend → you force a bad shot. You deflect a pass → your teammate gets the steal. You attack the glass → you get more rebounds. You move without the ball → you get open. You communicate → your team gets stops. You know the offense → you make better decisions. You take coaching → you improve. The things outside the box score can actually create the things inside the box score.
[S4] TRUST ME ON THIS. As a coach, I would rather have a player who impacts the game in 20 different ways than a player who only knows how to score. Because when the shot isn't falling, what do you have left? When you're having a bad offensive game, can you still help me win? When you aren't getting touches, do you still play hard? When you're sitting on the bench, are you still part of the team? When you make a mistake, how quickly do you recover? When the game gets hard, what kind of player are you? That's what coaches are watching. The box score is information. It is not your entire evaluation. So don't make the box score your goal. Make impact your goal. And let the box score be the result.
[S5] YOUR GOAL EVERY GAME: MAKE YOURSELF IMPOSSIBLE TO IGNORE. Not because you scored the most points. Not because you had the most steals. Not because your name was at the top of the stat sheet. But because when a coach watches you, they keep thinking: "I WANT THAT KID ON MY TEAM." And if you can become that player? Your recruiting looks can increase exponentially.
