## 👩‍👧 Parents should be responsible for:
[P1] Understanding the recruiting timeline and rules
[P2] Learning how the recruiting process actually works
[P3] Helping create a realistic plan
[P4] Keeping track of important dates, events, academics, film, and communication
[P5] Helping evaluate opportunities and spending decisions
[P6] Making sure their daughter has the resources and support she needs
[P7] Helping her communicate professionally when needed
[P8] Asking thoughtful questions and helping her make informed decisions
[P9] Protecting her from unnecessary pressure, confusion, and outside opinions
[P10] Encouraging her when recruiting gets frustrating or uncertain
[P11] Knowing when to step in — and when to step back

[¶1] Your job is to guide the process. Her job is to own it.

## 🏀 Athlete's Role
[¶2] The athlete cannot simply be the person everyone is talking about.
[¶3] She needs to be part of the process.
[¶4] She should gradually take ownership of:
[A1] Knowing what she wants from her basketball career
[A2] Understanding what coaches are looking for
[A3] Developing her game and athletic ability
[A4] Taking academics seriously
[A5] Learning how to advocate for herself
[A6] Communicating with coaches
[A7] Watching and understanding her own film
[A8] Knowing her schedule and recruiting information
[A9] Building confidence and learning to handle rejection
[A10] Developing the habits and character coaches evaluate
[A11] Asking questions
[A12] Being invested in her own future

[¶5] As she gets older, more and more of the responsibility should move from the parent to the athlete.
[¶6] The goal isn't for a parent to have a daughter who gets recruited.
[¶7] The goal is to help develop an athlete who knows how to advocate for herself, communicate, make decisions, and take ownership of her future.
