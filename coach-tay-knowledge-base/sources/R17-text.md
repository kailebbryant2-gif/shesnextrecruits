[¶1] As your daughter's recruiting process becomes more serious, coaches are going to start asking for information. They may want to see her grades. They may want her transcript. They may need her NCAA information. They may ask questions about her basketball history. And eventually, her college's compliance office is going to need official documentation.
[¶2] Don't wait until a coach asks for something to figure out where it is. One of the easiest things you can do as a family is create a recruiting folder and keep everything organized.

[OU1] OFFICIAL VS. UNOFFICIAL TRANSCRIPTS. This is something I want parents to understand because the two are not interchangeable.
[OU2] An unofficial transcript is essentially a copy of your daughter's academic record that can be viewed or shared by the family or athlete. It is useful during recruiting because a coach may want to get a quick look at: Current GPA; Classes; Grades; Academic progression; Graduation timeline; General academic profile.
[OU3] If a coach says: "Can you send me her transcript?" it is reasonable to ask: "Do you need an unofficial copy for recruiting, or does your compliance office need an official transcript?" Those are two different things. An unofficial transcript can be useful for recruiting conversations and preliminary evaluation.
[OU4] OFFICIAL TRANSCRIPTS. An official transcript is different. https://www.parchment.com/ Parchment the link above is an easy way to send official transcripts. For NCAA Eligibility Center purposes, an official transcript is one that is submitted directly by the high school to the Eligibility Center, rather than simply being handed to the student or parent. High schools can upload official transcripts through the NCAA High School Portal. Source: NCAA Questions and Resources / High School Resources.
[OU5] That distinction matters. You can't simply take a screenshot of your daughter's grades and assume the NCAA will accept that as an official transcript. The NCAA specifically says official academic records submitted by the high school are used to evaluate academic eligibility.
[OU6] One thing parents should also know: official transcripts usually cost money to request and/or send. The exact fee depends on the high school or transcript service, so check with the school when you need one. Unofficial copies are often available to the student at no additional cost.
[OU7] Think about it this way: Unofficial = useful for recruiting conversations. Official = used when the school or NCAA needs verified academic records.

[W1] WHEN WILL A COACH WANT AN UNOFFICIAL TRANSCRIPT? A coach may say "Can you send me her transcript?" They may simply want to see where daughter stands academically. Useful to assess admissibility. Remember: coaches aren't only recruiting basketball players; they're recruiting students. A coach may love her game, but if academic profile doesn't fit, problem. So having updated unofficial transcript ready saves back-and-forth.
[W2] WHEN DOES THE OFFICIAL TRANSCRIPT MATTER? Particularly NCAA eligibility certification. For D1/D2, Eligibility Center needs official academic records, and final official transcript with proof of graduation must ultimately be submitted for certification. High school submits official transcript to Eligibility Center. If multiple high schools, don't assume all records transfer. Missing documents can delay certification.

[ID1] KEEP YOUR NCAA INFORMATION HANDY. Your daughter should have NCAA Eligibility Center ID number saved. Keep it in recruiting folder. Have simple document: Athlete Name: / Graduation Year: / NCAA ID: / NCAA Eligibility Center Account Email: / High School: / High School Counselor: / Counselor Email: / Current GPA: / Core-Course GPA, if available: / High School Graduation Date:
[ID2] NCAA recommends email student checks regularly and will have after high school. Use professional email. First + last ideal. "This is her recruiting identity."

[F1] KEEP YOUR DOCUMENTS IN ONE PLACE. Digital recruiting folder: Google Drive/iCloud/Dropbox.
ACADEMICS: Current unofficial transcript; Previous transcripts; NCAA-approved core-course info; Test scores; Graduation requirements; Academic awards.
NCAA: NCAA ID; Account info; Correspondence; Completed tasks; Certification info.
BASKETBALL: Player profile; Measurements; AAU info; HS info; Schedule; Film links; Stats; Awards.
RECRUITING: School list; Coach contacts; Emails; Calls; Visits; Offers; Notes.
Goal: when coach asks, you know exactly where it is.

[AM1] NOW LET'S TALK ABOUT AMATEURISM. Amateurism is part of athletics eligibility; NCAA Eligibility Center reviews sports participation and athletics-related activities. It may ask about teams, events, expenses, awards, advisers, people who marketed athletic ability. Keep records.
[AM2] KEEP TRACK OF HER BASKETBALL HISTORY. Save: AAU teams; Club teams; High school teams; Camps; Tournaments; Showcases; Leagues; Training programs; Awards; Athletic expenses; People who have represented or marketed her; Organizations involved in basketball career. You don't necessarily need to send all immediately; be able to answer accurately if asked. Eligibility Center can include sports participation info.
[AM3] DON'T ASSUME "I PAID FOR IT" MEANS THERE'S A PROBLEM. Legitimate family-paid basketball expenses/training/tournament participation don't automatically mean eligibility lost, but details matter. If unsure about money, award, team, event, sponsor, person, ask before doing it. Use high school counselor, college compliance office, governing organization.
[AM4] NIL HAS ALSO CHANGED THE CONVERSATION. NIL isn't same as simply being paid to play basketball. Current NCAA recognizes NIL activities like social posts, appearances, endorsements, camps/clinics when requirements met. Don't assume automatically okay or violation; circumstances matter and rules differ by association/division/stage.
[AM5] KEEP YOUR ANSWERS HONEST. If NCAA asks, answer accurately. Don't omit because you think it doesn't matter. Don't guess. Don't give desired answer. If something happened years ago and you're unsure, find out. Eligibility Center can request more info. Goal isn't to hide; maintain clean, accurate record.

[C1] YOUR RECRUITING PAPERWORK CHECKLIST: ☐ Current unofficial transcript ☐ Official transcript submission information ☐ NCAA Eligibility Center ID ☐ NCAA account email ☐ Athlete's professional email ☐ High school counselor contact ☐ Current GPA ☐ NCAA core-course info ☐ Test scores, if applicable ☐ AAU team history ☐ HS team history ☐ Tournament/event history ☐ Awards/honors ☐ Current player profile ☐ Film links ☐ Schedule ☐ Recruiting school list ☐ Coach contact information ☐ Recruiting communication history
[C2] ONE OF THE BEST HABITS. Update folder throughout high school. New transcript, team, award, coach, film, school interest: update. Few minutes throughout year beats reconstructing four years senior year.
[C3] BIG TAKEAWAY. Recruiting is easier when prepared. Don't wait for coach to ask. Have transcript, NCAA info, email, film, schedule, records. Most importantly, keep accurate history. You don't need to become compliance expert; organized enough to answer. Coach says "Can you send me that?" Answer: "Absolutely. I'll send it over."
