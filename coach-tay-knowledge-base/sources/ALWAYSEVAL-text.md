# ALWAYSEVAL-T · You Are Always Being Evaluated (written text)

[¶1] Here's something I want every athlete to understand: The evaluation doesn't stop when you leave the court. In fact, sometimes the things you do away from the ball tell a coach more about you than what happens during the game. If a college coach is at a tournament watching your team, they aren't only watching when you have the ball. They're watching the entire environment. They're watching warmups. They're watching the bench. They're watching how you interact with your teammates. They're watching how you respond to your coach. They're watching how you carry yourself between games. And if they're at the same hotel? They may see you there, too. That doesn't mean you have to walk around like you're at a business meeting all weekend. You can laugh. You can have fun. You can be a teenager. But there is a difference between having fun and being careless. You need to understand that you have more at stake.
[¶2] TREAT THE ENTIRE WEEKEND LIKE A BUSINESS TRIP. If you're traveling to an exposure tournament, I want you to think about the entire weekend differently. You aren't just going on a basketball trip. You're going on a recruiting trip. That means: At the gym: Be locked in. During warmups: Be focused and energetic. On the bench: Be engaged. Between games: Be respectful and composed. At the hotel: Carry yourself appropriately. At restaurants: Be respectful to the people around you. In public: Remember that you represent yourself, your family, your school, and your team. You never know who is watching. And more importantly, you never know who knows who.
[¶3] WARMUP BEHAVIOR MATTERS. Warmups aren't the time to act like the game hasn't started yet. They're part of your preparation. A coach can walk into a gym and immediately start forming opinions. Are you moving with purpose? Are you getting shots up? Are you engaged with your teammates? Are you listening to your coach? Are you going through the motions? Are you laughing and messing around the entire time? Are you focused? A coach may not know anything about you yet. Your behavior may be the first thing they see. Give them a good first impression.
[¶4] THE BENCH IS NOT A BREAK FROM BEING EVALUATED. This is one of the biggest mistakes I see. A player thinks: "I'm not in the game, so nobody is watching me." Wrong. The bench is part of the evaluation. Are you watching the game? Are you cheering for your teammates? Are you communicating? Are you paying attention during timeouts? Are you standing up and celebrating great plays? Are you encouraging someone who just made a mistake? Are you mentally involved? Or are you: Sitting slouched over; Looking at your phone; Talking to your friends; Complaining; Making faces; Ignoring the game; Acting disinterested. A coach may be sitting across the gym watching your teammate. And while they're watching your teammate... they may be watching you, too.
[¶5] HOW DO YOU REACT WHEN YOU AREN'T PLAYING? This one tells me a LOT about a player. Maybe you're not getting the minutes you want. Maybe you got subbed out. Maybe you disagree with a call. Maybe your teammate is getting more shots than you. Maybe you're frustrated. What do you do? Do you pout? Do you stop cheering? Do you throw your hands up? Do you talk negatively about your coach? Do you sit there with a bad attitude? Or do you stay engaged and continue supporting your team? Because college coaches are thinking beyond basketball. They're thinking: "If I recruit this player, I'm going to have her on my team for four years." They aren't just recruiting the jump shot. They're recruiting the person who comes with it.
[¶6] THE HOTEL COUNTS TOO. This is where I really want parents and athletes to understand the stakes. If you're at a tournament and a college coach happens to be staying at the same hotel, you may see them. Maybe you see them in the lobby. Maybe you're getting breakfast. Maybe you're coming back from dinner. Maybe you're with your teammates. Maybe you run into another team. You don't need to become a robot. But you do need to carry yourself with poise. Be respectful. Use appropriate language. Be considerate of hotel staff. Don't run through the hallways. Don't scream and carry on late at night. Don't create problems. Don't treat the hotel like your personal playground. And absolutely don't assume: "Nobody from college is here." Because you don't know.
[¶7] THIS DOESN'T MEAN YOU CAN'T HAVE FUN. I want to make this very clear. Being recruited does not mean you have to be serious 24/7. Laugh with your teammates. Have fun. Enjoy the tournament. Go get food. Make memories. Be a kid. That's all okay. The difference is how you carry yourself while you're doing it. There is a huge difference between: having fun and being disrespectful, disruptive, immature, or out of control. You can be fun without being wild. You can be confident without being arrogant. You can be competitive without being disrespectful. You can be excited without losing your composure. That's maturity.
[¶8] THIS APPLIES AT SCHOOL TOO. And this isn't just a tournament rule. Your behavior at school matters. Think about the people who could potentially be connected to your recruiting process: Your high school coach. Your AAU coach. Your teachers. Your teammates. Your administrators. Other parents. Other coaches. College coaches. You don't get to turn your recruiting personality on when you walk into a gym. Your character follows you everywhere. And as an athlete who wants to play college basketball, you have more at stake. You have goals you're trying to accomplish. You have opportunities you're trying to create. You have a future you're working toward. Carry yourself like it.
[¶9] YOU HAVE MORE TO LOSE. This is not meant to scare you. It's meant to make you aware. If a college coach is considering two players who are similar athletically and one has a reputation for being respectful, coachable, responsible, and mature... While the other has a reputation for being disrespectful, disruptive, or difficult... Who do you think the coach is more comfortable bringing into their program? Remember: A coach isn't just recruiting you. They're recruiting the responsibility that comes with you. They have to coach you. They have to trust you. They have to put you around teammates. They have to represent you as part of their program. They have to deal with your family. They have to help you develop for four years. And if they see behavior that makes them think: "I don't know if I want to deal with that for four years..." That's an easy reason to cross your name off the list. Maybe you were talented. Maybe you could play. Maybe you could have helped them. But recruiting isn't just: "Can she play?" It's also: "Do we want her here?"
[¶10] YOUR REPUTATION TRAVELS. You might think one little interaction doesn't matter. But basketball is a small world. Coaches know other coaches. AAU coaches know college coaches. High school coaches know college coaches. Players talk. People remember players. And a coach doesn't need to have a dramatic negative experience with you. Sometimes it's something as simple as: "I really like her game, but I'm not sure about her attitude." And just like that, you may move down the board. You don't want to give a coach a reason to question you.
[¶11] CARRY YOURSELF LIKE SOMEONE WHO HAS GOALS. This is ultimately what I want you to take away. You don't need to be perfect. You are going to make mistakes. You're going to get frustrated. You're going to have bad days. You're going to laugh too loud sometimes. You're going to be a teenager. That's okay. But learn to carry yourself with maturity. Be respectful. Be coachable. Be aware of your surroundings. Be someone people enjoy being around. Be someone coaches trust. Be someone your teammates respect. And understand that your behavior is part of your marketability. Because the question isn't only: "Can she help us win?" It's also: "Do we want this person representing our program?" Make the answer easy.
[¶12] ON THE COURT: Compete. ON THE BENCH: Engage. IN WARMUPS: Prepare. AT THE HOTEL: Carry yourself with poise. AT SCHOOL: Protect your reputation. AROUND PEOPLE: Show respect. EVERYWHERE: Remember what you're working toward. You are building a reputation long before you are building a college career. And coaches are always looking. Make sure what they see is someone they want in their program.
