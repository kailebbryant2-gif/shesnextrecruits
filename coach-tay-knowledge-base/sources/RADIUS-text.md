[¶1] If high school basketball is going to be an important part of your daughter's recruiting exposure, one of the first things you need to understand is distance. Not every school is equally realistic to invite to a high school game. And this is where I want you to think differently.
[¶2] THE 2-HOUR SWEET SPOT. For high school recruiting, I would start with schools that are within about a 2-hour driving radius. That's your sweet spot. Why? Because you're trying to give a coach a reason to come see your daughter. A coach who is 45 minutes away has a much easier decision to make than a coach who is 4 hours away. A coach who is 1 hour and 15 minutes away? Very realistic. A coach who is 1 hour and 45 minutes away? Still very realistic, especially if she's playing in a tournament where that coach already has another reason to be there.
[¶3] Once you start getting beyond two hours, you can still absolutely recruit those schools. But you need a stronger reason for them to make the trip. And I would generally consider 3–4 hours your outer recruiting radius for high school exposure, not your primary target.
[¶4] The farther away the school is, the more you need something else working in your favor: They already know your daughter; They have already watched her film; They have another player they want to see; You're playing in a meaningful tournament; Your high school coach has a relationship with the program; The coach specifically asked to see her; There is already established recruiting interest. Distance doesn't make a school impossible. It just changes how hard it is to get them into the gym.
[EX1] LET'S USE A REAL EXAMPLE. Let's say you live in St. Louis. You could literally search: "NCAA Division II women's basketball schools within 2 hours of St. Louis" And start building your list from there. For example, schools such as: University of Missouri–St. Louis; Maryville University; McKendree University; Missouri S&T; Quincy University are all examples of Division II programs in the broader St. Louis regional recruiting area. The GLVC currently includes these programs along with several others across Missouri and Illinois.
[EX2] And notice something important: They aren't all the same distance away. McKendree is extremely close to St. Louis — Lebanon, Illinois is roughly 30–40 minutes from the city. Missouri S&T in Rolla is roughly 1 hour 40–50 minutes away. Quincy is around 2¼ hours from St. Louis, depending on the exact starting point and route. That gives you a very different recruiting strategy for each school. You might confidently invite McKendree to a regular-season game. You might invite Missouri S&T to a tournament or an important matchup. You might still include Quincy, but now you have a stronger reason for them to make the drive. This is how you should think about your list.
[DV1] DON'T JUST SEARCH ONE DIVISION. This is another mistake I see families make. They decide: "We want Division II." And then they only search Division II schools. Don't do that. If your goal is to find schools that make sense for your daughter, search all levels. For example, around the St. Louis area you could also research Division III programs. The St. Louis Intercollegiate Athletic Conference includes programs such as Webster, Principia, Fontbonne, Greenville, Spalding and Westminster, while Washington University in St. Louis is also a Division III program. You can also search NAIA programs. For example, Columbia College and William Woods are both Missouri programs that give you another level to research. Your recruiting radius should be built around opportunity — not around the division you decided you want before you understand your options.
[HW1] HERE'S EXACTLY HOW TO FIND THE SCHOOLS. You don't need a recruiting service to do this. You can start with Google.
[HW2] STEP 1: Search by division. Try searches like: "NCAA Division II women's basketball Missouri" "NCAA Division II women's basketball Illinois" "NCAA Division III women's basketball St. Louis" "NAIA women's basketball Missouri" "JUCO women's basketball Missouri Illinois" Then start writing down schools.
[HW3] STEP 2: Put the school into Google Maps. Search: [School name] to [your city] For example: "Missouri S&T to St. Louis" "McKendree University to St. Louis" "Quincy University to St. Louis" Don't worry about whether Google says 1 hour 52 minutes or 2 hours 7 minutes. You're looking for a realistic driving radius, not an exact mathematical cutoff. Traffic, where you live, game time and the location of the gym all matter.
[HW4] STEP 3: Find the women's basketball staff. Once you've found a school that makes sense, go directly to the school's athletic website. Search: [School name] women's basketball Then find: Roster → Coaches → Staff You are looking for names and emails for: Head coach; Associate/assistant coaches; Recruiting coordinator, if listed. Not every program will use the exact same title. That's okay. Your goal is to identify the actual women's basketball coaching staff.
[TR1] NOW YOU HAVE A REAL TARGET LIST. Let's say you find 25 schools. Don't email all 25 with the exact same message. Organize them.
[TR2] TIER 1 — UNDER 1 HOUR. These are your easiest schools to invite. You should be thinking: "If we're playing a meaningful game or tournament, there is very little geographic barrier."
[TR3] TIER 2 — 1–2 HOURS. This is your sweet spot. These schools are still realistically within driving distance, but you want to give them a reason to make the trip. A tournament becomes much more valuable here.
[TR4] TIER 3 — 2–3 HOURS. These schools are still worth recruiting. But don't expect them to regularly drive several hours to watch one high school game. You need more behind the invitation.
[TR5] TIER 4 — 3–4 HOURS. These are your outer-radius schools. Absolutely include them if they're a great fit. But don't make your high school recruiting strategy depend on getting these coaches into your regular-season gym. At this distance, film, AAU exposure, tournaments, coach relationships and prior communication become increasingly important.
[EM0] NOW LET'S TALK ABOUT WHAT YOU ACTUALLY SAY. This is where I want you to avoid the generic: "Hi Coach, I'm Taylor. I'm a 2028 guard. Come watch me play." Give the coach information that makes it easy to evaluate whether the trip makes sense.
[EM1] EXAMPLE #1 — LOCAL SCHOOL. Let's say you're playing a big game against a strong local opponent and the school is 30–45 minutes away. Your email could look like this: Subject: 2028 [Position] — [High School] — Upcoming Schedule / Coach [Last Name], / My name is [Player Name], and I'm a [graduation year] [position] at [High School] in [City, State]. / I wanted to introduce myself and send over my upcoming schedule because I know your program is relatively close to us and I would love the opportunity for you to see me play. / I'm #12, and our upcoming games are: [Date] — [Opponent] — [Time] (x3) / I've also included my player profile and film below. / Thank you for your time, [Name] [Phone] [Email]. Simple. Professional. And you're giving them a reason to consider coming.
[EM2] EXAMPLE #2 — A TOURNAMENT. This is where I really want you to understand the difference. Instead of: "Come watch me play Friday night." Try: Subject: 2028 [Position] — [Tournament Name] Schedule / Coach [Last Name], / My name is [Player Name], a 2028 [position] at [High School]. / I'm reaching out because we'll be playing in the [Tournament Name] in [City] over Christmas break, and I wanted to make sure you had our schedule. / I know this event brings several programs into the gym, so I wanted to introduce myself and make it easy for you to find me if you're attending. / I'm #12 for [High School]. / Our schedule: [Date] — [Time] — [Opponent] — [Court/Gym] (x3) / Here is my film and player profile: [Links] / Thank you for your time, Coach. [Name]. See the difference? You're not saying: "Please drive here just for me." You're saying: "If you're already coming to this event, here's where you can find me." That's a much easier decision for a coach.
[MS1] AND HERE'S THE BIGGEST MISTAKE. Don't send an email to a coach who is four hours away saying: "We're playing Tuesday at 6:00. We'd love for you to come." That is not impossible. It's just not strategic. Instead, ask yourself: WHY WOULD THIS COACH COME? If your answer is: "Because my daughter is really good." That's not enough. The coach doesn't know that yet. Your answer should be something more like: "They're 45 minutes away." or: "They already recruit our area." or: "They're already recruiting another player at this tournament." or: "They've already watched her film and asked to see her in person." or: "We're playing in a major tournament where they'll already be recruiting." Give the coach a reason to make the trip.
[FM1] YOUR HIGH SCHOOL RECRUITING FORMULA. Here's the formula I want you to remember: RIGHT SCHOOL + RIGHT DISTANCE + RIGHT EVENT + RIGHT COMMUNICATION + RIGHT PERFORMANCE = A MUCH BETTER CHANCE OF GETTING EVALUATED. You cannot control whether a coach ultimately recruits you. But you can control whether you're putting yourself in situations where it makes sense for them to evaluate you. And that's what this entire high school recruiting strategy is about. Don't just invite coaches to games. Give coaches a reason to come. Then when they come... Give them a reason to come back.
