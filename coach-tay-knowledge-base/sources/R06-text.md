[¶1] Now that we've established why I don't want you pigeonholing your daughter into one position too early, let's talk about the reality of how different positions are recruited. The truth is, some positions get recruited faster than others. And if you understand why, a lot of things you see during the recruiting process will suddenly make more sense.
[¶2] POST PLAYERS GET RECRUITED FIRST. If we're talking about the order in which college coaches typically have to address positions, post players are at the top of the list. Why? Height. Have you ever heard the phrase: "You can't teach height." There's a reason coaches say it.
[¶3] If your daughter is 6'3"+, she is going to have a much easier time getting recruited than most players. And the taller she is, the more interest she is likely to receive. That's just the reality of the game.
[¶4] That doesn't mean a 6'3" player can have no basketball ability and still get recruited. She can't. There still has to be a player there. But height gives a player more room for error. A 6'5" athlete who is still developing her skills can be incredibly intriguing to a college coach because the coach knows that if they can develop the skill set, they already have something you cannot teach.
[¶5] THINK ABOUT THE HOOP. The basket is 10 feet high. The closer you are to the basket, the easier it is to score. And the closer you are to the basket defensively, the easier it can be to affect shots, rebound and make things difficult for the other team. That's an oversimplification, obviously. But it explains why size is so valuable. There are only so many girls who are 6'3", 6'4", 6'5", 6'6"+. There are a lot more guards. And that brings us back to our recruiting board.
[¶6] WHY COACHES RECRUIT MORE POST PLAYERS AT ONCE. Remember the recruiting board we talked about? A coach isn't just asking: "Who is the best player?" They're asking: "What do I need, and how many players like this are actually available?" There are only so many high-level post players available in each recruiting class. And coaches know that. So if a coach needs a post player, they may have a much larger list of post players they're tracking than the number of players they actually intend to take. Why? Because coaches know they're going to hear no. One player may choose a bigger program. Another may choose to stay closer to home. Another may commit before the coach can offer her. Another may not be interested. Another may simply not be the right fit. So the coach has to protect the position on the recruiting board. There are fewer players to choose from, so they have to be prepared for more of those players to say no.
[¶7] That's why you may see something that looks strange from the outside. Your daughter may have a teammate who is significantly taller than her, and that teammate may start getting college interest much earlier. That doesn't necessarily mean the taller player is more skilled. It may simply mean she's harder to find. That's an important distinction.
[¶8] BUT WHAT IF YOUR TALL DAUGHTER DOESN'T WANT TO PLAY POST? This is where things get interesting. I've coached tall players who didn't want to play inside. They wanted to be guards. And honestly? That's great — if they can actually play on the perimeter. If your daughter is tall, coordinated, skilled and can genuinely handle the ball, pass, shoot, attack and defend on the perimeter, that's a fantastic skill set. But there is one thing I want you to understand. She has to be capable of playing there. And we're going to talk much more about what college coaches actually need from guards later in this module. Because the guard pool is significantly larger. There are simply more players competing for those spots.
[¶9] THIS IS WHERE VERSATILITY BECOMES SO VALUABLE. Let's say your daughter is 6'3". She can play inside. But she can also handle the ball. She can shoot. She can attack. She can pass. She can defend on the perimeter. She can guard inside. She can rebound. Now you have something really valuable. She's not just a post. She's not just a guard. She's a player who can swing. And I want you to remember that word.
[¶10] SWING. A "swing" player is someone who is capable of playing more than one position or filling more than one role. That can be incredibly valuable to a college coach. Maybe the coach loses a post player to injury. Maybe they need more size inside. Maybe they recruit another guard and suddenly need to use a bigger player differently. Maybe the coach has two players who can play the 4, but one of them can also play the 5. Options are valuable. If your daughter can legitimately give a coach multiple options, you've just widened the number of ways that coach can use her. And that's what coaches love.
[¶11] COACHES LOVE OPTIONS. This is one of the biggest things I want you to take away from this entire module. Be versatile. Don't try to become a player who can technically play every position but isn't good at anything. That's not what I'm saying. I'm saying: Develop enough skill to give a coach options. A player who can only play one very specific role has a smaller pool of opportunities. A player who can legitimately fill multiple roles gives a coach more ways to build a roster around her. And when you're recruiting a player, that matters. The more ways I can use you, the more reasons I have to recruit you.
[¶12] So if your daughter is tall and can play inside and on the perimeter, don't hide either part of her game. If she's a smaller player who can attack, shoot, defend and also make plays around the basket, don't hide that either. Don't market her as one thing when she can actually do more.
[¶13] THE BIG TAKEAWAY. Yes, post players tend to get recruited faster. Height is rare. That doesn't mean height replaces talent. It means height can give a player more opportunity and more room to develop before a coach gives up on the projection. And if your daughter is tall, don't automatically force her into the post. Teach her to become capable inside and capable on the perimeter. Because when you can honestly say: "She can play the 4, but she can also give you minutes at the 5." or "She's a bigger player, but she can also handle and play on the perimeter." You've just made her more valuable. Coaches LOVE options. And versatility can open doors that one-position players simply don't have.
