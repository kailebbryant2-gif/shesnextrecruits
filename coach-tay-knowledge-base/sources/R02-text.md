[¶1] One of the biggest things I want you to understand about recruiting is that college coaches are not recruiting one class at a time. They are recruiting multiple classes simultaneously.
[¶2] When I was a coach and recruiting coordinator, I was looking at our current roster, the players we already had committed, the players we had offered, the positions we needed, and the future classes we were trying to build — all at the same time.
[¶3] That means when you hear: "She's been offered." you still need to understand: Which class? How many spots? Who else has been offered? How badly does that school actually want her? And where is she on that coach's recruiting board? This is where understanding the recruiting board becomes extremely important.
[¶4] THIS EXAMPLE IS FROM 2026. The board you're learning from is an example of what a recruiting board could look like in 2026. The exact timing and number of offers will vary from program to program, but I want you to pay attention to the strategy behind the board.
[¶5] You will probably never actually see a college coach's recruiting board. These boards are generally kept private within a coaching staff. But know that it exists. Every coaching staff is keeping track of the players they are evaluating, recruiting, offering, waiting on, and potentially using as backup options.

[C27] 2027: THE CLASS IS BASICALLY BUILT. By this point, a college program recruiting the 2027 class would likely already have its major pieces in place. Remember, these athletes are heading into their senior year. If a program wants a player to sign during the early signing period, they need to have that recruiting relationship and offer situation figured out well before that signing date. So when you're thinking about the 2027 class in this example, you're not looking at a group of players who are all still wide open. Most of the decisions have already been made. There may still be movement. There may be a player who changes her mind. There may be a roster change. There may be a late addition. But generally, this class is already much further along than the younger classes.

[C28] 2028: THE HOLD-YOUR-BREATH CLASS. This is where recruiting starts to get really interesting. The 2028 class is likely to have a number of players who have already received offers. But here's something parents don't always understand: An offer does not mean every player who has an offer is going to get a spot. Let's say a coach has two spots available and has offered five players they would be happy to take. The coach may be waiting for those players to make their decisions. Maybe Player A is their favorite. Player B is their second choice. Player C is their third choice. The coach may tell you something like: "We have two spots and we've offered five players." That information is extremely valuable. Why? Because now you understand that the offer is real, but the spot is not necessarily waiting forever. If one of the players above your daughter commits, the coach may have to move on to another player. And if your daughter is one of the players they really want, they may start applying more pressure. You might hear: "We'd really like you to make a decision." Or: "You're one of our top choices." Or: "We have another player considering the same spot." This is where you need to understand what the coach is actually telling you. The coach isn't necessarily trying to pressure you for no reason. They are managing a limited number of roster spots while multiple players are making decisions at the same time. If another player commits, the coach may have to call the other recruits and say: "The spot has been filled." That is recruiting. And if you're sitting on an offer thinking, "I'll just wait and see what happens," you need to understand that the recruiting board is moving while you're waiting.

[C29] 2029: THE "GET A FOOT IN THE DOOR" CLASS. The 2029 class can look very different. At this point, a program may already have a lot of offers out. But here's something that can be confusing: Some of the players receiving those offers may look WAY too good for that particular program. And you may wonder: Why would that school offer her? She's probably going somewhere much bigger. There can actually be a strategy behind it. One reason is what we called "kick-back" players. A coach may know that a really talented local or regional player is probably headed toward a major state school or a higher-level program. But they still want to establish a relationship. Why? Because basketball careers change. A player may choose a major Division I program and later realize: This isn't the right fit for me. Maybe she isn't playing. Maybe she wants to be closer to home. Maybe she wants a smaller school. Maybe she wants a different style of basketball. And suddenly she remembers: "That school offered me when I was younger." That relationship already exists. The coach has already seen her play. They already know her. They already recruited her. That can be extremely valuable later. So an offer isn't always about: "We think you're definitely coming here." Sometimes it's about: "We want to establish this relationship now because we don't know where you'll end up later." That's a completely different recruiting strategy.

[C30] 2030: THE OFFERS START TRICKLING IN. Now we're getting into a much younger class. For 2030, coaches are beginning to identify players who are separating themselves. Maybe a player has made a major jump. Maybe she's grown. Maybe her skill level has improved dramatically. Maybe she's playing at a higher level of competition. Maybe a coach has a relationship with her AAU coach. Maybe someone they trust told them: "You need to go watch this kid." And suddenly that player is on the board. You may start seeing offers trickle in. But here's the important part: NOT EVERY EARLY OFFER IS GOING TO STICK. A lot can change between 8th grade and the end of high school. Players develop differently. Players grow. Players change positions. Players move teams. Players stop developing. Players develop much faster than expected. Roster needs change. Coaching staffs change. Programs change. And sometimes a player who received an offer when she was young simply isn't the same player a few years later. That's why I don't want you looking at an early offer and thinking: "She's set." She's not. She's still developing.

[C31] 2031: THEY MAY BE WATCHING — BUT THAT'S VERY DIFFERENT. Could a major Division I program be watching a 2031 player? Absolutely. Could they know her name? Yes. Could they have watched her? Yes. Could they even have started tracking her development? Absolutely. But that doesn't mean they have made a real recruiting decision. At this age, there is still a tremendous amount that can change. A player has a lot left to prove. So if your daughter is in this age group and a major program watches her, that's exciting. But don't confuse: "They're watching her." with: "They're recruiting her." And don't confuse: "They're recruiting her." with: "They've offered her." And don't confuse: "They've offered her." with: "She's going there." Those are completely different stages.

[B1] THE BIGGEST THING I WANT YOU TO UNDERSTAND. You may never see a college coach's recruiting board. But you need to understand that every coaching staff has some version of one. It may be a physical board. It may be a spreadsheet. It may be software. It may be notes inside a recruiting system. The format doesn't matter. The strategy does. Coaches are constantly organizing and evaluating their recruiting classes. So don't just count offers. Think about what is happening behind the scenes.
[B2] Ask: How many players are being recruited for her position? How many roster spots are actually available? How many players have been offered? Is the coach waiting on another player? Is your daughter a top option or a backup option? Is the coach actively communicating? Has the coach watched her recently? Is the coach trying to get her to campus? Is the coach asking about her timeline? Is the coach talking about what her role could look like? Is the coach trying to move the relationship forward? Those answers tell you much more than simply seeing the word OFFER next to a player's name.
[B3] THE BIG TAKEAWAY. When you understand that a recruiting board exists, you start to understand why recruiting can change so quickly. College coaches are constantly managing their boards. They are evaluating players. They are comparing players. They are waiting on decisions. They are adjusting to roster changes. They are protecting themselves in case a player chooses another school. And they are trying to build the best roster they can. Your job as a family is to understand where your daughter fits into that process. Don't just ask, "Does she have an offer?" Ask: "Where does she actually stand with that program?" That is a much better recruiting question.
