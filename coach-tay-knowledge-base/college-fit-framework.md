# College Fit Framework

Coach Tay's approach to finding appropriate college programs.
Labels and conventions: see `README.md`. This file documents her **reasoning** about fit. It is not a matching algorithm.

## 1. Division and pathway overview

### NCAA Division I
#### FIT-001 · D1 money doesn't depend on academics, but grades still break ties
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** Historically, D1 had a fixed number of full scholarships (she uses "10" for simplicity: "it's more than that"). Grades didn't change how much money you got: "There was no what you call equivalencies." "Now… they're changing it" to a pool of money, but D1 can "still… cover everything a lot easier than other levels." Academics don't affect D1 money the same way, but they still affect who gets recruited (EVAL-029).
- **Source:** [M3.L1 @00:09–02:24]
- **Written lesson (more precise):** "At Division I, the scholarship landscape changed significantly beginning in 2025. D1 schools that opt into the new House-settlement model no longer operate under the old sport-by-sport scholarship limits. Instead, they have roster limits, and schools can offer scholarships to athletes on those rosters." So don't treat D1 academics as a way "to earn scholarship money instead of basketball money," but academics still matter. [M3.L1-T ¶5–6]
- **Flags:** ⚠️ VERIFY (V-01). The written text is newer than the video's "they're changing it." Check current D1 roster-limit and scholarship rules, which may differ for schools that don't opt in.

### NCAA Division II
#### FIT-002 · D2: athletic money + academic money = "full ride packages"
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** At D2 (and NAIA and JUCO), "academics are huge" because coaches can combine basketball money with academic money. A coach can spread a limited budget, give full rides to top targets, and use academic aid to build near-full packages for "smart kids that are also good." Between two similar players, the coach goes after the one whose grades earn academic money, "because I know I can offer her pretty much a full ride and… she's most likely to say yes."
- **Written lesson:** "DII basketball operates under a partial-scholarship model… athletic aid can be combined with academic scholarships, grants and other financial assistance… Basketball money + academic money = a much stronger financial package." [M3.L1-T ¶7–8]
- **Source:** [M3.L1 @02:27–04:48], [M3.L1-T ¶7–8]
- **Flags:** Her dollar figures are illustrations ("this is obviously not correct"). ⚠️ VERIFY current D2 scholarship rules (V-01).

#### FIT-003 · D2 recruiting calendar is open, but budgets are tight
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** About a year ago, D2 contact and evaluation rules were "blown wide open." But D2 programs "do not have a lot of funds," so they mostly stick to the traditional live periods, which are set around the D1 calendar.
- **Source:** [R11 @05:28–07:35], [R12 @00:45–01:30, 10:25–10:41]
- **Flags:** ⚠️ VERIFY (V-04)

### NCAA Division III
#### FIT-004 · D3 doesn't offer athletic scholarships
- **Label:** EXPLICIT (brief)
- **Principle:** "Division 3 obviously doesn't have scholarships." D3 has fewer calendar rules.
- **Source:** [M3.L1 @02:31–02:33], [R12 @09:14–09:22]
- **Written lesson (clarifies the video):** "At Division III, there are no athletic scholarships. But that does NOT mean DIII is automatically expensive. DIII schools can provide merit-based and need-based financial aid, which means your daughter's academic profile can play a major role in what the family ultimately pays." Domestic D3 athletes generally don't go through NCAA academic certification; D3 schools set their own academic standards. [M3.L1-T ¶9], [R01-T D3]
- **Flags:** ⚔️ The video's "D3 obviously doesn't have scholarships, so not them" implied academics don't matter for D3 money. The text says they can matter a lot (Q-037). ⚠️ VERIFY (V-02).

### NAIA and JUCO (NJCAA)
#### FIT-005 · Don't overlook NAIA and JUCO. "It's just a label."
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** She pushes back on the stigma. "The best basketball I ever played was JUCO basketball." She played against D1-level players there. She played and coached at D2. NAIA "plays great basketball." Benefits she names:
  - Real playing time and involvement
  - National tournaments run "as close to… the big leagues as possible"
  - Often cheaper, with better combined packages
  - Coaches who are paid less and "so, so invested"
  - A path "to get to the level that you want"
  - "I've never met a person that went Juco and was like, I regret going Juco."

  But you don't choose JUCO just because it's fun. Her example: a player good enough for D1 chose NAIA "simply because she didn't want to leave home."
- **Her words:** "It's just a label, okay, but basketball is basketball." [04:09–04:12]
- **Source:** [M3.L2 @00:00–04:37], [M3.L1 @02:37–02:43]
- **Flags:** M3.L2 mentions written reasons in the text ("I go through here in the text") that we haven't received (Q-028).

#### FIT-020 · NJCAA: a development and transfer path, not the end
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** JUCO fits players who need more development time, want to play right away, need a different academic path, want to improve their recruiting profile, aren't ready for their target four-year level, want to save money, or want to transfer later. "Junior college does not mean your basketball career is over." Two years can be used "to develop, play, improve academically and then transfer." The NJCAA has three divisions with different aid structures, so "look at the specific school, program and financial package — not just the NJCAA label." NJCAA rules still apply. Verify eligibility with the school, especially after prior college classes, dual enrollment, transfers, or play at another college.
- **Source:** [M3.L2-T J1–J5]
- **Flags:** ⚠️ VERIFY (V-11)

#### FIT-021 · NAIA: its own eligibility center and standards
- **Label:** EXPLICIT (as stated); ⚠️ rules
- **Principle:** NAIA isn't "lesser." Every first-time NAIA athlete must register with **PlayNAIA** and get an eligible determination. The NCAA Eligibility Center doesn't cover it. Freshman eligibility as stated: graduate from an accredited high school with an overall HS GPA of at least 2.300/4.000, or the traditional route of meeting two of three (2.000+ GPA, top half of class, 18 ACT / 970 SAT). "Don't reduce the rule to simply 'you need a 2.3.'" In college: four seasons within the first 10 semesters, 12+ credit hours with normal progress, and no age limit like the NCAA's.
- **Source:** [M3.L2-T A1–A4]
- **Flags:** ⚠️ VERIFY all figures (V-10)

#### FIT-022 · The goal isn't the division. It's the right opportunity.
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** "They think the goal is to find a Division I, Division II or Division III NCAA school and everything else is a backup plan. That's not how I want you to think." "The best level is the level where your daughter can succeed — on the court, in the classroom, financially and personally." Instead of "Is it D1?" ask: Will she play? Will she develop? Does she like the coach, team and school? Can we afford it? Does the academic program fit? Will she be happy? Can she succeed? "Find the right school. Then find the right basketball fit."
- **Source:** [M3.L2-T ¶1–3, O1–O4]

#### FIT-023 · Rules change, so verify with the governing body and the school's compliance office
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** "NCAA, NAIA and NJCAA rules change. And sometimes they change quickly." Don't rely on what another parent or coach said. "Always verify the current rule with the governing organization and the school's compliance/athletic department. Your job is not to memorize every bylaw… [but] to know enough to ask the right questions."
- **Source:** [M3.L2-T O3], [R17-T AM3–AM4], [R01-T D3]
- **Flags:** Supports this knowledge base's ⚠️ VERIFY approach. A future tool should send families to official sources for rules.

The other is a big event "we know… everyone's coming out" for, because players want D1 coaches to see them. "They can be there" doesn't mean "they will be there."

## 3. Academic eligibility and academic fit

#### FIT-007 · Academics matter more than families think
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** Academics are "the stuff that you can actually control." They're a D1 tiebreaker and a money lever at D2/NAIA/JUCO. Coaches also feel institutional pressure: athletic directors watch team GPA ("a coach doesn't want their athletic director hounding them because the team GPA is a 2.5"), and athletics departments run GPA competitions between teams. "Do not let anyone tell you that's not important."
- **Source:** [M3.L1 @04:46–07:13]
- **Flags:** "2.5" is her example of a bad team GPA, not a recruiting threshold.

#### FIT-008 · NCAA Eligibility Center: necessary, but "the smallest part of the process"
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** Everyone says to register. "Yes, it's true… but it's… quite literally the smallest part of the process." Register around 8th grade or "probably before freshman year." It costs money; just make sure you're on the real NCAA site. Get familiar with the site, because you'll log in many times. College coaches often walk families through what they need. Three things to get comfortable with:
  1. Keep her eligibility number handy, because coaches will ask for it.
  2. Send unofficial and official transcripts.
  3. Protect amateurism. Watch TikTok and affiliate-brand deals: "make sure that she's… not getting paid for things."
- **Source:** [R01 @00:00–02:48]
- **Flags:** ⚠️ VERIFY: fee, timing, and especially **amateurism and NIL rules, which have changed a lot** (V-03, V-05). R17 (Batch 3) covers transcripts and official documents.

#### FIT-017 · Ask about the total financial package, not just basketball money
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** Don't only ask "How much basketball scholarship money can we get?" Ask "What is the total financial package this school could put together for my daughter?" With an offer, ask what it covers, what the family pays, whether academic money or other aid is included, and what conditions apply.
- **Source:** [M3.L1-T ¶11], [R03-T O2–O3]

#### FIT-018 · Academics belong in the plan from the start. "Qualifier" is only the minimum.
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** Your daughter should know: the grades and classes she needs, her target GPA, required core courses, whether she's on track for NCAA eligibility, the academic standards of schools she's interested in, scholarships she may qualify for, and test scores that may help. "Don't make the mistake of thinking: 'I just need to be a qualifier.' That's the minimum." Better academics keep "more doors… open — athletically, academically and financially." "She's being recruited as a student-athlete."
- **Source:** [M3.L1-T ¶18–24]

#### FIT-019 · The NCAA Eligibility Center, step by step (D1/D2)
- **Label:** EXPLICIT (as stated); ⚠️ rules
- **Principle:** "Being recruited does not automatically mean you're eligible." Her steps:
  1. Pick the right account: free Profile Page vs. Academic and Athletics Certification Account for D1/D2. Walk-ons are included. Use an email she'll keep after high school.
  2. Enter her full education history.
  3. Confirm her courses are NCAA-approved with the counselor early.
  4. Core courses (16 for D1/D2).
     - **D1:** 4 English, 3 math (Algebra I+), 2 science, 1 extra English/math/science, 2 social science, 4 additional. 10/7 rule. Minimum 2.3 core GPA.
     - **D2:** 3 English, 2 math, 2 science, 3 extra English/math/science, 2 social science, 4 additional. Minimum 2.2 core GPA.
     - Core-course GPA ≠ report-card GPA.
  5. Transcripts sent by each school.
  6. Sports information.
  7. Fee or waiver. "Registering is not the same thing as being certified."
  8. Watch the task list every year.
  9. Request final certification senior year, with final transcript and proof of graduation.

  D3 domestic athletes generally don't go through NCAA academic certification.
- **Source:** [R01-T ¶1–S9, D3]
- **Flags:** ⚠️ VERIFY all requirements and figures (V-09). ⚔️ Tone differs from the video's "the smallest part of the process" (Q-036).

## 4. What makes a program a realistic option

#### FIT-009 · Go after "legit options"
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** By 10th grade, choose live events and elite camps based on schools that have shown chatter or interest. Be "super realistic": a recruiting letter from Cornell doesn't make Cornell "feasible for me… in terms of basketball, for far away from home." Going where your real options are also shows you "how you match up."
- **Source:** [M2.G10 @01:16–02:13], [M2.G8 @02:30–02:41]
- **Flags:** none

#### FIT-010 · The right level beats the highest level
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** AAU coaches who "oversell" can land a player at a level above her real fit. She then plays fewer minutes, is unhappy, and transfers back down (a "kickback kid"). Ask: "is she truly happy? Is she truly going to be successful there and get… what she actually wants out of her college experience?" A good AAU coach asks what she wants in a college.
- **Source:** [R14 @07:45–11:03]
- **Flags:** How to tell her real level is MISSING (Q-027).

#### FIT-011 · Read the roster: who is graduating, at which position, and when
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** Coaches plan recruiting classes against their roster by position and graduation year (e.g., both point guards graduating by year X). "You can find all that stuff online… in two years when I'm going to graduate, they're going to be needing a point guard."
- **Source:** [R02 @00:53–02:08], [R07 @02:36–03:31, 05:04–05:27]
- **Flags:** The materials suggest this as research, not as a rule to rank schools.

#### FIT-012 · Inside the board: limited spots, placeholders, open spots, pulled offers
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** In her example, a class with four spots and two commits has two open spots and four offers out. Offers can be pulled once spots fill. Coaches may hold a spot for a transfer or late find, or leave one open on purpose for a stronger class next year. Visits ("wining and dining") are concentrated in the class going into junior year.
- **Source:** [R02 @02:00–06:30]
- **Flags:** none

#### FIT-013 · Interest is not an offer. Don't give up real offers while waiting.
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** Calls, texts, camp invites, coaches at games and talks with your coaches can all be "just interest." An offer is explicit: "we are offering you a scholarship… a full, or… a partial… It will not be confusing." Until a school offers, "we can't put all of our eggs in that basket." Her example: waiting on KU's interest while ignoring offers from Emporia State and Washburn, then losing both.
- **Source:** [R03 @00:00–04:42]
- **Flags:** none

#### FIT-014 · Read coaches' language and contact frequency to know where you stand
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:**
  - **What's appropriate to hear by grade:**
    - Freshman: "we like her… we're gonna keep watching her." Just be on their radar.
    - Sophomore: "we really like her… we want to keep recruiting her." Recruiting (in contact, visits) is different from watching.
    - Sophomore→junior: talk of what they can offer.
    - Junior: "we want to have an offer, a legit offer."
  - **No offer by junior year, but heavy contact** = probably "a backburner kid… keeping her warm."
  - **Top targets (her "player one/two")** get a text about weekly and a call about every two weeks "like clockwork," with constant pushes to schedule visits, personal follow-ups ("five steals last night"), and contact with mom and the high school coach.
  - **Back-burner players** get texts about every two weeks to monthly and vague visit timing ("we're thinking… late November").
  - **Questions to ask:** "Where does she currently stand in your recruiting class?" "Are you super interested or still evaluating?" "What do you need to see from her?"
  - When a coach says "we love her, she's one of our top players," "take that seriously."
- **Source:** [R04 @02:32–08:33]
- **Flags:** Contact frequencies are her estimates ("probably"), not rules. ⚠️ Contact is also limited by NCAA calendars (V-04).

#### FIT-015 · An elite camp invitation isn't proof of interest
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** Camps earn coaches money: she says a quarter to a third of a coach's promised salary can come from camps, so filling them is part of the job. If a school is already evaluating you (at games, messaging) and invites you, they're interested. If there's been little contact, the invite is "probably to fill a spot." Either way, "if you show up and show out," you can get on their radar or even earn an offer. "Hey girl, like come to elite camp and let's have a conversation after" is a very different invitation.
- **Source:** [R13 @00:30–03:48, 06:30–06:40]
- **Flags:** The salary share is her claim (⚠️ VERIFY if used publicly, V-06).

#### FIT-024 · Where her class stands on the board (2026 example)
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** Using fall 2026 as the example:
  - **2027 (seniors):** "basically built." Programs need things settled well before the early signing period.
  - **2028:** "hold-your-breath." Multiple offers for few spots ("We have two spots and we've offered five players"). The offer is real, but "the spot is not necessarily waiting forever… the recruiting board is moving while you're waiting."
  - **2029:** "get a foot in the door." Many offers, some to players who look "WAY too good" for the program (see FIT-025).
  - **2030:** offers "trickle in." "NOT EVERY EARLY OFFER IS GOING TO STICK."
  - **2031:** a major D1 program "may be watching," but watching ≠ recruiting ≠ offer ≠ going there.

  "You will probably never actually see a college coach's recruiting board." Ask:
  - How many players are being recruited for her position?
  - How many spots are there?
  - How many offers are out?
  - Is the coach waiting on someone?
  - Is she a top or backup option?
  - Has the coach watched her recently?
  - Is the coach pushing a visit, asking her timeline, talking about her role?

  "Don't just ask, 'Does she have an offer?' Ask: 'Where does she actually stand with that program?'"

#### FIT-025 · "Kick-back" offers: building a relationship for later
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** A program may offer a talented local or regional player "probably headed toward a major state school" to build a relationship in case she later transfers, because she isn't playing, wants to be closer to home, or wants a smaller school. "An offer isn't always about 'We think you're definitely coming here.'"

#### FIT-026 · Interest is everything before an offer. An offer is explicit and specific.
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** Her written list of "interest": calls, texts, emails, follows, mail, camp and campus invites, asking your coach, talking to parents, watching, "you're a great fit," "you're one of our top players," "we're recruiting you," contacting your AAU/HS coach. "Until there is an offer, there is no offer." An offer: "We are offering you a scholarship," with what it covers, family cost, academic money, other aid, and conditions. "INTEREST: 'We really like you.' OFFER: 'We want you here.'" "A coach can like 50 players." "DON'T COUNT CONTACT. COUNT COMMITMENT."

#### FIT-027 · Reading coach language: timing, frequency, and actions
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:**
  - **Timeline:** freshman = some interest; sophomore = big interest; junior = GO TIME; right before senior year = commitment.
  - **Back burner:** occasional contact, check-ins every week or two, no forward movement, no offer talk, no timeline questions, no visit push, "we'll keep watching."
  - **High on the list:** frequent calls, follow-ups after games, schedule and development questions, asking about other schools and what she wants, campus push, talk of her role and roster spots, timeline questions.
  - **Frequency** means more *with* an offer; without one, "it is still interest."
  - **Translate the language:** "We really like her" → how much? "One of our favorites" → compared to whom? "Keep watching" → what are you waiting to see? "Love to have her" → are you offering? "Top recruit" → how many ahead of her?
  - **Late junior year with no offer:** find out whether they just discovered her or lost earlier targets. It affects "how your daughter is viewed when she arrives on campus" ("fourth or fifth option").
  - **Watch actions:** "if they haven't watched her in six months, that's information."
- **Source:** [R04-T TL1–BT]
- **Flags:** Consistent with FIT-014 (video). ⚔️ Q-034.

#### FIT-028 · Is an elite camp invitation genuine?
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** Programs "can also generate revenue from camps." Check: Has a coach watched her? Has staff talked to her? Is the invite personal or a mass invite? Is the camp right for her level? Does the school fit her? Does it make sense for her plan? "Don't confuse an invitation with recruiting interest."

#### FIT-029 · If high school is her main exposure, start with schools within driving distance
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** "A school 30–60 minutes away has a very different recruiting equation than a school four hours away." #### FIT-030 · Offers posted online give coaches context
- **Label:** EXPLICIT

#### FIT-031 · Build the school list by distance and across all levels
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** For high school exposure, a "2-hour sweet spot." Tiers:

  - **Tier 2, 1–2 hours:** the sweet spot; tournaments help.
  - **Tier 3, 2–3 hours:** need more behind the invite.
  - **Tier 4, 3–4 hours:** outer radius; film, AAU, relationships matter more.

#### FIT-032 · The honest conversation about level: dream big, see clearly
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** "Separate emotion from evaluation." Interest coming mainly from D2/D3/NAIA/JUCO while you pursue D1 is "information." It's not a final verdict (she may grow, develop or change positions), but "pretending it isn't happening doesn't help her." Don't make the level a parent's ego ("not a report card on how well you parented her"). Other levels "are college basketball," not consolation prizes. Getting on a D1 roster ≠ getting on the floor. Realism means "This is where you are right now. Where do you want to go? And what would it take to get there?" "REALITY CAN GIVE THE DREAM A PLAN." "DON'T PUT A CEILING ON HER TOO EARLY. But don't put blinders on either." Don't lose a great D2/D3/NAIA/JUCO fit "waiting for a label."
- **Source:** [LEVEL-T S2–S14], [THREEMISTAKES-T M2]

#### FIT-033 · Know what you want before they start recruiting you: a living list from freshman year
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** "DECIDE WHAT YOU WANT BEFORE SOMEONE STARTS SELLING YOU WHAT THEY HAVE." Start around freshman year and revise it each year. Categories: **Basketball, Coaching, Role, School, Location, Academics, Finances, Lifestyle.** Mark each item **MUST HAVE / REALLY WANT / WOULD BE NICE**. Don't build the list around a division. On visits, guard against "the fairy tale": "Did I actually change my mind, or did I just get impressed?" When recruiting gets serious, "GO BACK TO YOUR LIST." The list also tells you what to investigate (e.g., playing early → ask about the depth chart).

#### FIT-034 · Narrowing the list: choose "the Tuesday in February," not the recruiting version
- **Label:** EXPLICIT

  - What "we see you playing…" really means (position, who's ahead)
  - The everyday coach vs. the recruiting coach
  - Life if she isn't playing
  - Real distance from home

  - "Walk me through a normal Tuesday during the season"

#### FIT-035 · Visits: enjoy the curated version, then go find "normal"
- **Label:** EXPLICIT (her experience); ⚠️ rules
- **Principle:** From running visits as a recruiting coordinator: "EVERY VISIT WAS NOT DESIGNED THE SAME." All-out visits for top targets (personalized jersey and locker, favorite foods, chosen player hosts and professors) vs. relationship-keeping visits for others. "YOU ARE BEING SHOWN A CURATED VERSION." Talk to people they didn't assign: the bench player, the injured player, the transfer, students. Ask about what isn't shown. "OFFICIAL VISIT ≠ AUTOMATIC OFFER." "Unofficial does not mean unimportant." Don't over-read small details ("they didn't get her favorite candy"). Look at the whole relationship. "Don't confuse FEELING SPECIAL FOR 48 HOURS with BEING HAPPY THERE FOR FOUR YEARS."
- **Rules as stated (2026–27 cycle, ⚠️ VERIFY):**
  - **D1 women's basketball:** official visits from Jan 1 of junior year; no total cap but generally one per school; transcript + Eligibility Center registration + IRL required; max 2 nights; no official or unofficial visits during July evaluation periods except for commitment-related exceptions; unofficial visits unlimited.
  - **D2:** official visits from June 15 before junior year; 48 hours max; unofficial visits unlimited.
  - **D3:** official visits from Jan 1 of junior year; one per school; 48 hours; unofficial visits unlimited with one on-campus meal.
  - **NAIA:** "no recruiting-calendar restrictions." Ask what's covered.
  - **NJCAA:** its own rules. Ask.
- **Source:** [VISITS-T I1–E4, R0–R5]
- **Flags:** ⚠️ V-15. ⚔️ Mostly consistent with M2.G10 ("unofficial visits" from sophomore year).

#### FIT-036 · Waiting for "better" usually backfires

#### FIT-037 · The "say and do" signals chart

## 6. College research and school selection process

#### FIT-016 · School list: build in 9th, narrow in 10th, sort in 11th, commit before senior year
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:**
  - **9th grade:** start the list, "the most basic," with her initial ideas of what she wants in a school. "This one might be the most important one out of all of them."
  - **10th grade:** "start narrowing the list down, narrow, narrow, narrow," and take outreach into your own hands.
  - **11th grade:** "really get our minds right on who's a for sure no" and who's a maybe. Everything she wants should be on the table. "Reality is here. Our offers are our offers." Have your research done.
  - **12th grade:** be committed around the summer live period going into senior year, or by fall of senior year.
- **Source:** [M2.G9 @03:47–04:02], [M2.G10 @02:36–03:44], [M2.G11 @00:00–00:37, 02:14–02:19], [M2.G12 @00:00–00:37]

## 7. How school selection changes by grade
