# Coach Tay's Recruiting Methodology

Her overall recruiting philosophy, core principles, and terminology. Category detail lives in the other files, and this one links to it.
Labels and conventions: see `README.md`. Every entry carries a label (EXPLICIT / INTERPRETATION / MISSING) and a source.

## 0. Conceptual model: how the whole methodology fits together
**Label: INTERPRETATION (labeled).** This is a synthesis of everything in this knowledge base, written so the methodology can be understood (and later reasoned with) as one system. Each point links to the source-backed entries it summarizes. It adds no new teachings. Coach Tay should review it (Q-070).

### 0.1 The core tension she teaches families to bridge
Families are trying to *get recruited*. Coaches are trying to *win games* with limited roster spots, budgets and time, and recruiting is "a business." Almost every lesson is about bridging that gap: make yourself valuable to what a coach needs, and easy to evaluate. → MTH-010, MTH-011, MTH-018–022, MTH-027, MTH-028, EVAL-006

### 0.2 The recruiting funnel (stages a player moves through)
| Stage | What it looks like | Key entries |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Preparation (about 7th–8th grade) | Not recruited yet. Building habits, skills, strength, academics, a stable AAU team, learning the process | MTH-004, MTH-015, TL-002, TL-018, EVAL-008–010 |
| 2. Foot in the door | Mail, follows, early mentions. "A long ways to go" | TL-006, FIT-024 |

### 0.3 What gets evaluated: the dimensions in her teaching
1. **Physical tools:** height (it can't be taught; posts go first), length, athleticism, strength that "shows up on the court." The size chart is context, not cutoffs. → EVAL-015–017, EVAL-037, EVAL-048, EVAL-052
2. **Skill and versatility:** the complete-player list; "capable" at more than one position; for 2/3 guards a superpower plus a fallback; the 4 as the ultimate swing player. → EVAL-011–021, EVAL-038–041, MTH-017, MTH-028
3. **Position scarcity / roster need:** the recruiting order (5s, point guards, 4s, then 2/3 guards) interacts with each program's graduation needs. → EVAL-015, FIT-011, FIT-063
4. **Intangibles:** her S–D tier list (sprinting the floor, communication, charges at the top; refs and blaming teammates at the bottom), response to mistakes, hustle as a skill, bench and warmup behavior, "always being evaluated." → EVAL-090, EVAL-044–047, EVAL-007, EVAL-098
5. **Basketball IQ and learning:** film study, coachability. → EVAL-049, EVAL-070s
6. **Academics and character:** a tiebreaker and a money lever; "can she handle college?" → EVAL-029, EVAL-033, FIT-007, FIT-018
7. **Context of performance:** competition level, consistency across viewings, HS + AAU together; the box score as "information, not the entire evaluation." → EVAL-024–026, EVAL-035, EVAL-043, EVAL-044, EVAL-066
8. **The family:** parent behavior can create "a question mark next to her name." → STR-056

### 0.4 How she determines level (no thresholds, by design)
Her method has three parts: (a) the factor list coaches weigh, compared nationally; (b) **market signals**: "What level is actively showing us that they value her?"; (c) honest outside evaluation with specific questions. Size ranges are rough context with large overlap. Participation odds (R29) set expectations, not individual verdicts. The aim: "reality can give the dream a plan." → EVAL-053, EVAL-052, FIT-032, MTH-006, Q-001, Q-015

### 0.5 How fit is decided (beyond basketball)
Her list (Basketball, Coaching, Role, School, Location, Academics, Finances, Lifestyle; marked Must have / Really want / Would be nice), tested against "the Tuesday in February" (23 investigation areas), total four-year cost, and real playing opportunity. The right level beats the highest label. → FIT-017, FIT-022, FIT-032–036, FIT-010

### 0.6 Exposure economics
Being *seen by the right coaches at the right time* matters more than activity. That means:
- The NCAA live windows (2026–27 D1 WBB: Apr 23–25, May 14–16, Jul 9–12, Jul 23–26)
- Coaches actually at her court
- A realistic driving radius for high school exposure (about 2 hours)
- Full-game film first
- Contact through several channels without spamming

→ MTH-029, STR-010–015, STR-040, STR-042, STR-045, STR-080, FIT-029, FIT-031, recruiting-timeline calendar tables

### 0.7 Roles
The athlete owns it; the parent guides it; a small circle supports her; HS/AAU coaches lend credibility and a back channel. Responsibility shifts toward the athlete with age. → MTH-003, MTH-008, MTH-009, MTH-023, STR-030–034, STR-038, STR-054–057

### 0.8 The grade arc in one line each
- **7th grade:** seeds, experience, choosy events.
- **8th grade:** weight room, lock in an AAU team, learn the process.
- **9th grade:** list started, filming starts, intensity up, elite camps for experience, "learn everything."
- **10th grade:** "prime time": live events only, outreach yourself, narrow the list, unofficial visits.
- **11th grade:** "go time": real offers, yes/no/maybe, visits with purpose, college-athlete habits.
- **12th grade:** commit (ideally before), certification, prepare. If unsigned, flood with film and outreach.

→ `recruiting-timeline.md`

## 1. Core philosophy

#### MTH-001 · Recruiting knowledge is a reference you return to, not a one-time course
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** The course is a library to go back to as each stage arrives, not something to finish once. Getting a general understanding of everything first is good. Families should then "deep dive" into a topic when it becomes relevant.
- **Her words:** "It's not just like a, okay, I learned this and then now we're good… these things are really, really meant for you to be able to go back into…" [00:36–00:46]
- **Applies to:** All families, all grades.
- **Source:** [M1.L1 @00:36–01:11]
- **Flags:** none

#### MTH-002 · There is some order, but recruiting isn't strictly step-by-step
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** The material "does not necessarily go in… a chronological order." Families may need to "hop around" and go back.
- **Source:** [M1.L1 @00:18–00:32]
- **Flags:** none. *INTERPRETATION (labeled):* This may also describe recruiting itself, not just how the course is organized.

#### MTH-004 · The biggest misunderstanding: recruiting does not start when offers start
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** In her opinion, the biggest misunderstanding parents have is "that recruiting starts when offers start to occur." If the dream is college basketball, "in a sense, it starts" in 7th–8th grade. What starts then is preparation, not being recruited.
- **Her words:** "the biggest misunderstanding that parents have, in my opinion, is that recruiting starts when offers start to occur… that just, like, could not be further from the truth." [00:54–01:11] "we're not getting recruited… But we're preparing ourselves to get recruited." [04:42–04:51]
- **Applies to:** 7th–8th grade families especially.
- **Source:** [M1.L2 @00:54–01:42, 04:35–04:51]
- **Written lesson adds:** The written text puts it as "The biggest mistake parents make in the recruiting process is waiting too long to start." Families think recruiting starts in sophomore or junior year, but "those are often the years when the biggest recruiting decisions are already being made." [M1.L2-T ¶1–2]
- **Also:** [THREEMISTAKES-T M1] "STARTING TOO LATE"; [COSTOFWAITING-T ¶1] "potential alone isn't enough… Starting early isn't about pressuring her to succeed. It's about making sure that when opportunity comes knocking, she's ready to answer."
- **Flags:** The video calls it the biggest *misunderstanding* (recruiting starts with offers). The text calls it the biggest *mistake* (waiting too long). Same idea from two angles. Not a conflict.

#### MTH-005 · The opposite misconception: chasing exposure too young
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** The second big misconception is the reverse: 7th/8th graders going to "all the exposure events" to get in front of coaches "for the wrong reasons. They're really not recruiting her at this point."
- **Source:** [M1.L2 @06:00–06:22]; reinforced [M2.G7 @00:45–01:39], [R11 @03:46–04:57]
- **Flags:** none

#### MTH-006 · Very few girls play college basketball
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** Only a small percentage get to play in college: on a typical team "probably like one or none." She uses her own high school and AAU teams as examples. In M2.G12 she recalls "our stat" as "like, almost 5%."
- **Written lesson figures:** "According to the NCAA's most recent participation data, approximately 4.7% of high school girls basketball players go on to compete at an NCAA school… and only about 1.4% go on to play Division I basketball." Her framing: "That doesn't mean your daughter can't be one of them. It means you shouldn't leave something this competitive to chance." [M1.L2-T ¶3–6]
- **Source:** [M1.L2 @01:47–02:42], [M2.G12 @02:24–02:30], [M1.L2-T ¶3–6]
- **Flags:** ⚠️ VERIFY. The written text gives the source as NCAA participation data, which matches her spoken "almost 5%." Check against the current NCAA "Estimated Probability of Competing" data before the tool shows these numbers (V-07). R29 (Batch 3) may add more.

#### MTH-007 · The goal is an athlete who can advocate for herself, not just "a daughter who gets recruited"
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** The goal isn't for a parent to have a daughter who gets recruited. The goal is to develop an athlete who knows how to "advocate for herself, communicate, make decisions, and take ownership of her future." She'll need all of these in college.
- **Her words:** "The goal is to help develop an athlete who knows how to advocate for herself, communicate, make decisions, and take ownership of her future." [02:40–02:51]
- **Applies to:** All families. This is the frame for the parent's role.
- **Source:** [M1.L3 @02:34–03:10]
- **Flags:** none

#### MTH-009 · Her voice has to be the biggest voice at the table
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** The athlete is the "number one chooser" and leads the decision. Parents guide her and help her get her voice across, but "we don't want to decide or talk for her."
- **Her words:** "her voice has to be the biggest voice at the table." [06:01] "it is her basketball career." [06:15]
- **Source:** [M1.L4 @00:52–01:16, 05:54–06:31]
- **Flags:** none. Details on the decision circle are in `recruiting-strategies.md` STR-031.

#### MTH-010 · Recruiting is a business
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** She repeats this throughout. Coaches are nice, but "at the end of the day, it is their job." Offers, timing, camp invitations and pulled offers follow business logic: limited spots, recruiting above their level, filling camps.
- **Her words:** "It just means that it's a business. I'm going to say that a bunch of times" [R13 @06:13–06:18]; "it's all a business is really the bottom line of it" [R03 @00:16–00:20]; "it's just a business" [R02 @07:54]
- **Source:** [R02 @07:54], [R03 @00:16], [R13 @06:13–06:30]

#### MTH-011 · You're trying to get recruited; coaches are trying to win games. Bridge the gap.
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** Families focus on getting recruited, but coaches care about winning games. The mindset shift: "how can I market myself so a coach believes that I can help them win games? That's how you get on the recruiting board." After that, think about how to get on the floor at that level.

- **Flags:** none

#### MTH-045 · "Staying ready so you don't have to get ready"
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** She uses this to explain why film starts in freshman year, before anyone asks for it. Interest can come at any time, and you can't know in advance which game will be the one worth sending. So the habit (filming, cropping, storing, sending) should already be in place.
- **Her words:** "Again, all of this is all about staying ready so you don't have to get ready." [R20 @01:20–01:23]; "just have it be the habit because you don't know what game you're going to go off for, 20 points, 15 points… 12 rebounds, 5 assists… so you want to get in the habit of having this stuff just ready" [R20 @10:14–10:31]
- **Applies to:** All grades. Applied explicitly from freshman year (film).
- **Source:** [R20 @01:08–01:23, 10:09–10:39]
- **Flags:** The same idea as MTH-013 ("Get ahead") and MTH-024 ("Prepared, not reactive"). This is the video's wording of it. It also matches the "OPEN. COPY. SEND." readiness in STR-044.

#### MTH-046 · Knowing the system saves money: see it from the college coaching side
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** Many families travel every weekend without knowing that a public calendar decides when coaches can be there. Learning the calendar shows "where do we go, when do we go." It connects the high school side with the college coaching side, and "at the end of the day, save[s] money." She asks families to "get comfortable" with the calendars instead of being intimidated by them.
- **Her words:** "I've trained a lot of girls that honestly their parents just didn't know that this even existed and that there was a reason rhyme or reason to it all and that there's not coaches every weekend and that there's an easy way to tell if there's going to be coaches" [R23 @10:49–11:06]; "just making sure you understand all of that can really help… bridge that gap of where do we go, when do we go, and trying to understand… the college coaching side when you're really on the high school side. And… at the end of the day, save money." [R23 @12:19–12:39]; "they can look kind of crazy and kind of scary, but I encourage you to get comfortable with them" [R22 @00:12–00:17]
- **Applies to:** Parents of every grade, most of all once events start to matter (9th grade on, per STR-010).
- **Source:** [R23 @00:00–00:41, 10:49–12:39], [R22 @00:09–00:28]
- **Flags:** Corroborates MTH-029 ("Don't confuse activity with progress"), MTH-012 and STR-011. It echoes the "bridge the gap" framing in MTH-011, but MTH-011 is about the gap between getting recruited and winning games, so it's a different gap.

#### MTH-058 · "It's not D1 or bust": recognize where you actually fit
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** With 356,000 high school players chasing about 27,000 college spots (FIT-070), "one of the first things that has to happen is just waking up to the fact that this NCAA Division one option just isn't your option. OK, and it's not a bad thing." Other levels are "high level basketball… only the best of this 356,000 are making it out." "It's not D1 or bust… it's not… anything against you for playing at a different level." Some "knocks against you" exist from birth, before a D1 coach ever sees her: "are you tall enough to play Division 1 basketball? Are you born with the athleticism…?" These can be worked on, but "a lot of times… they were already going to say no." Push for "the best offer, best offer, best offer. But the best offer doesn't just have D1 plastered all over it."
- **Her words:** "So my biggest thing is. Start recognizing where you actually fit in here. It is not a, you know, it's not a bust. It's not D1 or bust that people say." [R29 @09:03–09:15]
- **Applies to:** All families, from freshman year on (see TL-020).
- **Source:** [R29 @06:50–07:32, 08:37–09:57, 11:42–11:49]
- **Flags:** ⚔️ Tone tension with LEVEL-T S14 ("DON'T PUT A CEILING ON HER TOO EARLY… Let her chase Division I") (Q-069). "Just isn't your option" is said to her audience in general, not as a verdict on a specific athlete. Corroborates MTH-032, FIT-032, FIT-022, FIT-005, mistake #18 ("Treating non-NCAA schools as a backup plan").

## 2. Foundational principles

#### MTH-003 · The foundation is understanding the parent role vs. the athlete role
- **Label:** EXPLICIT (that this is the foundation). Partly answered in M1.L3 and M1.L4.
- **Principle:** Module 1, "the foundation," covers "the parent role versus athlete role" and "big misunderstandings." Families should finish Module 1 first.

- **Flags:** M1.L3 mentions a written list of parent vs. athlete responsibilities that we haven't received (Q-028).

#### MTH-008 · Responsibility moves from parent to athlete as she gets older
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** Early on, logistics fall on the parent: which tournaments, which team, even the first conversations with coaches. That's why parents need to be educated too. "As your athlete gets more and more comfortable and, quite frankly, older, more responsibility should go to the athlete." Example: a parent can sit in on early recruiting calls and prompt questions. By the next year, she shouldn't need that much help.
- **Source:** [M1.L3 @01:20–02:31], [M1.L3-T ¶5]
- **Flags:** none. Full written lists of parent and athlete responsibilities are in `recruiting-strategies.md` STR-030 and STR-034.

#### MTH-023 · "Your job is to guide the process. Her job is to own it."
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** This is the rule that divides the roles. Parents guide, educate, advocate, open doors and help with decisions, "but you cannot care more about her future than she does. She has to be invested." "The athlete cannot simply be the person everyone is talking about."
- **Source:** [M1.L3-T ¶1–3], [M1.L4-T ¶13–16]
- **Flags:** none

#### MTH-024 · Prepared, not reactive. Informed early, not pressured early.
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** The course "isn't to tell you that your daughter needs to be in full-on recruiting mode in 7th or 8th grade. It's also not about putting unnecessary pressure on a young athlete. It's about being informed early enough to make good decisions." Know what needs to happen this year and look ahead to the next two years. "That's the difference between being reactive and being prepared." Control what you can control "before the recruiting window gets smaller."
- **Source:** [M1.L2-T ¶7–16]
- **Flags:** Relevant to the ⚔️ "go time" tension in Q-018.

#### MTH-025 · The baking-a-cake principle: every part matters, so don't skip grades
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** "This information works like baking a cake. All aspects work together best." Leave out one ingredient and the result suffers "when it comes time to commit." Families entering late should "go back to previous grade levels and get caught up. DO NOT SKIP them, most of the information still needs to be consumed."
- **Source:** [M2.intro-T ¶3–4]
- **Flags:** none

#### MTH-016 · Recruiting is teamwork between parent and athlete
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** Except for one "parents only" part, every module is meant for both the athlete and the parent. "Nobody should be taking it on alone. It is a team, team, team, team, teamwork."
- **Source:** [M1.L3 @04:52–05:20]
- **Flags:** The "parents only" part hasn't been identified yet.

#### MTH-017 · Make yourself "more marketable": capable, versatile, separated
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** Her position videos come back to the same idea. Be good at your position and capable at others ("capable," "swing"). Have a "superpower" or separator. Be one notch above the player next to you on the board.
- **Her words:** "If you are capable at your position, or good at your position, and capable at another position, you are so much more marketable." [R05 @04:48–04:57] "How can I be one notch above her?" [R06 @09:35–09:40]
- **Source:** [R05], [R06], [R07], [R08], [R09]
- **Flags:** none. Detail in `athlete-evaluation.md` §3.

#### MTH-040 · "It's the athlete's career. We are all just helping."
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** Trainers, HS coaches and AAU coaches have had (or are having) their own careers. Recruiting is about the athlete's career, so she takes responsibility for much of it "with guidance and help." HS and AAU coaches are "a great bridge," but "it really just can't be all in their court." Let them "be an aid… rather than just spearheading the whole thing." Families stay "in control… and accounted for in our own process": "if you want anything done right, you gotta do it yourself."
- **Her words:** "this is not my career that we are training for, right? Essentially, uhm, I've had my career, uhm, your high school coaches had their career if they've had one, your AAU coach, all of that, right? We want it to be the athletes, uhm, that Career, and we are all just helping. So we want the athlete to take responsibility for a lot of this, you know, with guidance and help" [R19 @03:27–03:52]; "We want to stay in control, you know, and, um, accounted for in our own process to get recruited. That's the best way. You ever heard the saying, if you want anything done right, you gotta do it yourself? Kind of the situation here." [R16 @06:43–06:58]
- **Applies to:** All families. Defines how outside helpers fit in.
- **Source:** [R19 @00:22–01:19, 03:22–04:00], [R16 @06:37–06:58]
- **Flags:** Corroborates MTH-009, MTH-023 and R19-T ¶13 ("part of your recruiting team… not your recruiting department"). New: it extends athlete ownership to her relationship with coaches and trainers. *Tone note (not a conflict):* the video's "do it yourself" is blunter than R16-T ¶11 ("don't leave their help sitting on the table").

## 3. How college coaches think (her perspective as a former D1 recruiting coordinator)

#### MTH-018 · Coaches recruit above their level
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** Every level is "always going to be trying to get players that are better than their level" for a competitive edge. Example: a D2 coach wants a kid who "could have squeaked by at D1." She adds: "It's not to say your daughter's not good enough."
- **Source:** [R02 @07:52–08:48]
- **Flags:** none

#### MTH-019 · Coaches recruit several classes at once while managing their current roster
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** "No college coach is, like, recruiting one class at a time." They text and call athletes, AAU coaches and high school coaches across all classes, while also managing their current roster.

- **Flags:** none

#### MTH-021 · Coaches want players they won't have to babysit

#### MTH-022 · Coaches don't care who won the AAU game. They're evaluating.

- **Flags:** none

#### MTH-041 · Coaches are used to hearing no, but they know families won't say it
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** College coaches take what HS/AAU coaches say "pretty seriously." Families want options and won't tell a school they're not interested "right off the bat," in case something else falls through (keeping it "on the back burner"). Coaches know this. They're "very used to hearing no" (the list of kids is long, and they get very few of them), "but they know that you're really not going to tell them no." So they go to the player's coaches: "can you really tell me, is she really interested or is she… really looking at other schools?"
- **Her words:** "Coaches are very used to hearing no, like you've seen how long the list is of kids and they only get like very few of them. So they're used to hearing no, but they know that you're really not going to tell them no. And so they will go to the coaches and try to get a better idea" [R16 @04:31–04:47]
- **Applies to:** All families. Explains why the HS/AAU coach's read of her interest carries weight.
- **Source:** [R16 @04:04–04:55]
- **Flags:** Corroborates STR-038 (R16-T ¶7–8) and MTH-028. Placed here as coach psychology. The "back burner" here is the family's, the reverse of her usual use (a coach keeping a player warm, FIT-014).

#### MTH-050 · Get into the coach's mindset: "they know exactly what's going on"
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** Every staff tracks recruits on some kind of board ("as pretty as this one, or on a piece of paper, on a marker board"): who is offered, who they're waiting on, who is committed, and who to keep watching. They know their roster and their needs in detail and keep adjusting. Families who reason from the coach's side make better decisions. Examples: why a D2 coach goes to the big live events (FIT-060), why an offer is pulled (FIT-064), and why a "wait" player gets texts but no visit push (FIT-065).
- **Her words:** "coaches all across the country, every staff is going to have a board that's like this, whether it's going to be, um, as pretty as this one, or on a piece of paper, on a marker board… This is how they're staying organized… keeping track of girls, just like your daughter and remembering who they have offered, who they're waiting on and who they already have committed. And then… potential people to continue watching." [R26 @00:17–00:50] · "you understand that they truly, truly know everything that's going on with their program and who they want to come in." [R27 @09:16–09:23] · "this is never finalized… they are trying to actively always figure that out. So understand that they know exactly what's going on. And if you can try to get into their mindset here, um, it can be extremely valuable." [R27 @10:20–10:41] · "You have to realize from the mindset of an NCAA Division two coach, where should they go?" [R25 @06:16–06:22]
- **Applies to:** All families. It's a framing principle.
- **Source:** [R26 @00:14–00:50], [R27 @09:12–10:41], [R25 @06:16–07:01]
- **Flags:** Corroborates MTH-011, MTH-019 and R02-T B1/B3 ("every coaching staff has some version of one… The format doesn't matter. The strategy does").

#### MTH-051 · Coaches keep backups because "they're used to hearing no"
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** Staffs keep far more names than spots and hold some players as options "in case everybody else says no." This is built into the job because coaches are turned down often. Being a backup is a position on the board, not a personal judgment. The family's job is to recognize it and act on it (FIT-065).
- **Her words:** "You're just an option in case everybody else says no. Okay, because they do have to have other options because they're used to hearing no." [R27 @08:49–08:58]
- **Applies to:** All recruits with interest but no offer.
- **Source:** [R27 @08:37–08:58]
- **Flags:** Corroborates R02-T B3 ("They are protecting themselves in case a player chooses another school") and R02 @05:39–05:52 (a pulled offer is "not necessarily because of anything you did").

#### MTH-055 · How coaches plan a tournament day: commits, then offers ("babysitting"), then their list, then cold watching
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** "Coaches have already a schedule when they come to these tournaments… They're not going in there really just kind of winging it." Her order:
  1. **Committed players** not yet on campus: "they are going to go watch them first."
  2. **Players with offers out:** "they're not missing a game… We call that babysitting."
  3. **New names already on their list** "that need to be watched."
  4. **Open time → cold watching:** e.g., at 5 p.m. with no game scheduled, "I can go out looking." They scan the schedule for teams from their area ("only like four hours, maybe from my school") to "see if I can find any new kids."

  A **cold watch** = a coach sits at a court "and they don't already have anyone in mind." A far-away school (her example: a California school at a Nashville vs. Atlanta game) has little reason to cold watch "unless they're just like, Oh, I've heard of a girl that's really good."
- **Her words:** "They're going to know first and foremost, the kids that they have committed… Then they're going to go watch all the kids that they have offers out to, and… they're not missing a game… We call that babysitting." [R31 @04:12–04:37]
- **Applies to:** Families judging how much evaluation a tournament can really offer an unknown player.
- **Source:** [R31 @02:47–05:19]
- **Flags:** "Babysitting" here (coaches watching every game of offered players) is a **different meaning** from MTH-021 ("players they won't have to babysit"). Not a conflict in substance, but the glossary should keep the two apart (see MTH-059). "Four hours" is her example, not a rule. Corroborates EVAL-024 / STR-012 (no coaches at your court = no evaluation), FIT-031 radius tiers, MTH-019.

#### MTH-056 · Interest snowballs: coaches talk, search, and follow other offers
- **Label:** EXPLICIT
- **Principle:** One coach's interest can lead to a camp invite, a visit or an offer, and "that is a snowball effect… Maybe it's not your dream school… but that offer leads to another offer that leads to another offer. That's just the way coaches do it." Coaches work by word of mouth. If a coach can identify her, "I can go look up Jill on X on Twitter… and then I'm be like, Oh… that other team offered you or… you went on a visit to that other… school. Interesting. Let me take a deeper look… that's how all coaches are." So losing one coach to a small moment can cost "more and more interest."
- **Her words:** "that offer leads to another offer that leads to another offer. That's just the way coaches do it." [R31 @13:26–13:32]
- **Applies to:** All athletes, especially early in the process.
- **Source:** [R31 @13:02–14:12]
- **Flags:** "word of ear" @13:32 is a caption error for "word of mouth." Corroborates STR-016 / STR-048 (coaches check X for offers) and FIT-030 (offers give context). FIT-030 / SOCIAL-T ¶3 also says offers mainly from lower levels can raise questions; how that fits with "offer leads to another offer" is logged in Q-066 (e).
